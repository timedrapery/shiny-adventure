# Start Here: A Reading Order for Newcomers

This is a suggested order for reading through the lexicon's 36 translated
suttas if you're new to this material -- not a table of contents, a path.

It is deliberately **not** the same order as the project's internal
[Wave roadmap](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/next-suttas-roadmap.md).
That roadmap ranks texts by *editorial leverage* -- which sutta, translated
next, does the most to strengthen and stress-test the governed lexicon. This
page ranks the same texts by a completely different question: if you have
never read early Buddhist material before, what order actually makes sense?
Those two orderings sometimes agree and often don't, and that's expected --
they're solving different problems.

**A note on links:** five suttas on this list --
[AN 3.65](suttas/an3-65-kesamutta-sutta.md),
[SN 56.11](suttas/sn56-11-dhammacakkappavattana-sutta.md),
[SN 36.6](suttas/sn36-6-salla-sutta.md),
[MN 63](suttas/mn63-culamalukya-sutta.md), and
[SN 22.59](suttas/sn22-59-anattalakkhana-sutta.md) -- have built reader
pages so far (see the [homepage](index.md)). Every other sutta below links
to its source text on GitHub instead: the same governed translation, but as
a plain Markdown document with the full editorial apparatus attached, rather
than the clean reading layout this site is building toward. Read them there
for now; they'll move here over time.

## Stage 1 -- Before Any Doctrine

Three texts that set the tone before anything technical shows up: why to
trust this material at all, why some questions get deliberately left
unanswered, and who was actually asking these questions in the first place.

1. **[Test It Yourself](suttas/an3-65-kesamutta-sutta.md)** (AN 3.65, the Kālāma Sutta) -- "don't take my word for it, or anyone else's." The most naturally modern-feeling starting point: an explicit instruction to test claims against your own experience rather than accept them on authority.
2. **[MN 63: The Man Struck by a Poisoned Arrow](suttas/mn63-culamalukya-sutta.md)** -- why the teaching refuses to answer certain metaphysical questions. Directly answers the objection a skeptical reader will already be forming after text 1.
3. **[MN 26: Pāsarāsi Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn26-pasarasi-sutta.md)** -- the Buddha's own account of leaving home, studying under two teachers, and finding them insufficient. A narrative, not a doctrine -- a human anchor before the vocabulary gets technical.

## Stage 2 -- The Basic Diagnosis and the Path

The core claim and the practical response to it, kept concrete.

4. **[The First Teaching](suttas/sn56-11-dhammacakkappavattana-sutta.md)** (SN 56.11) -- the first sermon: the four noble truths and the eightfold path stated directly. Foundational, but reads better once Stage 1 has set the frame -- taken cold, its formulaic structure can feel like a list to memorize rather than a diagnosis to recognize.
5. **[One Arrow, Not Two](suttas/sn36-6-salla-sutta.md)** (SN 36.6) -- the one-arrow/two-arrows teaching: physical pain versus the added mental suffering piled on top of it. Concrete, bodily, and immediately recognizable.
6. **[MN 7: Vattha Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn7-vattha-sutta.md)** -- a mind is like cloth: dye it while it's dirty and the color comes out wrong. Ethics introduced through a simile instead of a rule list.
7. **[AN 4.113: Patoda Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/an4-113-patoda-sutta.md)** -- four kinds of horses, four kinds of people, and what it actually takes to be moved to practice. A jolt of urgency after three fairly calm texts.
8. **[AN 11.9: Saddhā Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/an11-9-saddha-sutta.md)** -- addresses the "do I have to believe something" question directly, which by this point the reader has probably already asked.

## Stage 3 -- Learning to Look at Your Own Mind

Practical method. This is where the material stops being about ideas and
starts being about what you actually do.

9. **[MN 19: Dvedhāvitakka Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn19-dvedhavitakka-sutta.md)** -- sorting thoughts into two bins, wholesome and not, and what to do with each. The simplest possible entry point into mind-training.
10. **[MN 2: Sabbāsava Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn2-sabbasava-sutta.md)** -- seven concrete methods for handling what erodes the mind: restraint, use, endurance, avoidance, removal, development. A toolkit, not a theory.
11. **[MN 118: Ānāpānasati Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn118-anapanasati-sutta.md)** -- structured breath-meditation instructions. The first text on this list that is a practice manual rather than a teaching about practice.
12. **[MN 10: Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn10-satipatthana-sutta.md)** -- the four foundations of mindfulness, and the longest, densest text so far on this list. Not really a one-sitting read: it's the reference manual for the pieces above, worth returning to rather than finishing.
13. **[DN 2: Sāmaññaphala Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/dn2-samannaphala-sutta.md)** -- a king asks what a renunciant actually gets out of the life. A full narrative walk through the gradual path from an outsider's curious, slightly skeptical point of view -- useful for seeing how all the pieces above fit into one arc.
14. **[AN 10.60: Girimānanda Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/an10-60-giriminanda-sutta.md)** -- ten perceptions taught to a sick monk. Practical and, unusually for this stage, comforting.
15. **[MN 39: Mahā-Assapura Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn39-maha-assapura-sutta.md)** -- what actually makes someone a genuine renunciant, as opposed to someone who merely looks like one. Ethics and practice fused.
16. **[SN 46.51: Āhāra Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn46-51-ahara-sutta.md)** -- what feeds the hindrances that block practice, and what starves them. A closing, practical text for this stage.

## Stage 4 -- Not-Self and Dependent Arising

The harder doctrinal core. Everything here assumes the reader already has
the practical vocabulary from Stages 1-3 -- this is where that vocabulary
gets tested against the two hardest ideas in the material.

17. **[Nothing Here Is You](suttas/sn22-59-anattalakkhana-sutta.md)** (SN 22.59) -- the second sermon. Not-self laid out as a clean, followable argument rather than an assertion.
18. **[MN 22: Alagaddūpama Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn22-alagaddupama-sutta.md)** -- the cobra simile and the raft simile carry genuinely difficult not-self doctrine on strong enough imagery that it stays followable.
19. **[SN 22.48: Khandha Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn22-48-khandha-sutta.md)** -- what the five heaps actually are, stated directly.
20. **[SN 22.89: Khemaka Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn22-89-khemaka-sutta.md)** -- a subtler point: even someone who has genuinely seen not-self can still carry a faint, hard-to-locate sense of "I am." Worth reading only after 17-19 make the basic claim solid.
21. **[MN 148: Chachakka Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn148-chachakka-sutta.md)** -- the six sextets: a systematic, almost mechanical working-through of not-self across every sense door. Dense, but by this point the pattern should be familiar.
22. **[SN 12.15: Kaccānagotta Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn12-15-kaccanagotta-sutta.md)** -- one page, defining right view as the middle way between "it exists" and "it doesn't." The clearest, shortest bridge into dependent arising.
23. **[SN 12.23: Upanisā Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn12-23-upanisa-sutta.md)** -- the positive chain, dissatisfaction leading step by step to freedom, mirroring the well-known negative chain.
24. **[SN 12.2: Paṭiccasamuppāda-vibhaṅga Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn12-2-paticcasamuppada-vibhanga-sutta.md)** -- the standard formula of dependent arising, defined term by term.
25. **[MN 38: Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhaya Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn38-mahatanhasankhaya-sutta.md)** -- a monk's wrong view (that the same consciousness travels on unchanged) gets corrected, and dependent arising gets restated in narrative, argued form rather than as a bare formula.
26. **[DN 15: Mahānidāna Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/dn15-mahanidana-sutta.md)** -- the deepest and longest exposition of dependent arising in the set. The capstone of this stage, not an entry point to it.
27. **[MN 9: Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn9-sammaditthi-sutta.md)** -- right view examined through more than a dozen different doctrinal lenses in one text. Reads best as a review once the pieces it's reviewing are already familiar.

## Stage 5 -- Advanced and Reference Texts

Technical material that rewards already knowing the vocabulary cold. These
are suttas people tend to return to rather than read straight through, and a
couple of them (MN 1 especially) are traditionally considered among the
hardest texts in the collection -- there's no reason to front-load that
difficulty.

28. **[MN 44: Cūḷavedalla Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn44-culavedalla-sutta.md)** -- a systematic question-and-answer catechism between two disciples, covering a wide sweep of doctrine efficiently.
29. **[MN 64: Mahāmālukya Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn64-mahamalukya-sutta.md)** -- the five lower fetters, and the companion piece to MN 63 (same disciple, later in his practice).
30. **[MN 137: Saḷāyatanavibhaṅga Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn137-salayatanavibhanga-sutta.md)** -- a technical analysis of the six sense bases.
31. **[MN 141: Saccavibhaṅga Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn141-saccavibhanga-sutta.md)** -- the four noble truths again, now in full analytical detail rather than the compressed form from Stage 2.
32. **[MN 117: Mahācattārīsaka Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn117-mahacattarisaka-sutta.md)** -- a technical analysis of the eightfold path, factor by factor.
33. **[SN 35.28: Āditta Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/sn35-28-aditta-sutta.md)** -- the fire sermon. Iconic imagery carrying a genuinely abstract point about the senses.
34. **[MN 18: Madhupindika Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn18-madhupindika-sutta.md)** -- the honeyball sutta, on how perception snowballs into proliferating thought. Famously dense even by this collection's standards.
35. **[MN 99: Subha Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn99-subha-sutta.md)** -- a dialogue defending renunciant life against a brahmin's claim that household life is better. Good late-stage read for weighing the whole path against the alternative.
36. **[MN 1: Mūlapariyāya Sutta](https://github.com/timedrapery/shiny-adventure/blob/main/docs/translations/mn1-mulapariyaya-sutta.md)** -- "the root of all things." Traditionally regarded as one of the most difficult suttas in the entire collection. Deliberately last on this list, not first -- it rewards everything that came before it and rewards very little read cold.

## If You Only Read Five

Kālāma (AN 3.65), the Poisoned Arrow (MN 63), the first sermon (SN 56.11),
one arrow/two arrows (SN 36.6), and the second sermon (SN 22.59). That's the
whole shape of the teaching -- test it yourself, some questions aren't the
point, here's the diagnosis, here's how pain and suffering differ, here's
why "self" doesn't hold up under examination -- in nine hundred words with none
of the reference-manual density.
