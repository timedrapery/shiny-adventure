# Test It Yourself

*AN 3.65, the Kesamutta Sutta -- widely known as the Kālāma Sutta*

## About this text

A group of villagers complain to the Buddha: teachers keep passing through
town, each one praising their own doctrine and tearing down everyone else's,
and the villagers have no way to tell who's actually right. Instead of
handing them a doctrine to trust, the Buddha gives them something else
entirely -- a method. Don't go by tradition, report, scripture, logic,
or a speaker's reputation alone. Go by what you can actually verify:
does a state of mind, acted on, lead to harm or to welfare, for yourself
and others.

This is the sutta most likely to feel immediately familiar to a modern
reader, since its core instruction is close to ordinary critical thinking.
It closes with an unusual argument: even setting aside every disputed
question about karma and rebirth, freeing your mind of greed, aversion, and
delusion pays off regardless of which metaphysical picture turns out to be
true.

## The Setting

So I have heard. At one time the Buddha was wandering in the land of the
Kosalans together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus when he arrived at a town of
the Kālāmas called Kesamutta.

The Kālāmas of Kesamutta heard: "The recluse Gotama — a Sakyan, gone forth
from a Sakyan family — has arrived at Kesamutta. This good report has spread
about that Blessed One: 'He is indeed the Buddha, the arahant, perfectly
and fully awakened...' It is good to see such arahants."

The Kālāmas of Kesamutta went to the Buddha. Some bowed and sat to one
side; some exchanged friendly greetings and polite conversation and sat to one
side; some raised cupped palms toward the Buddha and sat to one side; some
announced their name and clan and sat to one side; some sat to one side in
silence. Seated to one side, the Kālāmas of Kesamutta said to the Buddha:

## The Kālāmas' Uncertainty

"Bhante, there are some recluses and brahmins who come to Kesamutta. They
explain and illuminate only their own doctrine, while they attack, belittle,
disparage, and mock the doctrines of others. Then other recluses and brahmins
come to Kesamutta, and they too explain and illuminate only their own doctrine,
while they attack, belittle, disparage, and mock the doctrines of others. We
are left, Bhante, with doubt and uncertainty: which of these venerable recluses
and brahmins speaks the truth, and which speaks falsely?"

"It is right, Kālāmas, for you to doubt — right for you to be uncertain. You
have become uncertain about something genuinely uncertain.

## The Ten Criteria

Come, Kālāmas. Do not go by oral transmission; do not go by lineage; do not go
by hearsay; do not go by scriptural authority; do not go by logical
argumentation; do not go by inference; do not go by thinking through the
apparent reasons; do not go by agreeing with a view after reflecting on it; do
not go by the apparent competence of a speaker; and do not think 'This recluse
is our teacher.'

But when you know for yourselves: 'These things are unwholesome, blameworthy,
criticized by those with discernment; when taken up and undertaken they lead to
harm and suffering' — then give them up.

## The Three Roots of Harm

What do you think, Kālāmas — does greed arise within a person for their welfare
or for their harm?"

"For their harm, Bhante."

"A person seized by greed, their feeling mind overwhelmed and taken over: they
kill living creatures, take what is not given, go to another's partner, speak
falsely, and encourage others to do likewise. That is for their long-lasting
harm and suffering."

"Yes, Bhante."

"What do you think, Kālāmas — does aversion arise within a person for their
welfare or for their harm?"

"For their harm, Bhante."

"A person seized by aversion, their feeling mind overwhelmed and taken over:
they kill living creatures, take what is not given, go to another's partner,
speak falsely, and encourage others to do likewise. That is for their
long-lasting harm and suffering."

"Yes, Bhante."

"What do you think, Kālāmas — does delusion arise within a person for their
welfare or for their harm?"

"For their harm, Bhante."

"A person seized by delusion, their feeling mind overwhelmed and taken over:
they kill living creatures, take what is not given, go to another's partner,
speak falsely, and encourage others to do likewise. That is for their
long-lasting harm and suffering."

"Yes, Bhante."

"What do you think, Kālāmas — are these things wholesome or unwholesome?"

"Unwholesome, Bhante."

"Blameworthy or blameless?"

"Blameworthy, Bhante."

"Criticized or praised by those with discernment?"

"Criticized by those with discernment, Bhante."

"When taken up and undertaken, do they lead to harm and suffering, or not? How
do you see this?"

"When taken up and undertaken, they lead to harm and suffering. That is how we
see it."

"So, Kālāmas, when I said: 'Come, Kālāmas. Do not go by oral transmission; do
not go by lineage; do not go by hearsay; do not go by scriptural authority; do
not go by logical argumentation; do not go by inference; do not go by thinking
through the apparent reasons; do not go by agreeing with a view after
reflecting on it; do not go by the apparent competence of a speaker; and do not
think "This recluse is our teacher." But when you know for yourselves — "These
things are unwholesome, blameworthy, criticized by those with discernment; when
taken up and undertaken they lead to harm and suffering" — then give them up.'
That is what I said, and this is why I said it.

## The Three Roots of Welfare

Come, Kālāmas. Do not go by oral transmission; do not go by lineage; do not go
by hearsay; do not go by scriptural authority; do not go by logical
argumentation; do not go by inference; do not go by thinking through the
apparent reasons; do not go by agreeing with a view after reflecting on it; do
not go by the apparent competence of a speaker; and do not think 'This recluse
is our teacher.'

But when you know for yourselves: 'These things are wholesome, blameless,
praised by those with discernment; when taken up and undertaken they lead to
welfare and happiness' — then take them up and live accordingly.

What do you think, Kālāmas — does non-greed arise within a person for their
welfare or for their harm?"

"For their welfare, Bhante."

"A person free of greed, their feeling mind not overwhelmed and not taken over:
they do not kill living creatures, do not take what is not given, do not go to
another's partner, do not speak falsely, and do not encourage others to do
likewise. That is for their long-lasting welfare and happiness."

"Yes, Bhante."

"What do you think, Kālāmas — does non-aversion arise within a person for
their welfare or for their harm? ... Does non-delusion arise within a person
for their welfare or for their harm? ... Is that for their long-lasting welfare
and happiness?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"What do you think, Kālāmas — are these things wholesome or unwholesome?"

"Wholesome, Bhante."

"Blameworthy or blameless?"

"Blameless, Bhante."

"Criticized or praised by those with discernment?"

"Praised by those with discernment, Bhante."

"When taken up and undertaken, do they lead to welfare and happiness, or not?"

"When taken up and undertaken, they lead to welfare and happiness. That is how
we see it."

"So, Kālāmas, when I said: 'Come, Kālāmas. Do not go by oral transmission...
and do not think "This recluse is our teacher." But when you know for
yourselves — "These things are wholesome, blameless, praised by those with
discernment; when taken up and undertaken they lead to welfare and happiness"
— then take them up and live accordingly.' That is what I said, and this is
why I said it.

## Friendliness, Kindness, Gladness, and Dynamic Balance

That noble disciple, Kālāmas, free of coveting, free of aversive distraction,
unconfused, clearly knowing and remembering, dwells pervading one direction with
a feeling mind filled with friendliness, likewise the second, likewise the
third, likewise the fourth — thus above, below, across, everywhere, for all as
for oneself, they dwell pervading the entire world with a feeling mind filled
with friendliness, vast, expansive, boundless, without enmity and without ill
will.

They dwell pervading one direction with a feeling mind filled with kindness
towards others, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth —
thus above, below, across, everywhere, for all as for oneself, they dwell
pervading the entire world with a feeling mind filled with kindness towards
others, vast, expansive, boundless, without enmity and without ill will.

They dwell pervading one direction with a feeling mind filled with gladness,
likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth — thus above,
below, across, everywhere, for all as for oneself, they dwell pervading the
entire world with a feeling mind filled with gladness, vast, expansive,
boundless, without enmity and without ill will.

They dwell pervading one direction with a feeling mind filled with dynamic
balance, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth — thus
above, below, across, everywhere, for all as for oneself, they dwell pervading
the entire world with a feeling mind filled with dynamic balance, vast,
expansive, boundless, without enmity and without ill will.

## The Four Consolations

That noble disciple, Kālāmas, with a feeling mind free of enmity, free of ill
will, uncorrupted and pure, wins four consolations in this very life.

'If there is another world, and if good and bad actions do bear fruit and
result — then when this body breaks up, after death, I will be reborn in a good
destination, a heavenly world.' This is the first consolation won.

'If there is no other world, and if good and bad actions do not bear fruit and
result — then in this very life I keep myself free of enmity, free of ill will,
untroubled and happy.' This is the second consolation won.

'If bad things do come to those who do bad — I have no harmful intention toward
anyone. Not doing any bad action, how could suffering reach me?' This is the
third consolation won.

'If bad things do not come to those who do bad — then I see myself pure on both
sides.' This is the fourth consolation won.

That noble disciple, Kālāmas, with a feeling mind free of enmity, free of ill
will, uncorrupted and pure — they win these four consolations in this very
life."

"That is exactly so, Blessed One. That is exactly so, Well-Departed One. That
noble disciple, Bhante, with a feeling mind free of enmity, free of ill will,
uncorrupted and pure, wins four consolations in this very life.

'If there is another world, and if good and bad actions do bear fruit and
result — then when this body breaks up, after death, I will be reborn in a good
destination, a heavenly world.' This is the first consolation won.

'If there is no other world, and if good and bad actions do not bear fruit and
result — then in this very life I keep myself free of enmity, free of ill will,
untroubled and happy.' This is the second consolation won.

'If bad things do come to those who do bad — I have no harmful intention toward
anyone. Not doing any bad action, how could suffering reach me?' This is the
third consolation won.

'If bad things do not come to those who do bad — then I see myself pure on both
sides.' This is the fourth consolation won.

That noble disciple, Bhante, with a feeling mind free of enmity, free of ill
will, uncorrupted and pure — they win these four consolations in this very
life.

## Going for Refuge

Excellent, Bhante! Excellent! ... We, Bhante, go to the Buddha for
refuge, and to the Dhamma and the Bhikkhu Saṅgha. May the Buddha receive
us as lay followers who have gone for refuge from this day forward for as long
as life lasts."

The fifth discourse is finished.
