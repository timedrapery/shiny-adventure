Here the Buddha looks back to the time before his awakening and tells you
what he was asking.

He took each of the five *clung-to heaps* in turn (form, felt experience,
recognition, putting-together activities and knowing) and asked three
questions of it. What is its *gratification*: what real satisfaction does it
offer? What is its *danger*: what is wrong with leaning on it? And what is the
*escape* from it?

Notice the order. He does not start by calling the heaps worthless. Each one
really does give satisfaction and gladness, and the text says so first. The
danger is that each is impermanent and changing, and the escape is not getting
rid of the heaps but letting go of the desire and passion for them.

The last part is the claim itself: he says he did not call himself awakened
until he had seen all three sides of all five heaps as they really are.
