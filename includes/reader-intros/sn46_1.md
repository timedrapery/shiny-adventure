This is one image and one claim, and it takes about a minute to read.

The image: nāgas — great creatures of Indian story, somewhere between serpents
and elephants, and the texts rarely say which — grow up on the Himalaya until
they are big enough and strong enough to go down through the pools, then the
small rivers, then the great rivers, and finally into the ocean. Only there do
they reach full size.

The claim: how you actually behave does the same job for practice that the
mountain does for them. It is not a moral entry fee. It is the thing the seven
awakening factors get large on, and without it they stay small.

The seven awakening factors are the practice list this collection returns to
often: remembering, discernment of qualities, energy, rejoicing, relaxation,
composure, and dynamic balance. *What Feeds and Starves Distraction*, SN
46.51, introduces them at length; this page just says what they need
underneath them.
