The Buddha comes back from his alms round and finds many lodgings set out
for bhikkhus in a Sakyan's dwelling. It is the time for making robes, and
many bhikkhus have gathered. He uses the moment to talk to Ānanda about
company and seclusion.

A bhikkhu who loves company, he says, will not reach the deeper satisfactions
of practice at will. Then he describes his own way of dwelling: *emptiness*,
entered by not attending to any *signs*, and he lays out how a bhikkhu trains
toward it.

The training is honest about failure. You steady the heart, turn it toward
emptiness, and it may not go. When it does not, you clearly know that it does
not, steady the heart again, and turn it again. The same clear knowing then
goes with you into walking and sitting, into what you talk about and what you
think about, into any pull toward the senses, and into watching the five
*clung-to heaps* rise and pass until the conceit "I am" is gone.

The last part is about teachers and pupils: three ways a person who lives in
seclusion can come to ruin, and the Buddha's request to be treated as a
friend. He will not handle you gently, he says, like a potter with wet clay.
He will keep correcting you. Whatever is sound will stand.
