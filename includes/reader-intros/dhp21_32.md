This page is a short chapter of verses from the Dhammapada, the best-known
collection of the Buddha's sayings. All twelve verses are about one quality:
*heedfulness*.

Heedfulness means not drifting. It is the quality of someone who stays
awake to what they are doing, keeps making the effort, and comes back
when attention slips. Its opposite, heedlessness, is simply letting
things slide.

The first verse makes the whole claim at once: heedfulness is the path to
the deathless, and heedlessness is the path to death. The rest show what
that looks like: a person building an island no flood can reach, a fire
burning up its fetters, someone on a mountain looking out over the plain.

The numbers beside the verses are the Dhammapada's own verse numbers, so
you can find any verse in other translations. *Maghavā*, in verse 30, is a
name of Sakka, the chief of the devas.
