This short text names two ways a view can take over. One side clings to
continued becoming. The other is so disgusted by becoming that it treats the
destruction of a self after death as peace.

The Buddha does not ask you to pick one of those positions. The practical
alternative is to notice what has come to be, see it without turning it into
something permanent, and let fascination with it fade. The closing verse is
compact, but its point is direct: freedom is not wanting either to keep
becoming or to be wiped out.
