This teaching turns the path into one river image. Drifting downstream means
following the easy pull of sensual indulgence and harmful behavior. Going
upstream means resisting that pull, even when doing so feels difficult.

The first two descriptions are directions of movement, not permanent kinds
of human being. The last two use the early Buddhist map of awakening: one
person will not return to ordinary sensual existence, while the other has
completed the crossing. You can follow the practical image—drifting,
struggling, becoming steady, and reaching dry land—before deciding what you
think about the text's claims concerning rebirth.

One phrase is especially honest: a person may go upstream while distressed
and in tears. Beginning to act against a well-established habit is not always
peaceful, but it changes the direction of travel.
