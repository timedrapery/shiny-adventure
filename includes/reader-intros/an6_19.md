The Buddha asks a group of monks whether they practice *recollection of
death*, and six of them answer.

Each one says the same thing in a different size. If only I might live for a
night and a day, says the first, I would give my attention to the Buddha's
teaching and get a great deal done. The next says a day. Then the time it
takes to eat one meal, then four or five mouthfuls, then one mouthful, and
finally the time it takes to breathe in after breathing out.

The Buddha does not reject any of them, but he sorts them. The first four
are still *heedless*: they have left themselves enough time to put things
off. Only the last two are heedful.

The point is not to dwell on death gloomily. It is to stop assuming there
will be time later, and to do the work now.
