This teaching follows harmful behavior back to three ordinary causes: greed,
aversion, and delusion. It does not leave them as ideas. It shows what they
look like when they take over—abusing power, denying what is true, and living
in a state of strain.

The second half gives the opposites: non-greed, non-aversion, and clarity.
Here "clarity" translates a Pali word that literally means "non-delusion."
The final image is practical and physical: freedom requires pulling the roots
out so completely that the choking vines cannot grow back.

The text also makes a claim about what follows death. You do not need to settle
that claim before testing its central observation: these roots produce either
suffering or ease here and now.
