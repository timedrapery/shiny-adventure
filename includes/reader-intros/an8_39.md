Most lists of the five precepts read like rules you agree to keep. This short
talk turns them around and asks what other people get out of them.

The answer is concrete. Someone who has given up killing has handed every
living being one thing they can count on: they will not be killed by this
person. The same goes for stealing, for betrayal, for lying, and for the
recklessness that comes with being drunk. The text calls each of these a
*gift*, and a great one, because the number of people it protects was never
limited in advance.

The last turn is the surprising one. Whoever gives that freedom from fear ends
up sharing in it themselves, without limit.

The three refuges — the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha — are counted here
on the same list and in the same terms, as things that simply do you good.

A "stream of benefit" is this project's rendering of *puññābhisanda*: benefit
flowing in, the way water flows into a low place.
