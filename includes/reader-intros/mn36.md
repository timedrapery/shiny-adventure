Saccaka is a well-known debater who comes to the Buddha looking for a weak
spot. He is from a Jain family; the Jains were a rival group of renunciants
who were known for harsh physical austerities. His claim is simple: some
practitioners train the body and neglect the mind, and the Buddha's followers
train the mind and neglect the body.

The Buddha does not pick a side. He gives one test for both. When something
pleasant happens, does it take over your heart? When something painful
happens, does that take over your heart? Someone who is developed in body and
in mind is not taken over by either.

Then Saccaka asks, in effect, whether the Buddha has simply never felt
anything strong enough. The answer is the longest account in these texts of
what the Buddha went through before his awakening: two teachers who could
take him only so far, self-torment that brought him close to death, and the
moment he remembered a calm satisfaction he had known long before and asked,
"Could that be the path to awakening?"

Listen for the line that comes back after every stage of the story. The pain
was extreme, and it did not take over his heart. Later the satisfaction of
deep composure was extreme too, and it did not take over his heart either.
