A householder named Dīghajāṇu tells the Buddha, plainly, what his life is
like: a house full of children, nice things, money. He asks for a teaching
that will help people like him, in this life and the next.

The Buddha does not tell him to give it all up. He gives him four things for
a good life now: work hard and well at whatever you do; protect what you
earn; keep company with good people and learn from them; and live within
your means, neither extravagantly nor stingily. He adds four drains that
empty a household's wealth: chasing women, drinking, gambling, and bad
friends.

Then come four things for the life to come: confidence, good conduct,
generosity, and discernment. It is one of the most practical texts in the
collection for anyone living an ordinary life.
