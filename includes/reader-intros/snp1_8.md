This is one of the most often recited texts in the Buddhist world, usually
called the *Karaṇīya Metta Sutta* after its opening words. It is about
*friendliness*: wishing all beings well, without limit.

It starts with character, not with the wish. Someone who wants to practice
this should be capable, honest, easy to speak to, content, and not greedy.
Only then come the lines most people know: *may all beings be happy at
heart*, whatever and wherever they are.

The central image is a mother protecting her only child with her life. The
text asks you to turn that same care toward all beings, in every direction,
whether you are standing, walking, sitting, or lying down.

The numbers beside the verses count the verses within this discourse.
