This is the Path chapter of the Dhammapada: seventeen short verses about the
way out of dissatisfaction.

It opens by ranking things. The best path is the eightfold path, and there is
no other. Then comes the line many people remember from this chapter: *you
yourselves must make the effort; Tathāgatas only tell the way.*

Verses 277 to 279 are the heart of it. Each takes one of three facts, that
everything put together is impermanent, that it is unsatisfactory, and that
all phenomena are not-self, and says the same thing about it: when you see
this with discernment, you grow disenchanted, and that is the path. AN 3.136
states the same three facts in prose.

The rest is practical: get up when it is time to get up, guard your speech,
cut down the forest of desire, and do not assume there will always be more
time.

The numbers beside the verses are the Dhammapada's own verse numbers.
