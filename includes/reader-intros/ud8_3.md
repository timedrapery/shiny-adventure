This is the shortest page in the collection, and it makes one argument.

Everything you can point at is *conditioned*: it came about because other
things did, and it will stop when they stop. That is the ordinary subject of
these texts. The question this page raises is what it would mean if that were
all there is.

The Buddha's answer is conditional, and worth reading twice. If there were
nothing unborn, unmade and unconditioned, then there would be no discernible
way out of what is born, made and conditioned — you could only ever move
around inside it. Because there is, there is a way out.

Notice what the text does not do. It does not say what the unborn is, or
where, or what it is like. It only says that without it the escape would not
be findable. Readers have been filling that silence in for two thousand years;
the page itself leaves it open, and so does this translation.

*Unbecome* is an odd word on its own. It sits between *unborn* and *unmade* in
a list of four, and it means the same kind of thing: not having come to be.
