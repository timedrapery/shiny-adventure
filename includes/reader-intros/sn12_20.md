This short talk makes a distinction that is easy to miss and hard to unsee
once you have it. There is the pattern by which one thing depends on another,
and there are the things the pattern runs through. They are not the same, and
the Buddha here teaches them separately.

The first half says something unusual: the pattern holds whether or not anyone
ever discovers it. A teacher does not invent it. He wakes up to it, and then
points at it and says "Look." That is why the teaching is meant to be checked
rather than believed.

The second half stops the first half from becoming a metaphysics. Every single
item in the sequence—including ignorance, and including knowing—is
impermanent and on its way out. Nothing in the list is permanent, and the
stable pattern is not some further thing standing behind the list.

The ending is practical. Someone who has actually seen this has no use for the
questions "What was I? What will I be? What am I?" There is nothing left for
them to be about.
