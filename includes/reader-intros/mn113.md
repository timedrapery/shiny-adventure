This discourse takes on a quiet problem in spiritual life: pride.

The Buddha goes through twenty-six things a monk might be proud of, one at a
time. Coming from a good family. Being well known. Receiving plenty of
support. Being learned, or an expert in the rules, or a good speaker. Living
in the forest, wearing rag robes, never lying down. Even reaching the deep
meditative states.

Each time, the *bad person* uses it to praise themselves and put others
down. The *good person* sees that none of it ends greed, aversion, or
delusion; what matters is how someone actually practices. For the meditative
states, the good person's answer goes further: the Buddha taught not to
identify even with these.

It ends with someone who has gone beyond all of it and no longer thinks of
anything as "me" at all.
