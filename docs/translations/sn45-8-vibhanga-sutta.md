# SN 45.8: Vibhaṅga Sutta (The Analysis)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 45.8 Translation Notes](sn45-8-vibhanga-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The eight factors follow the repository's governed renderings: `right view`,
  `right attitude`, `right speech`, `right action`, `right livelihood`, `right
  effort`, `right remembering`, and `right composure`.
- `ariya` is rendered `noble`. This is the first reader surface that directly
  anchors the repository's main `ariya` term record.
- The Pali abbreviates the opening list and three of the four right-effort
  rounds. This edition expands them from the definitions stated in this
  discourse and the matching governed passages elsewhere in the corpus.
- In the right-action section, `abrahmacariyā veramaṇī` is `abstaining from
  sexual activity`. This is a celibacy formula, not the sexual-misconduct
  formula found in MN 141.

## Translation

### The Noble Eightfold Path

At Sāvatthī, the Buddha said:

"Bhikkhus, I will teach you the noble eightfold path and explain it. Listen and
pay close attention. I will speak."

"Yes, Bhante," the bhikkhus replied.

The Buddha said this:

"And what is the noble eightfold path?

It is:

- right view
- right attitude
- right speech
- right action
- right livelihood
- right effort
- right remembering
- right composure

### Right View

And what is right view?

Knowledge of dissatisfaction, knowledge of its origin, knowledge of its
quenching, and knowledge of the path of practice that leads to its quenching.

This is called right view.

### Right Attitude

And what is right attitude?

An attitude of renunciation, an attitude of non-ill-will, and an attitude of
non-harming.

This is called right attitude.

### Right Speech

And what is right speech?

Abstaining from false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter.

This is called right speech.

### Right Action

And what is right action?

Abstaining from taking life, taking what is not given, and sexual activity.

This is called right action.

### Right Livelihood

And what is right livelihood?

A noble disciple gives up wrong livelihood and makes a living by right
livelihood.

This is called right livelihood.

### Right Effort

And what is right effort?

A bhikkhu generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, takes hold of the
heart, and exerts themselves:

- so that unarisen harmful, unwholesome qualities do not arise
- so that arisen harmful, unwholesome qualities are abandoned
- so that unarisen wholesome qualities arise
- so that arisen wholesome qualities remain, do not fade, grow, expand,
  develop, and come to fulfilment

This is called right effort.

### Right Remembering

And what is right remembering?

A bhikkhu remains observing the body in relation to the body—ardent, clearly
knowing, and remembering—having removed coveting and distress regarding the
world.

They remain observing felt experience in relation to felt experience—ardent,
clearly knowing, and remembering—having removed coveting and distress
regarding the world.

They remain observing the heart in relation to the heart—ardent, clearly
knowing, and remembering—having removed coveting and distress regarding the
world.

They remain observing dhammas among dhammas—ardent, clearly knowing, and
remembering—having removed coveting and distress regarding the world.

This is called right remembering.

### Right Composure

And what is right composure?

A bhikkhu, quite secluded from sensuality and unwholesome qualities, enters
and remains in the first mental theme, with thinking and pondering, and with
rejoicing and satisfaction born of seclusion.

With thinking and pondering subsiding, the heart settles within and becomes
unified. They enter and remain in the second mental theme. It is without
thinking and pondering, with rejoicing and satisfaction born of composure.

With the fading of rejoicing, they remain in dynamic balance, remembering and
clearly knowing, and experience satisfaction in the body. They enter and
remain in the third mental theme, of which the noble ones say: 'One of dynamic
balance and remembering stays satisfied.'

With the abandoning of satisfaction and discomfort, and with the earlier
ending of joy and sadness, they enter and remain in the fourth mental
theme, without discomfort and without satisfaction, purified by dynamic
balance and remembering.

This is called right composure."

The eighth discourse is finished.
