## Translation

### The Setting

"Bhikkhus, I will teach you the penetrating exposition of the teaching. Listen
carefully and pay close attention. I will speak."

"Yes, Bhante," the bhikkhus replied.

The Buddha said this:

### What the Exposition Covers

"And what, bhikkhus, is the penetrating exposition of the teaching?

Sensuality should be understood; where sensuality comes from should be
understood; the differences among kinds of sensuality should be understood;
the result of sensuality should be understood; the quenching of sensuality
should be understood; and the path of practice leading to the quenching of
sensuality should be understood.

Felt experience should be understood, and so on through to the path of
practice leading to the quenching of felt experience.

Recognition should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of recognition.

The outflows should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of the outflows.

Action should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice leading
to the quenching of action.

Dissatisfaction should be understood, and so on through to the path of
practice leading to the quenching of dissatisfaction.

### Sensuality

'Sensuality should be understood; where sensuality comes from should be
understood; the differences among kinds of sensuality should be understood;
the result of sensuality should be understood; the quenching of sensuality
should be understood; and the path of practice leading to the quenching of
sensuality should be understood.' That is what was said. Why was it said?

There are these five strands of sensuality, bhikkhus:

- visible forms known by the eye that are wished for, loved, agreeable,
  delightful, charming, and bound up with sensuality;
- sounds known by the ear that are wished for, loved, agreeable, delightful,
  charming, and bound up with sensuality;
- smells known by the nose, the same;
- tastes known by the tongue, the same;
- tangibles known by the body that are wished for, loved, agreeable,
  delightful, charming, and bound up with sensuality.

But these are not sensuality, bhikkhus. In the noble discipline these are
called the strands of sensuality:

> The passion in a person's attitude is their sensuality.
> The pretty things in the world are not sensuality.
> The passion in a person's attitude is their sensuality.
> The pretty things stand just as they are in the world,
> and the wise remove the desire for them.

And where does sensuality come from?

Contact, bhikkhus, is where sensuality comes from.

And what are the differences among kinds of sensuality?

Sensuality for visible forms is one thing, sensuality for sounds another, for
smells another, for tastes another, for tangibles another. This is called the
differences among kinds of sensuality.

And what is the result of sensuality?

When someone wants something, they bring into being a state of being that
matches it, one that shares in benefit or one that shares in harm. This is
called the result of sensuality.

And what is the quenching of sensuality?

The quenching of contact, bhikkhus, is the quenching of sensuality. And this
noble eightfold path is the path of practice leading to the quenching of
sensuality: right view, right attitude, right speech, right action, right
livelihood, right effort, right remembering, right composure.

When a noble disciple understands sensuality this way, understands where it
comes from this way, understands the differences among its kinds this way,
understands its result this way, understands its quenching this way, and
understands the path of practice leading to its quenching this way, then they
understand this penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of sensuality.

'Sensuality should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of sensuality.' That is what was said, and this is
why it was said.

### Felt Experience

'Felt experience should be understood, and so on through to the path of
practice leading to the quenching of felt experience.' That is what was said.
Why was it said?

There are these three kinds of felt experience, bhikkhus: pleasant feeling,
painful feeling, and mixed feeling.

And where does felt experience come from?

Contact, bhikkhus, is where felt experience comes from.

And what are the differences among kinds of felt experience?

There is worldly pleasant feeling and unworldly pleasant feeling; worldly
painful feeling and unworldly painful feeling; worldly mixed feeling and
unworldly mixed feeling. This is called the differences among kinds of felt
experience.

And what is the result of felt experience?

When someone feels something, they bring into being a state of being that
matches it, one that shares in benefit or one that shares in harm. This is
called the result of felt experience.

And what is the quenching of felt experience?

The quenching of contact, bhikkhus, is the quenching of felt experience. And
this noble eightfold path is the path of practice leading to the quenching of
felt experience: right view, right attitude, right speech, right action, right
livelihood, right effort, right remembering, right composure.

When a noble disciple understands felt experience this way, and so on through
to the path of practice leading to its quenching, then they understand this
penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of felt experience.

'Felt experience should be understood, and so on through to the path of
practice leading to the quenching of felt experience.' That is what was said,
and this is why it was said.

### Recognition

'Recognition should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of recognition.' That is what was said. Why was it
said?

There are these six kinds of recognition, bhikkhus: recognition of visible
forms, of sounds, of smells, of tastes, of tangibles, and of dhammas.

And where does recognition come from?

Contact, bhikkhus, is where recognition comes from.

And what are the differences among kinds of recognition?

Recognition of visible forms is one thing, recognition of sounds another, of
smells another, of tastes another, of tangibles another, of dhammas another.
This is called the differences among kinds of recognition.

And what is the result of recognition?

Recognition results in how things get spoken of, I say. However someone
recognizes a thing, that is how they speak of it: 'I recognized it that way.'
This is called the result of recognition.

And what is the quenching of recognition?

The quenching of contact, bhikkhus, is the quenching of recognition. And this
noble eightfold path is the path of practice leading to the quenching of
recognition: right view, right attitude, right speech, right action, right
livelihood, right effort, right remembering, right composure.

When a noble disciple understands recognition this way, and so on through to
the path of practice leading to its quenching, then they understand this
penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of recognition.

'Recognition should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of recognition.' That is what was said, and this is
why it was said.

### The Outflows

'The outflows should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of the outflows.' That is what was said. Why was it
said?

There are these three outflows, bhikkhus: the outflow of sensuality, the
outflow of becoming, and the outflow of ignorance.

And where do the outflows come from?

Ignorance, bhikkhus, is where the outflows come from.

And what are the differences among the outflows?

There are outflows that lead to hell, outflows that lead to the animal realm,
outflows that lead to the realm of ghosts, outflows that lead to the human
world, and outflows that lead to the deva world. This is called the
differences among the outflows.

And what is the result of the outflows?

When someone is caught in ignorance, they bring into being a state of being
that matches it, one that shares in benefit or one that shares in harm. This
is called the result of the outflows.

And what is the quenching of the outflows?

The quenching of ignorance, bhikkhus, is the quenching of the outflows. And
this noble eightfold path is the path of practice leading to the quenching of
the outflows: right view, right attitude, right speech, right action, right
livelihood, right effort, right remembering, right composure.

When a noble disciple understands the outflows this way, and so on through to
the path of practice leading to their quenching, then they understand this
penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of the outflows.

'The outflows should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of the outflows.' That is what was said, and this is
why it was said.

### Action

'Action should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of action.' That is what was said. Why was it said?

Intention, bhikkhus, is what I call action. A person intends, and then acts —
by body, by speech, by mind.

And where does action come from?

Contact, bhikkhus, is where action comes from.

And what are the differences among kinds of action?

There is action to be experienced in hell, action to be experienced in the
animal realm, action to be experienced in the realm of ghosts, action to be
experienced in the human world, and action to be experienced in the deva
world. This is called the differences among kinds of action.

And what is the result of action?

I say the result of action is of three kinds, bhikkhus: in this very life, on
being reborn, or on some later occasion. This is called the result of action.

And what is the quenching of action?

The quenching of contact, bhikkhus, is the quenching of action. And this noble
eightfold path is the path of practice leading to the quenching of action:
right view, right attitude, right speech, right action, right livelihood,
right effort, right remembering, right composure.

When a noble disciple understands action this way, and so on through to the
path of practice leading to its quenching, then they understand this
penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of action.

'Action should be understood, and so on through to the path of practice
leading to the quenching of action.' That is what was said, and this is why it
was said.

### Dissatisfaction

'Dissatisfaction should be understood; where dissatisfaction comes from should
be understood; the differences among kinds of dissatisfaction should be
understood; the result of dissatisfaction should be understood; the quenching
of dissatisfaction should be understood; and the path of practice leading to
the quenching of dissatisfaction should be understood.' That is what was said.
Why was it said?

Birth is dissatisfaction; ageing is dissatisfaction; illness is
dissatisfaction; death is dissatisfaction; sorrow, lamentation, pain,
distress, and despair are dissatisfaction; wanting something and not getting
it is dissatisfaction; in brief, the five clung-to heaps are dissatisfaction.

And where does dissatisfaction come from?

Ignorant wanting, bhikkhus, is where dissatisfaction comes from.

And what are the differences among kinds of dissatisfaction?

There is dissatisfaction that is severe and dissatisfaction that is slight;
there is dissatisfaction that fades slowly and dissatisfaction that fades
quickly. This is called the differences among kinds of dissatisfaction.

And what is the result of dissatisfaction?

Here, bhikkhus, someone overcome by dissatisfaction, their mind consumed by
it, grieves, wears themselves out, wails, beats their chest and cries, and
loses their bearings. Or else, overcome by dissatisfaction, their mind
consumed by it, they go searching outside: 'Who knows one phrase, two phrases,
for the quenching of this dissatisfaction?'

Dissatisfaction results in bewilderment, I say, or in searching. This is
called the result of dissatisfaction.

And what is the quenching of dissatisfaction?

The quenching of ignorant wanting, bhikkhus, is the quenching of
dissatisfaction. And this noble eightfold path is the path of practice leading
to the quenching of dissatisfaction: right view, right attitude, right speech,
right action, right livelihood, right effort, right remembering, right
composure.

When a noble disciple understands dissatisfaction this way, understands where
it comes from this way, understands the differences among its kinds this way,
understands its result this way, understands its quenching this way, and
understands the path of practice leading to its quenching this way, then they
understand this penetrating spiritual life as the quenching of dissatisfaction.

'Dissatisfaction should be understood; where dissatisfaction comes from should
be understood; the differences among kinds of dissatisfaction should be
understood; the result of dissatisfaction should be understood; the quenching
of dissatisfaction should be understood; and the path of practice leading to
the quenching of dissatisfaction should be understood.' That is what was said,
and this is why it was said.

This, bhikkhus, is the penetrating exposition of the teaching."

The ninth discourse is finished.
