# SN 22.26 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[SN 22.26: Assāda Sutta](sn22-26-assada-sutta.md). The main translation is the
primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of SN 22.26 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`sn22.26:0.1` to `sn22.26:2.6`), fetched from the upstream
  Bilara root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: MN 19 for the opening, SN 56.11 for the declaration and
  the closing knowledge, DN 15 and MN 11 for `assāda`, `ādīnava`, and
  `nissaraṇa`, MN 137 for `aniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vipariṇāmadhammaṁ`, and SN 22.48 for
  the heap names.
- Working method: the discourse asks one three-part question of each of the
  five heaps, answers it for each, and then states that awakening was claimed
  only once those answers were directly known. The fivefold answer is kept
  fully parallel.

## Governing Decisions

- `assāda`, `ādīnava`, and `nissaraṇa` are `gratification`, `danger`, and
  `escape`, following DN 15 and MN 11. `nissaraṇa` is the governed headword;
  `assāda` and `ādīnava` have no record, and these are the corpus's existing
  renderings.
- The genitive is rendered with `in` and `from`: `the gratification in form`,
  `the danger in form`, `the escape from form`. `The gratification of
  recognition` stacks two abstract nouns, which the plain-English standard
  flags, and `in` reads as ordinary English for what a thing offers. It is
  also the long-standing English wording of this formula.
- The five heaps keep their governed names: `form`, `felt experience`,
  `recognition`, `putting-together activities` (the heap context rule for
  `saṅkhārā`), and `knowing`. `pañcupādānakkhandhā` is `five clung-to heaps`.
- `sukhaṁ somanassaṁ` is `satisfaction and gladness`, keeping the governed
  `sukha` and `somanassa`.
- `aniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vipariṇāmadhammaṁ` is `impermanent, dissatisfactory, and
  subject to change`. MN 137 has the same phrase with `inconstant`; this
  surface keeps `anicca`'s governed default `impermanent`.
- `chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṁ` is `the removal of desire and passion
  ..., the abandoning of desire and passion ...`. `chandarāga` follows its
  record (`desire and passion`) and `pahāna` its record (`abandoning`).
  `vinaya` is governed as `discipline`, which names a training; here it is the
  removing of something, so `removal` is a document-local rendering.
- `yathābhūtaṁ abbhaññāsiṁ` is `directly knew, as they really are`.
  `abhijānāti` keeps its governed `directly knows`, and `as they really are`
  follows the same declaration in SN 56.11.
- The awakening claim and `akuppā me vimutti; ayamantimā jāti; natthi dāni
  punabbhavo` are copied from SN 56.11, because they are the same formula.

## Re-audit Calls

- The source abbreviates the recognition passage (`sn22.26:1.15`, `…pe…`).
  The study edition expands it, as SN 22.48 does for its abbreviated heaps, so
  each heap gets all three answers.
- The setting is the abbreviated `Sāvatthinidānaṁ`, rendered `At Sāvatthī, the
  Buddha said:`, as in SN 22.22.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse does not say the heaps are only dangerous. It names a real
  gratification first: satisfaction and gladness do arise dependent on each
  heap. Seeing that honestly is part of the method, not a concession.
- The escape is not getting rid of the heaps. It is the removal of desire and
  passion for them. The heaps remain; the pull toward them ends.
- The Buddha ties his own claim to awakening to having seen all three sides as
  they really are. The discourse is therefore autobiography and instruction at
  once.
