# SN 48.10: Dutiyavibhaṅga Sutta (The Second Analysis)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of SN 48.10, designed to be readable
  as a single Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 48.10 Translation Notes](sn48-10-dutiyavibhanga-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of SN 48.10 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `indriya` is rendered `faculty`;
  `saddhā` is rendered `confidence`; `vīriya` is rendered `energy`; `sati` is
  rendered `remembering`; `samādhi` is rendered `mental composure`; `paññā` is
  rendered `discernment`; `jhāna` is rendered `mental theme`; `pīti` and
  `sukha` are rendered `rejoicing` and `satisfaction`; `ekaggatā` is rendered
  `directness`; `upekkhā` is rendered `dynamic balance`; `citta` is rendered
  `feeling mind`; and `chanda` is rendered `desire`.
- This edition is the control surface for the five faculties. Each faculty is
  defined here by what a noble disciple actually does, not by a definition of
  the abstract noun, and the translation keeps that shape.
- Four of the five definitions are stock passages that other governed surfaces
  already carry. Their wording is reused rather than re-solved: the four right
  efforts follow SN 51.13, the four establishments of remembering follow
  MN 10, the four mental themes follow the governed jhāna formula, and the
  epithets of the Buddha follow MN 38.
- The Pali abbreviates three passages with peyyala — the middle three
  faculties in the opening list, and the middle two establishments of
  remembering. This edition marks the elisions rather than silently expanding
  them.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's SN 48.10
  translation surface.

## Translation

### The Five Faculties

"Bhikkhus, there are these five faculties.

What five? The faculty of confidence ... the faculty of discernment.

### The Faculty of Confidence

And what, bhikkhus, is the faculty of confidence?

Here a noble disciple has confidence. They place confidence in the awakening
of the Tathāgata: 'So indeed is the Buddha — an arahant, perfectly and fully
awakened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, well-departed, knower of the
world, unsurpassed guide for those to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans,
awakened, blessed.'

This, bhikkhus, is called the faculty of confidence.

### The Faculty of Energy

And what, bhikkhus, is the faculty of energy?

Here a noble disciple lives with aroused energy for the abandoning of
unwholesome qualities and the taking up of wholesome qualities — steadfast,
firm in exertion, not putting down the burden with respect to wholesome
qualities.

They generate desire for the non-arising of unarisen harmful, unwholesome
qualities; they make an effort, arouse energy, take hold of the feeling mind,
and exert themselves.

They generate desire for the abandoning of arisen harmful, unwholesome
qualities; they make an effort, arouse energy, take hold of the feeling mind,
and exert themselves.

They generate desire for the arising of unarisen wholesome qualities; they
make an effort, arouse energy, take hold of the feeling mind, and exert
themselves.

They generate desire for the continuation, non-confusion, increase,
abundance, development, and fulfilment of arisen wholesome qualities; they
make an effort, arouse energy, take hold of the feeling mind, and exert
themselves.

This, bhikkhus, is called the faculty of energy.

### The Faculty of Remembering

And what, bhikkhus, is the faculty of remembering?

Here a noble disciple remembers. They possess the highest carefulness in
remembering, and they recall and call to mind what was done and said long ago.

They remain observing the body in relation to the body, ardent, with clear
knowing, with remembering, having removed coveting and distress regarding the
world.

They remain observing felt experience ...

They remain observing the feeling mind ...

They remain observing dhammas among dhammas, ardent, with clear knowing, with
remembering, having removed coveting and distress regarding the world.

This, bhikkhus, is called the faculty of remembering.

### The Faculty of Mental Composure

And what, bhikkhus, is the faculty of mental composure?

Here a noble disciple, having made relinquishment the object, gains mental
composure, gains directness of feeling mind.

Quite secluded from sensuality, secluded from unwholesome dhammas, they enter
and remain in the first mental theme, which has thinking and pondering, and
the rejoicing and satisfaction born of seclusion.

With the subsiding of thinking and pondering, with inner clarity and
unification of the feeling mind, they enter and remain in the second mental
theme, without thinking and pondering, with the rejoicing and satisfaction
born of mental composure.

With the fading of rejoicing, they remain in dynamic balance, remembering and
clearly knowing, and experience satisfaction in the body. They enter and
remain in the third mental theme, of which the noble ones say: 'One of dynamic
balance and remembering stays satisfied.'

With the abandoning of satisfaction and discomfort, and with the earlier
ending of gladness and sadness, they enter and remain in the fourth mental
theme, without discomfort and without satisfaction, purified by dynamic
balance and remembering.

This, bhikkhus, is called the faculty of mental composure.

### The Faculty of Discernment

And what, bhikkhus, is the faculty of discernment?

Here a noble disciple discerns. They possess the discernment that leads to
arising and passing away — noble, penetrating, rightly leading to the wearing
away of dissatisfaction.

They discern it as it has come to be: 'This is dissatisfaction.' They discern
it as it has come to be: 'This is the origin of dissatisfaction.' They discern
it as it has come to be: 'This is the quenching of dissatisfaction.' They
discern it as it has come to be: 'This is the way of practice leading to the
quenching of dissatisfaction.'

This, bhikkhus, is called the faculty of discernment.

These, bhikkhus, are the five faculties."
