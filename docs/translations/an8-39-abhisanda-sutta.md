# AN 8.39: Abhisanda Sutta (Streams of Benefit)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [AN 8.39 Translation Notes](an8-39-abhisanda-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `puññābhisanda` and `kusalābhisanda` are `stream of benefit` and `stream of
  what is wholesome`, keeping the governed `puñña` (`benefit`) and `kusala`
  (`wholesome`).
- `sukha` takes its recorded alternate `ease` in all three of its appearances
  in the repeated formula. `happiness` is a discouraged rendering here.
- The eight items are three refuges followed by the five gifts, and the
  discourse counts the five gifts as items four through eight. The source
  writes items one, three, four, and eight out in full and abbreviates the
  rest. This edition keeps every full statement where the source puts it and
  names each abbreviated item once in between.

## Translation

### Eight Streams of Benefit

"Bhikkhus, there are eight streams of benefit, streams of what is wholesome,
food for ease. They are heavenly, they ripen in ease, they lead to heaven, and
they lead to what is wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare and ease.

Which eight?

### Going for Refuge

Here, bhikkhus, a noble disciple has gone to the Buddha for refuge. This is
the first stream of benefit, a stream of what is wholesome, food for ease. It
is heavenly, it ripens in ease, it leads to heaven, and it leads to what is
wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare and ease.

Again, bhikkhus, a noble disciple has gone to the Dhamma for refuge. This is
the second stream of benefit, and everything said of the first holds for it.

Again, bhikkhus, a noble disciple has gone to the Saṅgha for refuge. This is
the third stream of benefit, a stream of what is wholesome, food for ease. It
is heavenly, it ripens in ease, it leads to heaven, and it leads to what is
wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare and ease.

### Five Great Gifts

There are five gifts, bhikkhus—great gifts, known to be foremost, known to be
long-standing, known to belong to an old lineage, ancient, uncorrupted, never
corrupted before, not corrupted now, and never to be corrupted; and recluses
and brahmins who have discernment do not fault them.

Which five?

Here, bhikkhus, a noble disciple gives up taking life and refrains from taking
life. By refraining from taking life, bhikkhus, a noble disciple gives
countless beings freedom from fear, gives them freedom from enmity, gives them
freedom from affliction. And by giving countless beings freedom from fear,
freedom from enmity, and freedom from affliction, they come to share in
freedom from fear, freedom from enmity, and freedom from affliction without
limit.

This is the first gift, bhikkhus, a great gift, known to be foremost, known to
be long-standing, known to belong to an old lineage, ancient, uncorrupted,
never corrupted before, not corrupted now, and never to be corrupted; and
recluses and brahmins who have discernment do not fault it.

And this is the fourth stream of benefit, a stream of what is wholesome, food
for ease. It is heavenly, it ripens in ease, it leads to heaven, and it leads
to what is wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare and ease.

The next three go the same way:

- a noble disciple gives up taking what is not given and refrains from taking
  what is not given
- a noble disciple gives up sexual misconduct and refrains from sexual
  misconduct
- a noble disciple gives up false speech and refrains from false speech

In each case a noble disciple gives countless beings freedom from fear,
freedom from enmity, and freedom from affliction, and comes to share in that
same freedom without limit. Each of these is a great gift in the same way—the
second, the third, and the fourth—and each is a stream of benefit in the same
way: the fifth, the sixth, and the seventh.

Again, bhikkhus, a noble disciple gives up intoxicants that lead to
heedlessness and refrains from intoxicants that lead to heedlessness. By
refraining from intoxicants that lead to heedlessness, bhikkhus, a noble
disciple gives countless beings freedom from fear, gives them freedom from
enmity, gives them freedom from affliction. And by giving countless beings
freedom from fear, freedom from enmity, and freedom from affliction, they come
to share in freedom from fear, freedom from enmity, and freedom from
affliction without limit.

This is the fifth gift, bhikkhus, a great gift, known to be foremost, known to
be long-standing, known to belong to an old lineage, ancient, uncorrupted,
never corrupted before, not corrupted now, and never to be corrupted; and
recluses and brahmins who have discernment do not fault it.

And this is the eighth stream of benefit, a stream of what is wholesome, food
for ease. It is heavenly, it ripens in ease, it leads to heaven, and it leads
to what is wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare and ease.

### The Eight Together

These, bhikkhus, are the eight streams of benefit, streams of what is
wholesome, food for ease. They are heavenly, they ripen in ease, they lead to
heaven, and they lead to what is wished for, loved, and agreeable, to welfare
and ease."

The ninth discourse is finished.
