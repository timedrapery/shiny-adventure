# AN 8.54 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[AN 8.54: Dīghajāṇu Sutta](an8-54-dighajanu-sutta.md). The main translation
is the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of AN 8.54 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`an8.54:1.1` to `an8.54:18.5`), fetched from the upstream
  Bilara root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: SN 45.2 for `kalyāṇamittatā`; AN 3.65 for `hitāya
  sukhāya` in lay teaching; SN 48.10 and AN 5.14 for the confidence and
  discernment definitions; AN 8.39 for the five precepts; AN 11.12 for the
  generosity formula; Dhp 21-32 for `appamatta`.
- Working method: two sets of four, each named and then defined, with the
  reservoir simile in the middle and closing verses. The source abbreviates
  the epithets and the five precepts (`…pe…`); the study edition writes both
  out.

## Governing Decisions

- `diṭṭhadhammahitāya diṭṭhadhammasukhāya` and `samparāyahitāya
  samparāyasukhāya` are `welfare and happiness in this life` and `welfare and
  happiness in the life to come`. `sukha` is `happiness` here, as in AN
  3.65's lay teaching, not the governed `satisfaction`: the householder is
  asking about a good life, and `satisfaction in this life` would narrow it.
  This is document-local.
- `kulaputta` is `a son of good family`. The record's `good-family son` is
  kept in sense; the word order reads as English.
- `Byagghapajja` (`Tiger's Path`) is Dīghajāṇu's clan name, used by the
  Buddha as a form of address. It is kept as a name.
- `uṭṭhānasampadā` is `accomplishment in initiative`; `uṭṭhāna` is getting up
  and getting on with it. `ārakkhasampadā` is `accomplishment in protection`,
  and `samajīvitā` is `balanced living`.
- `kalyāṇamittatā` is `good friendship`, as in SN 45.2. In the drains and
  inlets, `pāpamitto pāpasahāyo pāpasampavaṅko` is `bad friends, bad
  companions, and bad associates`, and `kalyāṇamitto kalyāṇasahāyo
  kalyāṇasampavaṅko` is `good friends, good companions, and good associates`.
  `pāpamitta` keeps its record, `bad friend`.
- `itthidhutto, surādhutto, akkhadhutto` is `chasing women, drinking,
  gambling`.
- `udumbarakhādī` is `like someone eating figs`: shaking a fig tree for one
  fig and letting the rest fall. `ajeṭṭhamaraṇaṁ marissati` is `will die like
  a starving man`, following the usual reading.
- The confidence definition follows SN 48.10 and AN 5.14, with the epithets
  written out. The conduct definition writes out the five precepts in AN
  8.39's wording, compressed into one sentence.
- The generosity formula follows AN 11.12: `free from the stain of
  stinginess. ... openhanded, glad to let go, responsive when asked, and glad
  to give and share.`
- The discernment definition follows SN 48.10 and AN 5.14.
- `saccanāmena` is `the one truly named`, an epithet of the Buddha.
- `cāgo puññaṁ pavaḍḍhati` is `generosity and benefit grow`. `puñña` keeps its
  governed `benefit`.

## Re-audit Calls

- `Catutthaṁ` is rendered `The fourth discourse is finished.`

## Practice Clarifications

- The Buddha does not tell Dīghajāṇu to leave household life. He takes the
  householder's life as given and says what makes it go well now and later.
- Good friendship appears in both halves: as one of the four things for this
  life, and as the inlet that keeps wealth from draining away.
