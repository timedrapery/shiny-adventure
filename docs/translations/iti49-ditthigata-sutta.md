# Iti 49: Diṭṭhigata Sutta (Taken Over by Views)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [Iti 49 Translation Notes](iti49-ditthigata-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The opening says that two views can take people over. One clings to
  continued becoming; the other welcomes annihilation as the answer.
- The middle way is practical rather than speculative: see what has come to
  be, stop treating it as permanent, and practice toward disenchantment,
  fading, and quenching.

## Translation

### Two Ways Views Take Over

This was said by the Buddha, said by the arahant—so I heard:

"Bhikkhus, gods and humans are taken over by two views. Some hold back, while
others go too far. Those who see clearly recognize what is happening.

### Holding Back

"How do some hold back?

"Gods and humans delight in becoming. They enjoy it and celebrate it. So when
the Dhamma for the quenching of becoming is taught, their hearts do not take
to it, grow confident in it, settle on it, or commit to it.

"This is how some hold back.

### Going Too Far

"And how do some go too far?

"Some are distressed, ashamed, and disgusted by becoming itself. They welcome
non-becoming, thinking:

"'When the body breaks up after death, this self is cut off, destroyed, and no
longer exists. This is peaceful. This is excellent. This is how things really
are.'

"This is how some go too far.

### Seeing Clearly

"And how do those who see clearly recognize what is happening?

"Here a bhikkhu sees what has come to be simply as what has come to be. Seeing
it this way, they practice for disenchantment with it, for its fading, and for
its quenching.

"This is how those who see clearly recognize what is happening."

This is what the Buddha said. And in reference to this, it was said:

### Verses

"Those who see what has come to be
simply as what has come to be,
and see the way beyond it,
are released in line with reality
when ignorant wanting for becoming is completely worn away.

"Fully understanding what has come to be,
free of wanting for becoming or non-becoming,
a bhikkhu does not come to further becoming
when what has come to be is gone."

This too was said by the Buddha—so I heard.

The twelfth discourse is finished.
