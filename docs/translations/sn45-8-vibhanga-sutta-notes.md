# SN 45.8 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[SN 45.8: Vibhaṅga Sutta](sn45-8-vibhanga-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `sn45.8:1.1`–`sn45.8:10.7`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/sn45.8/pli/ms>.
- Control surfaces: the matching path definitions in MN 141, the right-effort
  formula in SN 51.13, the remembering and composure definitions in SN 48.10,
  and the governed path-factor records.
- The discourse has about 300 Pali words after front matter is excluded.

## Governing Decisions

- **`ariya` remains `noble`.** The adjective appears in the collective path
  name, in `noble disciple`, and in the noble ones' description of the third
  mental theme. The established compound `noble eightfold path` stays intact.
  This surface directly anchors the previously orphaned `ariya` major record.
- **The path-factor names follow current house policy.** In particular,
  `sammāsaṅkappa` is `right attitude`, `sammāsati` is `right remembering`, and
  `sammāsamādhi` is `right composure`. The translation does not rotate back to
  `right intention`, `right mindfulness`, or `right concentration`.
- **The four-truth definition of right view follows the existing control
  passage.** Repeating `knowledge` preserves the four occurrences of `ñāṇa`
  and avoids confusing that term with the repository's separate `knowing`
  rendering for `viññāṇa`.
- **`abrahmacariyā veramaṇī` is `abstaining from sexual activity`.** This
  differs legitimately from MN 141, whose right-action definition uses
  `kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī`, abstaining from sexual misconduct. The present
  discourse is addressed to bhikkhus and states the celibacy formula.
- **The right-effort wording follows SN 51.13.** `chandaṁ janeti vāyamati
  vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati` is rendered `generates desire,
  makes an effort, arouses energy, takes hold of the heart, and exerts
  themselves`. The repeated tasks are formatted as a list so readers can see
  the four different jobs without losing the repeated governing action.
- **The four establishments of remembering follow the existing control
  surfaces.** `kāya`, `vedanā`, and `citta` are `body`, `felt experience`, and
  `heart`; the technical fourth object remains `dhammas` rather than being
  flattened into the same English used for wholesome and unwholesome mental
  qualities.
- **Right composure is defined by the four mental themes.** The translation
  follows the current governed sequence: `pīti` is `rejoicing`, `sukha` is
  `satisfaction`, `upekkhā` is `dynamic balance`, and `jhāna` is `mental
  theme`. The passage stays practical and avoids trance or concentration
  language. `Somanassa` takes its controlled alternate `joy` in the direct
  pair `joy and sadness`; this prevents the line from being confused with the
  separate `muditā` use of `gladness`. The second theme's inner settling is
  expressed as a verb so it is not confused with `amoha`, governed elsewhere
  as `clarity`.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source abbreviates the opening factor list as `sammādiṭṭhi …pe…
sammāsamādhi`. Because all eight factors are then defined one by one, the
reader edition prints the complete list as a navigation aid.

The source also writes the first and fourth right-effort rounds in full while
abbreviating the two middle rounds. The edition expands those middle rounds
from the identical governed formula. No new doctrinal content is supplied;
only the repeated wording represented by the source's peyyāla is restored.
