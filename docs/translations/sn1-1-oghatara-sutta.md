# SN 1.1: Oghataraṇa Sutta

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 1.1 Translation Notes](sn1-1-oghatara-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `oghaṃ atari` is rendered `crossed over the flood`, preserving the
  source's concrete crossing image.
- `appatiṭṭhaṃ anāyūhaṃ` is rendered `without standing still and
  without struggling`. The following lines explain both sides of the paradox.
- `parinibbuta` is expressed as `has reached complete quenching` because the
  verse describes the living Buddha, not final nibbāna at death.

## Translation

### A Question in the Night

So I have heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

Late one night, a radiant deity lit up the whole of Jeta's Grove. The deity
approached the Buddha, bowed, stood to one side, and asked:

"My friend, how did you cross over the flood?"

"Friend, I crossed over the flood without standing still and without
struggling."

"But my friend, how did you cross over the flood without standing still or
struggling?"

"When I stood still, I sank.

When I struggled, I was swept away.

That is how I crossed over the flood without standing still or struggling."

### The Deity's Verse

"After such a long time, at last I see
a brahmin who has reached complete quenching.
Without standing still and without struggling,
they have crossed over the world's entanglement."

The Teacher agreed with what the deity had said.

Thinking, "The Teacher agrees with me," the deity bowed to the Buddha,
circled him clockwise, and vanished right there.
