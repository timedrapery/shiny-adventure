# SN 46.1: Himavanta Sutta (The Himalaya)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 46.1 Translation Notes](sn46-1-himavanta-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `sīla` keeps its governed `conduct`, and the English repeats `relying on`
  for `nissāya` on both sides of the simile, as the Pali does.
- `mahantatta vepulatta` is `greatness and fullness` in both halves. The pair
  is the hinge of the comparison and has to be the same words each time.
- `nāga` is kept in Pali. The word covers both great serpents and elephants,
  and this discourse does not say which; see the notes.
- The seven awakening factors and their four-part tail follow the established
  wording in SN 46.51 and MN 118. The source abbreviates factors two through
  six; this edition writes all seven out, because the source states the tail
  in full for the first and the last, and the abbreviation stands for nothing
  else.

## Translation

### Growing Big Enough for the Ocean

At Sāvatthī.

"Bhikkhus, relying on the Himalaya, the king of mountains, nāgas grow in body
and gather strength. Once they have grown in body and gathered strength
there, they go down into the little pools. From the little pools they go down
into the big pools. From the big pools they go down into the small rivers.
From the small rivers they go down into the great rivers. From the great
rivers they go down into the sea, the great ocean. And there they come to
greatness and fullness in body.

In just the same way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu who relies on conduct and is
established in conduct, developing the seven awakening factors and cultivating
them, reaches greatness and fullness in qualities.

And how does a bhikkhu who relies on conduct and is established in conduct,
developing the seven awakening factors and cultivating them, reach greatness
and fullness in qualities?

Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu develops:

- the remembering awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading, based
  on quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the discernment-of-qualities awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on
  fading, based on quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the energy awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading, based on
  quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the rejoicing awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading, based
  on quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the relaxation awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading, based
  on quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the composure awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading, based
  on quenching, culminating in relinquishment;
- the dynamic-balance awakening factor, based on seclusion, based on fading,
  based on quenching, culminating in relinquishment.

That, bhikkhus, is how a bhikkhu who relies on conduct and is established in
conduct, developing the seven awakening factors and cultivating them, reaches
greatness and fullness in qualities."

The first discourse is finished.
