## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery. There the Buddha addressed the bhikkhus:
"Bhikkhus."

"Bhadante," those bhikkhus replied.

The Buddha said this:

### The Lion's Roar

"Only here, bhikkhus, is there a recluse; only here a second recluse; only
here a third recluse; only here a fourth recluse. Other schools are empty of
recluses.

That, bhikkhus, is how you should rightly roar your lion's roar.

### What Others Will Ask

It is possible, bhikkhus, that wanderers of other schools might say: 'And on
what confidence, on what strength, do the venerable ones say this — that only
here is there a recluse, only here a second, only here a third, only here a
fourth, and that other schools are empty of recluses?'

Wanderers of other schools who speak that way should be told: 'Friends, four
things have been declared by the Buddha, who knows and sees, the arahant, the
fully awakened one, and it is because we see them in ourselves that we speak
this way.

What four?

Friends, we have confidence in the Teacher, we have confidence in the Dhamma,
we fulfil the precepts, and our companions in the Dhamma are dear and
agreeable to us — both those who live at home and those who have gone forth.

These, friends, are the four things declared by the Buddha, who knows and
sees, the arahant, the fully awakened one, and it is because we see them in
ourselves that we speak this way.'

### When They Claim the Same

It is possible, bhikkhus, that wanderers of other schools might say: 'Friends,
we too have confidence in the Teacher — our own Teacher. We too have confidence
in the Dhamma — our own Dhamma. We too fulfil the precepts — our own precepts.
Our companions in the Dhamma are dear and agreeable to us too, both those who
live at home and those who have gone forth. So what is the distinction here,
friends, what is the difference, what sets you and us apart?'

Wanderers of other schools who speak that way should be told: 'Friends, is the
goal one, or are there many goals?'

Answering rightly, bhikkhus, wanderers of other schools would answer:
'Friends, the goal is one, not many.'

'And is that goal for someone with passion, friends, or for someone free of
passion?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone free of
passion, friends, not for someone with passion.'

'And is that goal for someone with aversion, or for someone free of aversion?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone free of
aversion, friends, not for someone with aversion.'

'And is that goal for someone with delusion, or for someone free of delusion?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone free of
delusion, friends, not for someone with delusion.'

'And is that goal for someone with ignorant wanting, or for someone free of
ignorant wanting?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone free of
ignorant wanting, friends, not for someone with ignorant wanting.'

'And is that goal for someone who takes things personally, or for someone who
does not?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone who does not
take things personally, friends, not for someone who does.'

'And is that goal for someone who understands, or for someone who does not?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone who
understands, friends, not for someone who does not.'

'And is that goal for someone who favours and opposes, or for someone who does
neither?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone who neither
favours nor opposes, friends, not for someone who does.'

'And is that goal for someone who enjoys and delights in proliferation, or for
someone who enjoys and delights in non-proliferation?'

Answering rightly, they would answer: 'That goal is for someone who enjoys and
delights in non-proliferation, friends, not for someone who enjoys and
delights in proliferation.'

### The Two Views

There are these two views, bhikkhus: the view of becoming and the view of
non-becoming.

Any recluses or brahmins who cleave to the view of becoming, who have gone
over to it and hold to it, are opposed to the view of non-becoming.

Any recluses or brahmins who cleave to the view of non-becoming, who have gone
over to it and hold to it, are opposed to the view of becoming.

Any recluses or brahmins who do not discern, as they have come to be, the
arising, the vanishing, the gratification, the danger, and the escape of these
two views — they have passion, they have aversion, they have delusion, they
have ignorant wanting, they take things personally, they do not understand,
they favour and oppose, they enjoy and delight in proliferation. They are not
freed from birth, ageing, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, dissatisfaction,
distress, and despair. They are not freed from dissatisfaction, I say.

Any recluses or brahmins who do discern, as they have come to be, the arising,
the vanishing, the gratification, the danger, and the escape of these two
views — they are free of passion, free of aversion, free of delusion, free of
ignorant wanting, they do not take things personally, they understand, they
neither favour nor oppose, they enjoy and delight in non-proliferation. They
are freed from birth, ageing, and death, from sorrow, lamentation,
dissatisfaction, distress, and despair. They are freed from dissatisfaction, I
say.

### The Four Kinds of Taking Personally

There are these four kinds of taking personally, bhikkhus.

What four?

Sensual clinging, taking views personally, clinging to rules and observances,
and taking self-doctrine personally.

There are some recluses and brahmins, bhikkhus, who claim to teach the full
understanding of all taking personally. They do not rightly describe the full
understanding of all taking personally: they describe the full understanding
of sensual clinging, but not of taking views personally, not of clinging to
rules and observances, not of taking self-doctrine personally.

Why is that?

Because those good recluses and brahmins do not discern three of these as they
have come to be. So although they claim to teach the full understanding of all
taking personally, they do not rightly describe it.

There are some recluses and brahmins who claim to teach the full understanding
of all taking personally. They describe the full understanding of sensual
clinging and of taking views personally, but not of clinging to rules and
observances, not of taking self-doctrine personally.

Why is that?

Because those good recluses and brahmins do not discern two of these as they
have come to be.

There are some recluses and brahmins who claim to teach the full understanding
of all taking personally. They describe the full understanding of sensual
clinging, of taking views personally, and of clinging to rules and
observances, but not of taking self-doctrine personally.

Why is that?

Because those good recluses and brahmins do not discern one of these as it has
come to be.

### Why Confidence There Does Not Lead Anywhere

In a Dhamma and discipline like that, bhikkhus, confidence in the Teacher is
said not to be rightly directed; confidence in the Dhamma is said not to be
rightly directed; fulfilment of the precepts is said not to be rightly
directed; and fondness for companions in the Dhamma is said not to be rightly
directed.

Why is that?

Because that is how it goes, bhikkhus, in a Dhamma and discipline badly
declared, badly proclaimed, not leading out, not conducive to peace, not
declared by a fully awakened one.

But the Tathāgata, bhikkhus, the arahant, the fully awakened one, claiming to
teach the full understanding of all taking personally, does rightly describe
it: he describes the full understanding of sensual clinging, of taking views
personally, of clinging to rules and observances, and of taking self-doctrine
personally.

In a Dhamma and discipline like that, bhikkhus, confidence in the Teacher is
said to be rightly directed; confidence in the Dhamma is said to be rightly
directed; fulfilment of the precepts is said to be rightly directed; and
fondness for companions in the Dhamma is said to be rightly directed.

Why is that?

Because that is how it goes, bhikkhus, in a Dhamma and discipline well
declared, well proclaimed, leading out, conducive to peace, declared by a
fully awakened one.

### Where They Come From

And these four kinds of taking personally, bhikkhus — what is their source?
What is their origin? What are they born from? What produces them?

Ignorant wanting is their source, ignorant wanting is their origin; they are
born from ignorant wanting, and ignorant wanting produces them.

And this ignorant wanting, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin?
What is it born from? What produces it?

Felt experience is its source, felt experience is its origin; it is born from
felt experience, and felt experience produces it.

And this felt experience, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin?
What is it born from? What produces it?

Contact is its source, contact is its origin; it is born from contact, and
contact produces it.

And this contact, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What is it
born from? What produces it?

The six fields of experience are its source, the six fields of experience are
its origin; it is born from the six fields of experience, and the six fields
of experience produce it.

And these six fields of experience, bhikkhus — what is their source? What is
their origin? What are they born from? What produces them?

Name-and-form is their source, name-and-form is their origin; they are born
from name-and-form, and name-and-form produces them.

And this name-and-form, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What
is it born from? What produces it?

Knowing is its source, knowing is its origin; it is born from knowing, and
knowing produces it.

And this knowing, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What is it
born from? What produces it?

Putting things together is its source, putting things together is its origin;
it is born from putting things together, and putting things together produces
it.

And this putting things together, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its
origin? What is it born from? What produces it?

Ignorance is its source, ignorance is its origin; it is born from ignorance,
and ignorance produces it.

### When Ignorance Is Given Up

When a bhikkhu has given up ignorance, bhikkhus, and clear knowledge has
arisen, then with the fading of ignorance and the arising of clear knowledge
they no longer take sensuality personally, no longer take views personally, no
longer take rules and observances personally, no longer take self-doctrine
personally.

Not taking anything personally, they are not agitated. Not agitated, they
personally reach final nibbāna.

They discern: 'Birth is worn away, the spiritual life has been lived, what had
to be done has been done, there is no more of this state of being.'"

That is what the Buddha said. Those bhikkhus were glad and welcomed what the
Buddha had said.

The Cūḷasīhanāda Sutta, the first, is finished.
