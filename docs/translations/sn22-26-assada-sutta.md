## Translation

### The Setting

At Sāvatthī, the Buddha said:

"Bhikkhus, before my awakening, while I was still unawakened but intent on
awakening, this thought arose in me:

### The Question

'What is the gratification in form, what is the danger in it, and what is
the escape from it? What is the gratification in felt experience, what is the
danger in it, and what is the escape from it? What is the gratification in
recognition, what is the danger in it, and what is the escape from it? What is
the gratification in putting-together activities, what is the danger in them,
and what is the escape from them? What is the gratification in knowing, what
is the danger in it, and what is the escape from it?'

### The Answer

"Then this thought arose in me:

'The satisfaction and gladness that arise dependent on form: this is the
gratification in form. That form is impermanent, dissatisfactory, and subject
to change: this is the danger in form. The removal of desire and passion for
form, the abandoning of desire and passion for form: this is the escape from
form.

The satisfaction and gladness that arise dependent on felt experience: this is
the gratification in felt experience. That felt experience is impermanent,
dissatisfactory, and subject to change: this is the danger in felt
experience. The removal of desire and passion for felt experience, the
abandoning of desire and passion for felt experience: this is the escape from
felt experience.

The satisfaction and gladness that arise dependent on recognition: this is the
gratification in recognition. That recognition is impermanent,
dissatisfactory, and subject to change: this is the danger in recognition.
The removal of desire and passion for recognition, the abandoning of desire
and passion for recognition: this is the escape from recognition.

The satisfaction and gladness that arise dependent on putting-together
activities: this is the gratification in putting-together activities. That
putting-together activities are impermanent, dissatisfactory, and subject to
change: this is the danger in putting-together activities. The removal of
desire and passion for putting-together activities, the abandoning of desire
and passion for putting-together activities: this is the escape from
putting-together activities.

The satisfaction and gladness that arise dependent on knowing: this is the
gratification in knowing. That knowing is impermanent, dissatisfactory, and
subject to change: this is the danger in knowing. The removal of desire and
passion for knowing, the abandoning of desire and passion for knowing: this is
the escape from knowing.'

### The Buddha's Declaration

"As long as I did not directly know, as they really are, the gratification in
these five clung-to heaps as gratification, the danger in them as danger, and
the escape from them as escape, I did not claim unsurpassed full awakening in
the world with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, in this generation with its
samaṇas and brahmins, its rulers and its people.

But when I directly knew, as they really are, the gratification in these five
clung-to heaps as gratification, the danger in them as danger, and the escape
from them as escape, then I did claim unsurpassed full awakening in the world
with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, in this generation with its samaṇas and
brahmins, its rulers and its people.

Knowledge and seeing arose in me:

'My release is unshakeable.
This is the last birth.
Now there is no more renewed becoming.'"

The fifth discourse is finished.
