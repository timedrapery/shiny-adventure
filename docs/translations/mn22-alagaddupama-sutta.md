## Translation

### The Setting

At one time the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

### Ariṭṭha's Wrong View

Now at that time a bhikkhu called Ariṭṭha, formerly a vulture-trapper, had
developed the following pernicious view: "As I understand the teaching taught
by the Blessed One, the obstructing qualities named by the Blessed One do not
obstruct the one who engages in them."

Several bhikkhus heard this rumour. They went to the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha and
said: "Is it really true, Reverend Ariṭṭha, that you hold this pernicious
view: 'As I understand the teaching taught by the Blessed One, the obstructing
qualities named by the Blessed One do not obstruct the one who engages in
them'?"

"It is exactly so, reverends. As I understand the teaching taught by the
Blessed One, the obstructing qualities named by the Blessed One do not
obstruct the one who engages in them."

Then, wishing to dissuade the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha from this pernicious view,
those bhikkhus pressed him, challenged him, and argued with him: "Do not say
that, Reverend Ariṭṭha. Do not misrepresent the Blessed One — misrepresenting
the Blessed One is not good, and the Blessed One would not say that. In many
ways, Reverend Ariṭṭha, the Blessed One has named the obstructing qualities as
obstructing, and they do obstruct the one who engages in them. The Blessed One
has said that sensual pleasures offer little gratification and much suffering
and distress, and the danger in them is even greater — comparing them to: a
skeleton of bones; a scrap of meat; a grass torch held against the wind; a pit
of glowing coals; a dream; borrowed goods; fruit on a tree; a butcher's knife
and chopping board; swords and spears; and a snake's head."

But even when pressed in this way, the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha held to that pernicious
view stubbornly, insisting on it. When those bhikkhus were unable to dissuade
him, they went to the Blessed One, bowed, sat to one side, and told him what
had happened.

Then the Blessed One addressed a certain bhikkhu: "Come, bhikkhu. In my name,
tell the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha that the teacher summons him."

"Yes, Bhante," that bhikkhu replied. He went to Ariṭṭha and said: "Reverend
Ariṭṭha, the teacher summons you."

"Yes, reverend," Ariṭṭha replied. He went to the Blessed One, bowed, and sat
to one side. The Blessed One said to him: "Is it really true, Ariṭṭha, that
you hold this pernicious view: 'As I understand the teaching taught by the
Blessed One, the obstructing qualities named by the Blessed One do not
obstruct the one who engages in them'?"

"It is exactly so, Bhante."

"Misguided man, who on earth have you ever known me to teach in that way?
Misguided man, have I not said in many ways that obstructing qualities obstruct
and do obstruct the one who engages in them? I have said that sensual pleasures
offer little gratification and much suffering and distress, and the danger in
them is even greater — comparing them to a skeleton of bones; a scrap of meat;
a grass torch held against the wind; a pit of glowing coals; a dream; borrowed
goods; fruit on a tree; a butcher's knife and chopping board; swords and spears;
and a snake's head. But you, misguided man, by your wrong grasp, misrepresent
me, harm yourself, and accumulate much demerit. That will be for your
long-lasting harm and suffering."

Then the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus: "What do you think, bhikkhus? Has
this bhikkhu Ariṭṭha kindled even a spark of warmth in this teaching and
training?"

"How could he, Bhante? He has not."

When this was said, the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha sat silent, abashed, shoulders
drooping, downcast, dejected, unable to respond.

Knowing this, the Blessed One said: "Misguided man, you will be known by your
own pernicious view. I shall now question the bhikkhus about this."

Then the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus: "Do you understand my teaching as
the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha does, when he misrepresents me by his wrong grasp, harms
himself, and accumulates much demerit?"

"No, Bhante. In many ways the Blessed One has named the obstructing qualities
as obstructing, and they do obstruct the one who engages in them. The Blessed
One has said that sensual pleasures offer little gratification and much
suffering and distress, and the danger in them is even greater — comparing
them to a skeleton of bones; a scrap of meat; a grass torch; a pit of glowing
coals; a dream; borrowed goods; fruit on a tree; a butcher's knife and
chopping board; swords and spears; and a snake's head."

"Good, good, bhikkhus! It is good that you understand my teaching in this way.
For in many ways I have named the obstructing qualities as obstructing, and
they do obstruct the one who engages in them. I have said that sensual
pleasures offer little gratification and much suffering and distress, and the
danger in them is even greater — comparing them to a skeleton of bones; a
scrap of meat; a grass torch; a pit of glowing coals; a dream; borrowed goods;
fruit on a tree; a butcher's knife and chopping board; swords and spears; and
a snake's head. But still this bhikkhu Ariṭṭha misrepresents me by his wrong
grasp, harms himself, and accumulates much demerit. That will be for his
long-lasting harm and suffering.

Bhikkhus, it is quite impossible to engage in sensual pleasures without
sensual desire, sensual recognition, and sensual thinking.

### Wrong Grasp and Right Grasp

Here, some misguided persons learn the teaching — suttas, mixed texts,
analyses, verses, inspired utterances, 'thus it was said' texts, birth stories,
remarkable accounts, and question-and-answer texts. Having learned it, they do
not examine the meaning of those teachings with discernment. Not examining the
meaning with discernment, they do not arrive at a considered acceptance of
those teachings. They learn the teaching for the sake of finding fault and
winning arguments. They do not experience the goal for which they learned.
Those teachings, being wrongly grasped, lead to their long-lasting harm and
suffering. Why is that? Because of wrong grasp.

Suppose, bhikkhus, a person wanted a cobra and went searching for one. They
find a large cobra and grasp it by the coil or the tail. That cobra twists
back and bites them on the hand or the arm or some other limb — resulting in
death or death-level pain. Why is that? Because of wrong grasp of the cobra.

In the same way, those misguided persons learn the teaching — suttas, mixed
texts, analyses, and the rest. Having learned it, they do not examine the
meaning with discernment and do not arrive at a considered acceptance. They
learn for the sake of finding fault and winning arguments. Those teachings,
being wrongly grasped, lead to their long-lasting harm and suffering. Why is
that? Because of wrong grasp.

But here, some gentlemen learn the teaching — suttas, mixed texts, analyses,
verses, inspired utterances, 'thus it was said' texts, birth stories,
remarkable accounts, and question-and-answer texts. Having learned it, they
examine the meaning of those teachings with discernment. Examining the meaning
with discernment, they arrive at a considered acceptance. They do not learn for
the sake of finding fault and winning arguments. They experience the goal for
which they learned. Those teachings, being correctly grasped, lead to their
long-lasting welfare and happiness. Why is that? Because of correct grasp.

Suppose, bhikkhus, a person wanted a cobra and went searching for one. They
find a large cobra, and carefully pin it down with a forked stick. Having
pinned it, they grasp it firmly by the neck. That cobra, though it wraps its
coils around the person's hand or arm or other limbs, does not cause death or
death-level pain. Why is that? Because of correct grasp of the cobra.

In the same way, those gentlemen learn the teaching — suttas, mixed texts,
analyses, and the rest. Having learned it, they examine the meaning with
discernment and arrive at a considered acceptance. Those teachings, being
correctly grasped, lead to their long-lasting welfare and happiness. Why is
that? Because of correct grasp.

Therefore, bhikkhus, when you understand what I have said, remember it in that
way. But if there is anything I have said that you do not understand, ask me
about it, or ask competent bhikkhus.

### The Teaching as a Raft

Bhikkhus, I shall teach you the teaching as like a raft — for crossing over,
not for holding on. Listen carefully, apply your mind well, and I shall speak."

"Yes, Bhante," they replied.

"Suppose, bhikkhus, a person traveling along a road came to a large flood.
The near shore was dubious and dangerous; the far shore was safe and free of
danger. There was no ferryboat or bridge for crossing from this shore to that.
They thought: 'This flood is vast. The near shore is dubious and dangerous;
the far shore is safe and free of danger. There is no ferryboat or bridge.
What if I gathered grass, sticks, branches, and leaves, bound them into a raft,
and riding on that raft, paddling with my hands and feet, made it safely across
to the far shore?'

And so they did exactly that, and made it safely across to the far shore.
Having crossed and arrived at the far shore, they thought: 'This raft has been
very helpful to me. Riding on it and paddling with my hands and feet, I made it
safely across. What if I were now to hoist it on my head or take it on my
shoulder and go wherever I wish?'

What do you think, bhikkhus — would that person be doing what should be done
with that raft?"

"No, Bhante."

"And what should that person do with the raft? Having crossed and arrived at
the far shore, they should think: 'This raft has been very helpful to me.
What if I were to beach it on dry land, or set it adrift on the water, and
then go wherever I wish?' That is what that person should do with the raft.

In the same way, bhikkhus, I have taught the teaching as like a raft — for
crossing over, not for holding on. Understanding the teaching's likeness to a
raft, you should let go even of teachings — let alone what is not the teaching.

### Six Grounds for Views

Bhikkhus, there are these six grounds for views. What six?

Here, an unlearned ordinary person who has not seen the noble ones, who is
unskilled and untrained in the teaching of the noble ones, who has not seen
true persons and is unskilled and untrained in their teaching — regards form
as: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.' They regard felt experience as:
'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.' They regard recognition as: 'This
is mine, I am this, this is my self.' They regard what is put together as:
'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.' They regard whatever is seen,
heard, sensed, known, reached, sought, and explored by the feeling mind as:
'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.' And as for this ground for views
— 'The world and self are one and the same. After death I will be that:
permanent, stable, eternal, not subject to change, lasting as long as eternity
itself' — they regard this too as: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.'

But a learned noble disciple who has seen the noble ones, who is skilled and
trained in their teaching, who has seen true persons and is skilled and trained
in their teaching — regards form as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is not my
self.' They regard felt experience as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is not
my self.' They regard recognition as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is not my
self.' They regard what is put together as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is
not my self.' They regard whatever is seen, heard, sensed, known, reached,
sought, and explored by the feeling mind as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is
not my self.' And as for this ground for views — 'The world and self are one
and the same. After death I will be that: permanent, stable, eternal, not
subject to change, lasting as long as eternity itself' — they regard this too
as: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.'

Seeing in this way, they are not anxious about what does not exist."

### Anxiety About What Doesn't Exist

When this was said, a certain bhikkhu asked: "But, Bhante, can there be anxiety
about what does not exist externally?"

"There can, bhikkhu," the Blessed One said. "Here, someone thinks: 'I once
had it, but no longer have it. I could have it, but I am not getting it.'
They sorrow, wail, and lament, beating their breast and falling into confusion.
That is how there is anxiety about what does not exist externally."

"But can there be no anxiety about what does not exist externally?"

"There can, bhikkhu. Here, someone does not think: 'I once had it, but no
longer have it. I could have it, but I am not getting it.' They do not sorrow,
wail, or lament, beating their breast and falling into confusion. That is how
there is no anxiety about what does not exist externally."

"But, Bhante, can there be anxiety about what does not exist internally?"

"There can, bhikkhu. Here, someone holds this view: 'The world and self are
one and the same. After death I will be that: permanent, stable, eternal, not
subject to change, lasting as long as eternity itself.' They hear the Tathāgata
or a disciple of the Tathāgata teaching the Dhamma for the uprooting of all
grounds for views, fixations, obsessions, insistences, and underlying
tendencies; for the calming of all putting-things-together; for the
relinquishing of all supports; for the ending of ignorant wanting; for fading,
cessation, nibbāna. They think: 'I will be cut off! I will be destroyed! I
will not exist at all!' They sorrow, wail, and lament, beating their breast
and falling into confusion. That is how there is anxiety about what does not
exist internally."

"But can there be no anxiety about what does not exist internally?"

"There can, bhikkhu. Here, someone does not hold this view: 'The world and
self are one and the same. After death I will be that: permanent, stable,
eternal, not subject to change, lasting as long as eternity itself.' They
hear the Tathāgata or a disciple of the Tathāgata teaching the Dhamma for the
uprooting of all grounds for views, fixations, obsessions, insistences, and
underlying tendencies; for the calming of all putting-things-together; for the
relinquishing of all supports; for the ending of ignorant wanting; for fading,
cessation, nibbāna. They do not think: 'I will be cut off! I will be
destroyed! I will not exist at all!' They do not sorrow, wail, or lament,
beating their breast and falling into confusion. That is how there is no
anxiety about what does not exist internally.

### No Permanent Possession, No Safe Self-View

Bhikkhus, take hold of whatever possession is permanent, stable, eternal, not
subject to change, lasting as long as eternity itself. Do you see any such
possession?"

"No, Bhante."

"Good, bhikkhus! I also do not see any such possession.

Bhikkhus, grasp a theory of self such that grasping it would not give rise to
sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair. Do you see any such theory
of self?"

"No, Bhante."

"Good, bhikkhus! I also do not see any such theory of self.

Bhikkhus, rely on a view such that relying on it would not give rise to sorrow,
lamentation, pain, distress, and despair. Do you see any such view?"

"No, Bhante."

"Good, bhikkhus! I also do not see any such view.

Bhikkhus, if a self existed, would there be the thought 'belonging to my
self'?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"And if what belongs to a self existed, would there be the thought 'my self'?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"But since a self and what belongs to a self are not found as a genuine fact,
bhikkhus, is not the following an utterly foolish teaching: 'The world and self
are one and the same. After death I will be that: permanent, stable, eternal,
not subject to change, lasting as long as eternity itself'?"

"It could not be otherwise, Bhante — it is an utterly foolish teaching."

### Impermanent, Suffering, Not Self

What do you think, bhikkhus — is form permanent or impermanent?"

"Impermanent, Bhante."

"And what is impermanent — is it suffering or satisfaction?"

"Suffering, Bhante."

"And what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change — is it fitting to
regard it as: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self'?"

"No, Bhante."

"What do you think — is felt experience permanent or impermanent?... Is
recognition permanent or impermanent?... Is what is put together permanent
or impermanent?... Is knowing permanent or impermanent?"

"Impermanent, Bhante."

"And what is impermanent — is it suffering or satisfaction?"

"Suffering, Bhante."

"And what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change — is it fitting to
regard it as: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self'?"

"No, Bhante."

### The Full Sweep

Therefore, bhikkhus, whatever form there is — past, future, or present;
internal or external; coarse or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near —
all form is to be seen as it really is with right discernment: 'Not mine, I
am not this, this is not my self.'

Whatever felt experience there is — past, future, or present; internal or
external; coarse or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near — all felt
experience is to be seen as it really is with right discernment: 'Not mine, I
am not this, this is not my self.'

Whatever recognition there is... all recognition is to be seen: 'Not mine, I
am not this, this is not my self.'

Whatever is put together... all of what is put together is to be seen: 'Not
mine, I am not this, this is not my self.'

Whatever knowing there is — past, future, or present; internal or external;
coarse or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near — all knowing is to be
seen as it really is with right discernment: 'Not mine, I am not this, this is
not my self.'

Seeing this, bhikkhus, a learned noble disciple grows disenchanted with form,
grows disenchanted with felt experience, grows disenchanted with recognition,
grows disenchanted with what is put together, grows disenchanted with knowing.
Growing disenchanted, they fade. Fading, they are released. Being released, the
knowledge arises: 'Released.' They understand: 'Birth is finished, the
spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is
nothing further for this.'

### The Five Epithets of Liberation

Such a bhikkhu is called: one who has lifted the crossbar; one who has filled
in the moat; one who has pulled up the stake; one who is unlatched; and a
noble one with banner lowered, burden dropped, and fully detached.

And how has a bhikkhu lifted the crossbar? Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned
ignorance, cut it off at the root, made it like a palm stump, done away with
it, so that it is not liable to arise in the future. That is how a bhikkhu has
lifted the crossbar.

And how has a bhikkhu filled in the moat? Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned the
transmigration through births in future becoming, cut it off at the root, made
it like a palm stump, done away with it, so that it is not liable to arise in
the future. That is how a bhikkhu has filled in the moat.

And how has a bhikkhu pulled up the stake? Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned
ignorant wanting, cut it off at the root, made it like a palm stump, done away
with it, so that it is not liable to arise in the future. That is how a bhikkhu
has pulled up the stake.

And how is a bhikkhu unlatched? Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned the five lower
fetters, cut them off at the root, made them like a palm stump, done away with
them, so that they are not liable to arise in the future. That is how a bhikkhu
is unlatched.

And how is a bhikkhu a noble one with banner lowered, burden dropped, and
fully detached? Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned the conceit 'I am', cut it off
at the root, made it like a palm stump, done away with it, so that it is not
liable to arise in the future. That is how a bhikkhu is a noble one with banner
lowered, burden dropped, and fully detached.

### The Liberated One Is Not Found

When a bhikkhu's feeling mind is released in this way, bhikkhus, gods together
with Indra, with the Brahmā, and with Pajāpati, searching, cannot find it:
'This is what the Tathāgata's knowing rests upon.' Why is that? Even in this
very life, bhikkhus, I say the Tathāgata is not to be traced.

### What I Describe Is Suffering and Its Cessation

Although I state and assert this, some recluses and brahmins misrepresent me
with a false and untrue claim: 'The recluse Gotama is an annihilationist — he
describes the cutting off, destruction, and non-existence of an existing being.'
That is not what I am, and not what I say — yet those venerable recluses and
brahmins misrepresent me thus. In the past, as today, bhikkhus, what I describe
is suffering and the cessation of suffering.

If others abuse, attack, harass, and trouble the Tathāgata because of this,
the Tathāgata feels no resentment, no irritation, no bitterness of feeling mind
about it. And if others honor, respect, revere, and venerate the Tathāgata
because of this, the Tathāgata feels no delight, no gladness, no elation of
feeling mind. If they show honor, the Tathāgata simply thinks: 'Such things are
done for me with regard to what was already fully understood in the past.'

Therefore, bhikkhus, if others abuse, attack, harass, and trouble you, do not
make yourselves resentful, irritated, or bitter of feeling mind. And if others
honor, respect, revere, and venerate you, do not make yourselves delighted,
glad, or elated. If they show honor, simply think: 'Such things are done for
us with regard to what was already fully understood in the past.'

### Give Up What Isn't Yours

Therefore, bhikkhus, give up what is not yours. Giving it up will be for your
long-lasting welfare and happiness.

And what is not yours? Form, bhikkhus, is not yours — give it up. Giving it up
will be for your long-lasting welfare and happiness. Felt experience is not
yours — give it up. Giving it up will be for your long-lasting welfare and
happiness. Recognition is not yours — give it up. Giving it up will be for your
long-lasting welfare and happiness. What is put together is not yours — give it
up. Giving it up will be for your long-lasting welfare and happiness. Knowing
is not yours — give it up. Giving it up will be for your long-lasting welfare
and happiness.

What do you think, bhikkhus? If a person were to carry off the grass, sticks,
branches, and leaves of this Jeta's Grove — or burn them or do with them as
they wished — would you think: 'This person is carrying us off, burning us, or
doing with us as they wish'?"

"No, Bhante."

"Why not?"

"Because that is neither self nor belonging to self, Bhante."

"In the same way, bhikkhus, give up what is not yours. Giving it up will be
for your long-lasting welfare and happiness. What is not yours? Form is not
yours — give it up. Felt experience is not yours — give it up. Recognition is
not yours — give it up. What is put together is not yours — give it up.
Knowing is not yours — give it up. Giving it up will be for your long-lasting
welfare and happiness.

### The Teaching Well Explained

The teaching thus well explained by me is open, laid bare, illuminated, and
without patchwork. In this teaching thus well explained, those bhikkhus who
are arahants — with outflows exhausted, who have lived the spiritual life,
done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached the highest goal,
with the fetter of becoming fully exhausted, and fully released through right
understanding — for them there is no further cycle to be found.

In this teaching thus well explained, those bhikkhus who have abandoned the
five lower fetters are all spontaneously reborn — they are extinguished there,
not liable to return from that world.

In this teaching thus well explained, those bhikkhus who have abandoned three
fetters and whose passion, aversion, and delusion are weakened — all of them
are once-returners, who will return to this world one more time and then make
an end of suffering.

In this teaching thus well explained, those bhikkhus who have abandoned three
fetters — all of them are stream-enterers, not liable to fall into the lower
realms, assured, and heading for awakening.

In this teaching thus well explained, those bhikkhus who are followers of the
teaching and followers of confidence — all of them are heading for awakening.

In this teaching thus well explained, those who have even a measure of
confidence and affection for me — all of them are heading for the higher
realms."

That is what the Blessed One said. Satisfied, those bhikkhus approved of the
Blessed One's words.

The second discourse is finished.
