# SN 12.44: Loka Sutta (The World)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 12.44 Translation Notes](sn12-44-loka-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `loka` is rendered `world`. Here it means the world of lived experience as
  it develops through seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, and
  thinking—not the creation or destruction of a planet.
- The sense-contact wording follows MN 148, and the dependent-arising tail
  follows the repository's governed renderings: `felt experience`, `ignorant
  wanting`, `taking personally`, `becoming`, `birth`, and `quenching`.
- The source writes the eye and mind cases and abbreviates the four between
  them. This edition names all six sense doors, then prints their shared
  sequence once in each half instead of repeating the same tail six times.

## Translation

### How the World Arises

At Sāvatthī, the Buddha said:

"Bhikkhus, I will teach you how the world arises and how it ends. Listen and
pay close attention. I will speak."

"Yes, Bhante," the bhikkhus replied.

The Buddha said this:

"And how does the world arise?

Dependent on the eye and visible forms, eye-knowing arises. The meeting of
the three is contact.

The same sequence begins at each of the other sense doors:

- dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-knowing arises
- dependent on the nose and smells, nose-knowing arises
- dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-knowing arises
- dependent on the body and touchable things, body-knowing arises
- dependent on the mind and ideas, mind-knowing arises

In every case, the meeting of the three is contact.

With contact as condition, felt experience;
with felt experience as condition, ignorant wanting;
with ignorant wanting as condition, taking personally;
with taking personally as condition, becoming;
with becoming as condition, birth;
with birth as condition, ageing and dying, sorrow, lamentation,
dissatisfaction, distress, and despair come to be.

This is how the world arises.

### How the World Ends

And how does the world end?

Dependent on the eye and visible forms, eye-knowing arises. The meeting of
the three is contact.

The same sequence begins at each of the other sense doors:

- dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-knowing arises
- dependent on the nose and smells, nose-knowing arises
- dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-knowing arises
- dependent on the body and touchable things, body-knowing arises
- dependent on the mind and ideas, mind-knowing arises

In every case, the meeting of the three is contact.

With contact as condition, felt experience;
with felt experience as condition, ignorant wanting.

With the complete fading and quenching of that very ignorant wanting,
taking personally is quenched;
with the quenching of taking personally, becoming is quenched;
with the quenching of becoming, birth is quenched;
with the quenching of birth, ageing and dying, sorrow, lamentation,
dissatisfaction, distress, and despair are quenched.

That is how this whole heap of dissatisfaction quenches.

This is how the world ends."

The fourth discourse is finished.
