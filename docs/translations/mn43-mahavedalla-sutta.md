# MN 43: Mahāvedalla Sutta (The Longer Analysis)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of MN 43, designed to be readable as
  a single Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [MN 43 Translation Notes](mn43-mahavedalla-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of MN 43 as segmented in SuttaCentral's Bilara
  data.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `paññā` is rendered
  `discernment`; `viññāṇa` is rendered `knowing`; `vedanā` is rendered `felt
  experience`; `saññā` is rendered `recognition`; `cetovimutti` is rendered
  `release of mind`; `paññāvimutti` is rendered `release through discernment`;
  `suññatā` is rendered `emptiness`; `animitta` is rendered `signless`;
  `nimitta` is rendered `sign`; `jhāna` is rendered `mental theme`; `saṅkhāra`
  compounds keep the governed `conditioner` family; and the four `nīvaraṇa`
  are rendered as the four `distraction` compounds.
- This edition treats MN 43 as the control passage for the
  emptiness / signless / wishless interface, for the relation between
  discernment and knowing, and for the four releases of mind that are
  different in letter and one in meaning.
- MN 43 and [MN 44](mn44-culavedalla-sutta.md) are the paired Vedalla
  discourses and share the threefold conditioner set and the attainment of
  cessation. Wording is held in common between them deliberately.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's MN 43 translation
  surface.

## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's park.

Then in the late afternoon Venerable Mahākoṭṭhika came out of seclusion and
went to Venerable Sāriputta. He exchanged greetings with him, and after
sharing a courteous and memorable conversation, sat to one side. Sitting
there, he said to Venerable Sāriputta:

### Poor In Discernment

"Friend, people say, 'poor in discernment, poor in discernment.'

In what way, friend, is someone said to be poor in discernment?"

"'They do not discern, they do not discern,' friend: that is why someone is
said to be poor in discernment.

And what do they not discern?

They do not discern 'this is dissatisfaction.' They do not discern 'this is
the origin of dissatisfaction.' They do not discern 'this is the quenching of
dissatisfaction.' They do not discern 'this is the path of practice leading to
the quenching of dissatisfaction.'

'They do not discern, they do not discern,' friend: that is why someone is
said to be poor in discernment."

"Good, friend," Venerable Mahākoṭṭhika said. He was pleased with what
Venerable Sāriputta had said, and he agreed with it. Then he asked a further
question:

### Discerning

"Friend, people say, 'discerning, discerning.'

In what way, friend, is someone said to be discerning?"

"'They discern, they discern,' friend: that is why someone is said to be
discerning.

And what do they discern?

They discern 'this is dissatisfaction.' They discern 'this is the origin of
dissatisfaction.' They discern 'this is the quenching of dissatisfaction.'
They discern 'this is the path of practice leading to the quenching of
dissatisfaction.'

'They discern, they discern,' friend: that is why someone is said to be
discerning."

### Knowing

"Friend, people say, 'knowing, knowing.'

In what way, friend, is it said to be knowing?"

"'It knows, it knows,' friend: that is why it is said to be knowing.

And what does it know?

It knows 'satisfying,' it knows 'dissatisfying,' and it knows 'neither
dissatisfying nor satisfying.'

'It knows, it knows,' friend: that is why it is said to be knowing."

### Discernment and Knowing

"Friend, discernment and knowing: are these dhammas mixed together or
separate? And is it possible to take them apart, again and again, and
describe the difference between them?"

"Friend, discernment and knowing are mixed together, not separate. It is not
possible to take them apart, again and again, and describe the difference
between them.

For what someone discerns, friend, they know; and what they know, they
discern.

That is why these dhammas are mixed together, not separate, and why it is not
possible to take them apart, again and again, and describe the difference
between them."

"Friend, discernment and knowing: these dhammas are mixed together, not
separate. What then is the difference between them?"

"Friend, discernment and knowing are mixed together, not separate. Of these,
discernment is to be developed, and knowing is to be fully understood.

That is the difference between them."

### Felt Experience

"Friend, people say, 'felt experience, felt experience.'

In what way, friend, is it said to be felt experience?"

"'It feels, it feels,' friend: that is why it is said to be felt experience.

And what does it feel?

It feels what is satisfying, it feels what is dissatisfying, and it feels what
is neither dissatisfying nor satisfying.

'It feels, it feels,' friend: that is why it is said to be felt experience."

### Recognition

"Friend, people say, 'recognition, recognition.'

In what way, friend, is it said to be recognition?"

"'It recognizes, it recognizes,' friend: that is why it is said to be
recognition.

And what does it recognize?

It recognizes blue, it recognizes yellow, it recognizes red, and it
recognizes white.

'It recognizes, it recognizes,' friend: that is why it is said to be
recognition."

### Felt Experience, Recognition, and Knowing

"Friend, felt experience, recognition, and knowing: are these dhammas mixed
together or separate? And is it possible to take them apart, again and again,
and describe the difference between them?"

"Friend, felt experience, recognition, and knowing are mixed together, not
separate. It is not possible to take them apart, again and again, and describe
the difference between them.

For what someone feels, friend, they recognize; and what they recognize, they
know.

That is why these dhammas are mixed together, not separate, and why it is not
possible to take them apart, again and again, and describe the difference
between them."

### What Purified Mind-Knowing Reaches

"Friend, what can be reached by purified mind-knowing, released from the five
faculties?"

"Friend, by purified mind-knowing, released from the five faculties, the
sphere of boundless space can be reached as 'space is boundless'; the sphere
of boundless knowing can be reached as 'knowing is boundless'; and the sphere
of nothingness can be reached as 'there is nothing.'"

"Friend, with what does someone discern a dhamma that can be reached?"

"Friend, someone discerns a dhamma that can be reached with the eye of
discernment."

"Friend, what is the purpose of discernment?"

"Friend, the purpose of discernment is higher knowing, full understanding, and
abandoning."

### The Conditions for Right View

"Friend, how many conditions are there for the arising of right view?"

"Friend, there are two conditions for the arising of right view: the word of
another, and wise attention.

These, friend, are the two conditions for the arising of right view."

"Friend, by how many factors is right view supported when it has release of
mind as its fruit and benefit, and release through discernment as its fruit
and benefit?"

"Friend, right view is supported by five factors when it has release of mind
as its fruit and benefit, and release through discernment as its fruit and
benefit.

Here, friend, right view is supported by conduct, supported by learning,
supported by discussion, supported by tranquility, and supported by insight.

By these five factors, friend, right view is supported when it has release of
mind as its fruit and benefit, and release through discernment as its fruit
and benefit."

### Becoming

"Friend, how many kinds of becoming are there?"

"Friend, there are these three kinds of becoming: sensual existence, form
existence, and formless existence."

"Friend, how does the production of renewed becoming in the future come to
be?"

"Friend, for beings hindered by ignorance and fettered by ignorant wanting,
delighting now here and now there: that is how the production of renewed
becoming in the future comes to be."

"Friend, how does the production of renewed becoming in the future not come to
be?"

"Friend, with the fading of ignorance, the arising of true knowledge, and the
quenching of ignorant wanting: that is how the production of renewed becoming
in the future does not come to be."

### The First Mental Theme

"Friend, what is the first mental theme?"

"Here, friend, quite secluded from sensuality, secluded from unwholesome
dhammas, a bhikkhu enters and remains in the first mental theme, which has
thinking and pondering, and the rejoicing and satisfaction born of seclusion.

This, friend, is called the first mental theme."

"Friend, how many factors does the first mental theme have?"

"Friend, the first mental theme has five factors.

Here, friend, for a bhikkhu who has entered the first mental theme there occur
thinking, pondering, rejoicing, satisfaction, and directness of feeling mind.

That, friend, is how the first mental theme has five factors."

"Friend, how many factors has the first mental theme left behind, and how many
factors does it possess?"

"Friend, the first mental theme has left behind five factors and possesses
five factors.

Here, friend, for a bhikkhu who has entered the first mental theme, sensual
distraction is abandoned, aversive distraction is abandoned, dull distraction
is abandoned, agitated distraction is abandoned, and uncertain distraction is
abandoned;

and there occur thinking, pondering, rejoicing, satisfaction, and directness
of feeling mind.

That, friend, is how the first mental theme has left behind five factors and
possesses five factors."

### The Five Faculties and What They Rely On

"Friend, there are these five faculties with different scopes and different
ranges, and they do not experience one another's range and scope. They are:

- the eye faculty
- the ear faculty
- the nose faculty
- the tongue faculty
- the body faculty

Now, friend, these five faculties have different scopes and different ranges,
and they do not experience one another's range and scope. What is their
resort, and what experiences their range and scope?"

"Friend, there are these five faculties with different scopes and different
ranges, and they do not experience one another's range and scope. They are:

- the eye faculty
- the ear faculty
- the nose faculty
- the tongue faculty
- the body faculty

Now, friend, these five faculties have different scopes and different ranges,
and they do not experience one another's range and scope. The thinking mind is
their resort, and the thinking mind experiences their range and scope."

"Friend, there are these five faculties. They are:

- the eye faculty
- the ear faculty
- the nose faculty
- the tongue faculty
- the body faculty

Depending on what, friend, do these five faculties remain?"

"Friend, there are these five faculties. They are:

- the eye faculty
- the ear faculty
- the nose faculty
- the tongue faculty
- the body faculty

Depending on vitality, friend, these five faculties remain."

"Depending on what, friend, does vitality remain?"

"Vitality remains depending on warmth."

"Depending on what, friend, does warmth remain?"

"Warmth remains depending on vitality."

"Just now, friend, we understood Venerable Sāriputta to say, 'vitality remains
depending on warmth.' And just now, friend, we understood Venerable Sāriputta
to say, 'warmth remains depending on vitality.'

How, friend, should the meaning of this statement be seen?"

"Then, friend, I will give you a simile. Some intelligent people here
understand the meaning of a statement through a simile.

Suppose, friend, an oil lamp were burning. Its light appears depending on its
flame, and its flame appears depending on its light.

In just the same way, friend, vitality remains depending on warmth, and
warmth remains depending on vitality."

### Vitality Conditioners and Felt Dhammas

"Friend, are the vitality conditioners the very same as the dhammas that are
felt, or are the vitality conditioners one thing and the dhammas that are felt
another?"

"Friend, the vitality conditioners are not the very same as the dhammas that
are felt.

For if the vitality conditioners were the very same as the dhammas that are
felt, friend, no emergence would be discerned for a bhikkhu who had entered
the cessation of recognition and felt experience.

It is because the vitality conditioners are one thing and the dhammas that are
felt another, friend, that emergence is discerned for a bhikkhu who has
entered the cessation of recognition and felt experience."

### When the Body Is Left Behind

"Friend, when how many dhammas leave this body does it lie discarded and cast
aside like an unfeeling log?"

"Friend, when three dhammas leave this body -- vitality, warmth, and knowing
-- it lies discarded and cast aside like an unfeeling log."

"Friend, what is the difference between someone who has died and passed away,
and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of recognition and felt
experience?"

"Friend, for someone who has died and passed away, the body conditioner has
ceased and subsided, the verbal conditioner has ceased and subsided, the mind
conditioner has ceased and subsided, vitality is exhausted, warmth is stilled,
and the faculties are broken up.

For a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of recognition and felt
experience, the body conditioner has ceased and subsided, the verbal
conditioner has ceased and subsided, the mind conditioner has ceased and
subsided, but vitality is not exhausted, warmth is not stilled, and the
faculties are bright.

That, friend, is the difference between someone who has died and passed away
and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of recognition and felt
experience."

### The Neither-Dissatisfying-Nor-Satisfying Release of Mind

"Friend, how many conditions are there for entering the release of mind that
is neither dissatisfying nor satisfying?"

"Friend, there are four conditions for entering the release of mind that is
neither dissatisfying nor satisfying.

Here, friend, with the abandoning of satisfaction and the abandoning of
dissatisfaction, and with the earlier ending of gladness and sadness, a
bhikkhu enters and remains in the fourth mental theme, which is neither
dissatisfying nor satisfying and has the purity of dynamic balance and
remembering.

These, friend, are the four conditions for entering the release of mind that
is neither dissatisfying nor satisfying."

### The Signless Release of Mind

"Friend, how many conditions are there for entering the signless release of
mind?"

"Friend, there are two conditions for entering the signless release of mind:
not attending to any signs, and attending to the signless element.

These, friend, are the two conditions for entering the signless release of
mind."

"Friend, how many conditions are there for remaining in the signless release
of mind?"

"Friend, there are three conditions for remaining in the signless release of
mind: not attending to any signs, attending to the signless element, and the
earlier putting together.

These, friend, are the three conditions for remaining in the signless release
of mind."

"Friend, how many conditions are there for emerging from the signless release
of mind?"

"Friend, there are two conditions for emerging from the signless release of
mind: attending to all signs, and not attending to the signless element.

These, friend, are the two conditions for emerging from the signless release
of mind."

### Different in Meaning, or Different Only in Letter

"Friend, there is the measureless release of mind, the release of mind through
nothingness, the release of mind through emptiness, and the signless release
of mind. Are these dhammas different in meaning and different in letter, or
are they one in meaning and different only in letter?"

"Friend, there is the measureless release of mind, the release of mind through
nothingness, the release of mind through emptiness, and the signless release
of mind. There is a mode of exposition, friend, by which these dhammas are
different in meaning and different in letter;

and there is a mode of exposition, friend, by which these dhammas are one in
meaning and different only in letter.

And what, friend, is the mode of exposition by which these dhammas are
different in meaning and different in letter?

Here, friend, a bhikkhu remains pervading one direction with a feeling mind
imbued with friendliness, and likewise the second, the third, and the fourth.
So above, below, across, everywhere, to all as to himself, he remains
pervading the whole world with a feeling mind imbued with friendliness,
abundant, expansive, measureless, without hostility, without ill will.

He remains pervading with a feeling mind imbued with kindness towards
others ...

with a feeling mind imbued with gladness ...

He remains pervading one direction with a feeling mind imbued with dynamic
balance, and likewise the second, the third, and the fourth. So above, below,
across, everywhere, to all as to himself, he remains pervading the whole world
with a feeling mind imbued with dynamic balance, abundant, expansive,
measureless, without hostility, without ill will.

This, friend, is called the measureless release of mind.

And what, friend, is the release of mind through nothingness?

Here, friend, by completely surmounting the sphere of boundless knowing, a
bhikkhu enters and remains in the sphere of nothingness, aware that 'there is
nothing.'

This, friend, is called the release of mind through nothingness.

And what, friend, is the release of mind through emptiness?

Here, friend, a bhikkhu who has gone to the wilderness, or to the root of a
tree, or to an empty dwelling, reflects like this:

'This is empty of a self or of what belongs to a self.'

This, friend, is called the release of mind through emptiness.

And what, friend, is the signless release of mind?

Here, friend, by not attending to any signs, a bhikkhu enters and remains in
the signless composure of mind.

This, friend, is called the signless release of mind.

This, friend, is the mode of exposition by which these dhammas are different
in meaning and different in letter.

### One in Meaning, Different Only in Letter

And what, friend, is the mode of exposition by which these dhammas are one in
meaning and different only in letter?

Passion, friend, is a maker of measurement; aversion is a maker of
measurement; delusion is a maker of measurement.

For a bhikkhu whose outflows are exhausted, these are abandoned, cut off at
the root, made like a palm stump, done away with, and unable to arise in the
future.

Of all the measureless releases of mind, friend, the unshakable release of
mind is declared the foremost.

And that unshakable release of mind is empty of passion, empty of aversion,
empty of delusion.

Passion, friend, is a something; aversion is a something; delusion is a
something.

For a bhikkhu whose outflows are exhausted, these are abandoned, cut off at
the root, made like a palm stump, done away with, and unable to arise in the
future.

Of all the releases of mind through nothingness, friend, the unshakable
release of mind is declared the foremost.

And that unshakable release of mind is empty of passion, empty of aversion,
empty of delusion.

Passion, friend, is a maker of signs; aversion is a maker of signs; delusion
is a maker of signs.

For a bhikkhu whose outflows are exhausted, these are abandoned, cut off at
the root, made like a palm stump, done away with, and unable to arise in the
future.

Of all the signless releases of mind, friend, the unshakable release of mind
is declared the foremost.

And that unshakable release of mind is empty of passion, empty of aversion,
empty of delusion.

This, friend, is the mode of exposition by which these dhammas are one in
meaning and different only in letter."

### Closing

That is what Venerable Sāriputta said.

Venerable Mahākoṭṭhika was satisfied and delighted in what Venerable Sāriputta
had said.
