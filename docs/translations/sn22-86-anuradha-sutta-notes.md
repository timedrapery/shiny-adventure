# SN 22.86 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[SN 22.86: Anurādha Sutta](sn22-86-anuradha-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of SN 22.86 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`sn22.86_root-pli-ms.json`).
- Control surfaces: the repository's current term policy, especially
  `tathāgata`, `rūpa`, `vedanā`, `saññā`, `saṅkhārā`, `viññāṇa`, `anicca`,
  `dukkha`, `attā`, and `nirodha`; the five-heaps argument and release sequence
  in SN 22.59; and the four post-death positions in MN 63 and DN 15.
- Working method: every Bilara segment was represented in order. The
  narrative retelling abbreviated by `pe` remains condensed, while the
  doctrinal heap sequence abbreviated by `pe` is expanded because the
  questions that follow depend on seeing all five members.

## Governing Decisions

- `tathāgata` remains `Tathāgata`. The passage uses the title as the very term
  under examination, so replacing it with `Buddha`, `awakened person`, or an
  etymological guess would settle what the discourse keeps technically open.
- The reader title `Can You Pin Down the Tathāgata?` comes from the climactic
  `anupalabbhiyamāne` question. It states the practical problem without
  turning one of the four rejected post-death claims into the title.
- `uttamapuriso paramapuriso paramapattipatto` is rendered `the highest kind
  of person, a supreme person who has reached the highest attainment`. The
  repetition is emphatic. The English keeps that emphasis but avoids the
  elevated `superlative man` register.
- `aññatra imehi catūhi ṭhānehi` is rendered `apart from these four
  positions`. Anurādha's answer does not add a hidden fifth proposition. The
  Buddha later repeats the answer and shows that framing the Tathāgata as
  somehow outside the four is still not a sound declaration.
- `navo ... acirapabbajito` becomes `new and recently gone forth`; `thero vā
  pana bālo abyatto` becomes `a senior bhikkhu who is foolish and
  incompetent`. This keeps the wanderers' insult direct without antique
  language.
- The stock accuracy question is recast as three speakable questions: repeat
  what the Buddha said, do not falsely misrepresent him, and explain in line
  with the Dhamma without giving sound grounds for criticism.
- The heap sequence reuses SN 22.59's governed wording: `form`, `felt
  experience`, `recognition`, `putting-together activities`, and `knowing`;
  `impermanent`; `dissatisfaction`; `subject to change`; and `This is mine, I
  am this, this is my self`.
- The de-appropriation and release sequence likewise reuses SN 22.59. The
  source marks the whole continuation with `pe`, but explicitly cues the
  standard `tasmātiha` and `evaṁ passaṁ` sequence and its final line. Expanding
  it makes the bridge from not-self to the Tathāgata questions visible.
- `rūpaṁ tathāgatoti samanupassasi` and its parallels become `Do you regard
  form as the Tathāgata?` The following `rūpasmiṁ` / `aññatra rūpā` pairs
  become `the Tathāgata as being in form` / `apart from form`.
- The five-member question at segment 10.2 is rendered with `taken together`.
  English otherwise makes the list sound like five new separate questions,
  duplicating the section before it. The Pali presents the five-member
  combination as one further identification.
- `ayaṁ so arūpī ... aviññāṇo tathāgato` becomes `the Tathāgata as someone who
  has no form ... and no knowing`. The translation does not introduce a
  disembodied essence; the question rejects that proposed description too.
- `diṭṭheva dhamme saccato thetato tathāgate anupalabbhiyamāne` becomes
  `since you cannot pin down the Tathāgata as something true and real even
  here and now`. `Pin down` is ordinary English for the unsuccessful act of
  establishing the Tathāgata under one of the proposed descriptions. It does
  not translate the line as `the Tathāgata does not exist`, which would simply
  choose one of the four rejected claims.
- `dukkhañceva paññapemi, dukkhassa ca nirodhaṁ` is rendered `I describe only
  dissatisfaction and the ending of dissatisfaction`. `Dissatisfaction`
  is the governed `dukkha` default. `Ending` is a recorded `nirodha` alternate
  and is already the wording used by the repository's SN 22.86 scope note; it
  makes the compact closing sentence immediately understandable.

## Source-Audit Calls

- The Pali root does not include an `evaṁ me sutaṁ` opening. The translation
  begins where the root begins, with `At one time`, rather than importing `So
  I have heard` from the usual setting formula.
- Segments 4.1 through 6.3 abbreviate Anurādha's report to the Buddha. The
  translation says that he told the Buddha what happened instead of silently
  manufacturing a second full copy of the dialogue.
- Segments 7.9 through 7.16 abbreviate the middle heaps, the complete
  de-appropriation refrain, and the release sequence. The Pali gives the
  opening and closing cues of the known SN 22 formula. This edition expands
  that doctrinal sequence in full because the next four question sets test
  every possible relation between the Tathāgata and those same five members.
- Segments 8.4 through 11.2 also use ellipsis. The translation expands every
  marked member and relation so a reader does not have to reconstruct the
  central argument from punctuation.
- The four post-death positions use the same governed wording as MN 63 and DN
  15: exists, does not exist, both, or neither after death.
- The final line limits what the Buddha describes; it does not answer the
  post-death question with a hidden fifth theory.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse does not teach that the Tathāgata `does not exist`. That is
  one of the positions it refuses to establish.
- Nor does it teach that the Tathāgata is a separate essence outside the five
  heaps. That is Anurādha's attempted answer, and the Buddha tells him it is
  not a sound declaration.
- The questioning moves from appropriation to description. Once form, felt
  experience, recognition, putting-together activities, and knowing are not
  fit to be treated as self, none of them supplies a fixed basis for pinning
  down the Tathāgata.
- The closing turn to dissatisfaction and its ending keeps the exchange
  practical. Speculation about what remains after death is not replaced with
  a new metaphysical scheme.

## Editorial Presentation

- The headings are editorial navigation and do not stand in the Pali root.
- The opening four positions are set as separate paragraphs so their logical
  alternatives remain easy to compare on a narrow screen or when read aloud.
- The repeated `No, Bhante` answers remain separate. Their rhythm is the
  argument; compressing them into a summary would make the central refusal
  less visible.
- The public reader places this text after the direct five-heaps definition in
  SN 22.48 and before the subtler residual `I am` analysis in SN 22.89.
