# AN 3.88 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[AN 3.88: Tatiyasikkhā Sutta](an3-88-tatiyasikkha-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `an3.88:1.1`–`an3.88:3.3`, stored locally in
  `.bilara-cache/an3.88_root-pli-ms.json`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/an3.88/pli/ms>.
- Cross-check: Bhikkhu Sujato's published English segmentation in
  SuttaCentral's Bilara data was used to confirm the source boundary and the
  five non-returner categories. Its wording does not govern this edition.
- The source has about 230 Pali words after collection and chapter headings are
  excluded.
- The title `Tatiyasikkhāsutta` means "The Third Discourse on Training."

## Term-Family Inventory

The source brings several already-governed families together in running prose:

- `sikkhā` and `sikkhāpada`: `training` and `training rule`
- `adhisīla`, `adhicitta`, and `adhipaññā`: `higher conduct`, `higher
  mind`, and `higher discernment`
- `sīla`, `samādhi`, and `paññā`: `conduct`, `composure`, and
  `discernment`
- `brahmacariya`: `spiritual life`
- `āsava`, `khaya`, `cetovimutti`, and `paññāvimutti`: `outflows`,
  `wearing away`, `release of mind`, and `release through discernment`
- `saṃyojana`, `loka`, and `dukkha`: `fetter`, `world`, and
  `dissatisfaction`

The main collision is structural rather than lexical. The compounds in the
opening are names of trainings, while the next paragraph uses their three
domains without the `adhi-` prefix. The translation therefore says `training
in higher conduct` but later says `fulfills conduct, composure, and
discernment`.

## Governing Decisions

- **More than 150 rules are recited every two weeks.** `sādhika ...
  diyaḍḍhasikkhāpadasataṃ` is an amount above one and a half hundred.
  `anvaddhamāsaṃ` is the half-monthly recitation, expressed in ordinary English
  as `every two weeks` rather than `fortnightly`.
- **The people entering training are described, not labeled.** `attakāmā
  kulaputtā` is translated `young men from respectable families who want what
  is good for themselves`. This keeps the male historical setting while
  avoiding the opaque governed label `good-family sons` on the reader surface.
- **The three compounds stay visibly connected to training.** The list uses
  `training in higher conduct`, `training in higher mind`, and `training in
  higher discernment`. This applies the stable headword renderings without
  turning the compounds into three unexplained abstract qualities.
- **Minor faults do not erase the path.** `āpajjati ... vuṭṭhāti` refers to
  committing a lesser offense and completing the prescribed restoration.
  `Make things right and return to good standing` states both movements in
  language a reader does not need monastic legal training to understand.
- **`abhabbatā` is made explicit from context.** The Pali says the practitioner
  is not thereby incapable, without repeating the object. `Incapable of
  success` anticipates the closing contrast between complete and partial
  success and prevents a dangling `incapable of that` in English.
- **The release formula reuses the AN 4.5 control.** `āsavānaṃ khayā` is
  `with the wearing away of the outflows`; `cetovimutti` and
  `paññāvimutti` remain separately audible as `release of mind` and `release
  through discernment`; and the realization occurs `here and now`.
- **`taṃ ... anabhisambhavaṃ appaṭivijjhaṃ` is `if they do not get that
  far`.** This plain sentence carries the repeated pair of not attaining and
  not penetrating without making each rung sound like a legal formula.
- **The five non-returner types are translated as outcomes.** The source names
  `antarāparinibbāyī`, `upahaccaparinibbāyī`,
  `asaṅkhāraparinibbāyī`, `sasaṅkhāraparinibbāyī`, and
  `uddhaṃsoto akaniṭṭhagāmī`. The reader gets the distinctions—between
  lives, soon after rebirth, without extra effort, with extra effort, and
  upstream to Akaniṭṭha—without having to learn five Pali labels first.
  `Akaniṭṭha` is retained as a proper name and glossed as the Highest Realm.
- **The three stream-enterer outcomes are also descriptive.** `ekabījī`,
  `kolaṅkolo`, and `sattakkhattuparamo` become one more human life, rebirth
  in two or three families, and at most seven rebirths among gods and humans.
  The details are in the source itself, so the English foregrounds them rather
  than the traditional category names.
- **`avañjha` is `not wasted`.** The agricultural image literally says the
  rules are not barren or fruitless. `None of these training rules is wasted`
  states the practical force in contemporary English and joins it to the
  immediately preceding full/partial contrast.

## Repetition Handling

Segments `2.7`–`2.11` repeat the same lead-in and the complete wearing away of
the five lower fetters before naming one outcome each. The translation prints
that shared condition once and presents the five outcomes as a list. Segments
`2.13`–`2.15` similarly repeat the wearing away of three fetters; that shared
condition is also printed once before a list. No attainment or condition is
removed.

## Reader Placement

The discourse follows SN 45.8 in Stage 2. After the path has been named and
defined, AN 3.88 gives a second organizing frame: all the detailed training can
be gathered into conduct, the mind, and discernment. The newcomer introduction
explicitly tells readers that the technical middle ladder is evidence for the
closing point, not a list they must memorize.
