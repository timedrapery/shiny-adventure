# MN 121 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 121: Cūḷasuññata Sutta](mn121-culasunnata-sutta.md). The main translation
is the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 121 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`mn121:1.1` to `mn121:13.8`), fetched from the upstream Bilara
  root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: MN 122 for `suññatā`, `pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati`,
  and `arañña`; MN 26 for the names of the formless spheres; MN 43 for
  `animitta cetosamādhi`; MN 36 for the release of the heart from the three
  outflows and the knowledge that follows; MN 118 for the setting; MN 18 for
  the setting formula and the close.
- Working method: the discourse is one pattern applied nine times. Each step
  stops attending to two recognitions, attends to the oneness of the next,
  and then sees what the new state is empty of and what remains. The source
  writes every step out in full, and so does the translation, because the
  steady repetition is how the teaching works.

## Governing Decisions

- `suññatā` is `emptiness` and `suñña` `empty`. `suññatāvihāra` is
  `dwelling in emptiness`, following MN 122's `way of dwelling`.
- `saññā` is `recognition`, its governed default. `saññāgata` is `field of
  recognition`: what is present to recognition at that step.
- `ekatta` is `oneness`. `X-saññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ` is
  `attends to the oneness that depends on the recognition of X`. The Pali
  keeps `oneness` and the recognition it depends on apart, and so does the
  translation.
- `pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati` is `leaps forward, becomes
  clear, settles, and commits to it`. The first three follow MN 122. MN 122's
  fourth verb is `vimuccati` (`is released`); here it is `adhimuccati`,
  which names committing to the object, so it is rendered differently.
- `daratha` is `disturbance`, and `darathamattā` `this much disturbance`.
  `daratha` has no record.
- `gāmasaññā`, `manussasaññā`, `araññasaññā`, and `pathavīsaññā` are `the
  recognition of the village`, `of people`, `of the forest`, and `of earth`.
  `arañña` is `forest`, as in MN 122's list of secluded lodgings.
- The formless spheres keep MN 26's names: `sphere of boundless space`,
  `sphere of boundless knowing`, `sphere of nothingness`, and `sphere of
  neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition`.
- `animitta cetosamādhi` is `signless unification of mind`, the record and
  MN 43's wording.
- `abhisaṅkhata abhisañcetayita` is `constructed and intended`, and
  `aniccaṁ nirodhadhammaṁ` is `impermanent and subject to cessation`.
- The release passage follows MN 36: `the heart is released from the sensual
  outflow, from the outflow of becoming, and from the outflow of ignorance.
  When it is released, there is the knowledge: "It is released." ... Birth is
  worn away, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been
  done, there is no more of this state of being.`
- `imameva kāyaṁ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṁ jīvitapaccayā` is `the six fields of
  experience that depend on this very body and are conditioned by life`.
  `saḷāyatana` keeps its governed `six fields of experience`.
- `yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti` is `this is how they
  come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion, and purely`, and the
  last step adds `paramānuttarā`, `in the highest and unsurpassed way`.
- `santamidaṁ atthi` is `this is present`.
- The mansion of Migāra's Mother (`migāramātupāsāda`) in the Eastern
  Monastery (`pubbārāma`) is named as in MN 118.

## Re-audit Calls

- MN 121 repeats the signless step twice (`mn121:10` and `mn121:11`). In the
  first, the bhikkhu sees what the signless unification of mind is empty of.
  In the second, they see that it too is constructed and intended, and so
  impermanent, and the heart is released. The translation keeps both.
- `santi` in `tedha na santi` is the verb `are`, not the noun `peace`. It
  is not cited for the orphan record `santi`.
- `Cūḷasuññatasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ paṭhamaṁ` is rendered `The first discourse is
  finished.`

## Practice Clarifications

- Emptiness in MN 121 is always empty of something. Each step names what is
  absent and what remains, and the text tells the reader to regard what
  remains as simply present. It does not explain emptiness as a void or as a
  metaphysical claim, and the translation does not either.
- Each step is a real simplification of experience, not a doctrine to accept:
  the village and the people drop away in the forest, the forest drops away
  in the recognition of earth, and so on.
- The turn comes at the end. Even the signless unification of mind is seen
  as constructed and intended, so impermanent. Seeing that releases the
  heart, and what remains is only this body with its six fields, as long as
  it lives.
- MN 122 describes the Tathāgata's way of dwelling in emptiness and how to
  train toward it; MN 121 lays out the gradual entry step by step. Read
  together, they are the repository's teaching on emptiness as practice.
