# AN 11.12: Dutiyamahānāma Sutta (With Mahānāma, the Second)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [AN 11.12 Translation Notes](an11-12-dutiyamahanama-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The Pali gives the recollection of the Buddha and the recollection of devas
  in detail, while abbreviating the four recollections between them. This
  edition restores those familiar formulas from the parallel discourse AN
  11.11, then states their repeated result once in plain English.
- `citta` is rendered `heart`, meaning the affective or feeling mind;
  `samādhi` is `composure`; and `sati` and its compounds keep their active
  sense of remembering.
- `pāmojja`, `pīti`, `passaddhi`, and `sukha` remain distinct: an `actively
  happy state`, `rejoicing`, `relaxation`, and `satisfaction`.

## Translation

### Mahānāma Asks How to Practice

At one time the Buddha was staying among the Sakyans near Kapilavatthu, in the
Banyan Tree Monastery.

Mahānāma the Sakyan had recently recovered from an illness. At that time,
several bhikkhus were making a robe for the Buddha. People were saying:

"When the robe is finished and the three months are over, the Buddha will set
out traveling."

Mahānāma heard about this. He went to the Buddha, bowed, sat to one side, and
said:

"Bhante, I have heard that several bhikkhus are making a robe for the Buddha,
and that when it is finished and the three months are over, the Buddha will
set out traveling.

We live in many different ways. How should we live?"

"Good, Mahānāma, good. It is fitting for people like you to come to the
Buddha and ask this.

### Five Qualities to Stand On

People with confidence succeed; people without it do not.

People who have aroused their energy succeed; lazy people do not.

People who keep remembering succeed; forgetful people do not.

People who are composed succeed; uncomposed people do not.

People with discernment succeed; people without discernment do not.

Stand firmly in these five qualities, Mahānāma. Then develop six more.

### Six Things to Remember

Remember the Buddha:

> That Blessed One is the arahant, the fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in
> knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide for
> those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.

Remember the Dhamma:

> The Dhamma is well taught by the Buddha, visible here and now, timeless,
> inviting you to come and see, leading onward, and to be known personally by
> the wise.

Remember the Saṅgha:

> The Buddha's Saṅgha of disciples practices well, directly, properly, and
> rightly. It consists of the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of
> individuals. This Saṅgha is worthy of offerings, hospitality, gifts, and
> reverential greeting—the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

Remember your own conduct: conduct that is unbroken, intact, spotless, and
freeing; praised by the wise, not clung to, and leading to composure.

Remember your own generosity:

> Among people stained by stinginess, I live at home free from stinginess. I
> am openhanded, glad to let go, responsive when asked, and glad to give and
> share.

Remember the devas. Devas live in the heavens of the Four Great Kings, the
Thirty-Three, Yāma, the Contented, those Delighting in Creation, those who
Control What Others Create, Brahmā's company, and still higher realms. They
were reborn there with qualities such as confidence, conduct, learning,
generosity, and discernment. Remember that these qualities are present in you
too.

### What This Does to the Heart

While a noble disciple develops any of these recollections, their heart is not
taken over by passion, aversion, or confusion. At that time, the recollection
points their heart straight.

With the heart pointed straight, they find inspiration in the meaning and in
the Dhamma. An actively happy state connected with the Dhamma arises.

From that active gladness comes rejoicing. With a rejoicing heart, the body
relaxes. A relaxed body feels satisfaction. With satisfaction, the heart
becomes composed.

### Practice Anywhere

Develop these recollections while walking, standing, sitting, or lying down.
Develop them while working and while living at home with your children around
you."

The second discourse is finished.
