# Dhp 273-289: Maggavagga (The Path)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of the twentieth chapter of the
  Dhammapada, verses 273 to 289, designed to be readable as a single Markdown
  document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [Dhp 273-289 Translation Notes](dhp273-289-maggavagga-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of Dhp 273-289 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data. The verse titles from the later commentary are not translated.
- House choices that most affect this chapter: `magga` is `path`; verses 277
  to 279 keep the formula wording of AN 3.136, `all that has been put
  together is impermanent`, `... is unsatisfactory`, and `all phenomena are
  not-self`; `nibbidā` is `disenchantment`; `virāga` is `fading`; `jhāyin` is
  `those who practice the mental themes`, as in Dhp 21-32; `salla` is
  `arrow`; and `santi` is `peace`.
- Each verse carries its Dhammapada number.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's Dhp 273-289
  translation surface.

## Translation

### Verses

**273** Of paths, the eightfold is the best;
of truths, the four statements;
of dhammas, fading is the best;
and of those who walk on two feet, the one with vision.

**274** This is the path; there is no other
for the purifying of vision.
Follow this path:
it leaves Māra bewildered.

**275** Following it,
you will make an end of dissatisfaction.
I have taught you the path,
knowing how the arrow is drawn out.

**276** You yourselves must make the effort;
Tathāgatas only tell the way.
Those who follow it and practice the mental themes
will be freed from Māra's bonds.

**277** 'All that has been put together is impermanent':
when someone sees this with discernment,
they grow disenchanted with dissatisfaction.
This is the path to purity.

**278** 'All that has been put together is unsatisfactory':
when someone sees this with discernment,
they grow disenchanted with dissatisfaction.
This is the path to purity.

**279** 'All phenomena are not-self':
when someone sees this with discernment,
they grow disenchanted with dissatisfaction.
This is the path to purity.

**280** Not getting up when it is time to get up,
young and strong, but given to idleness,
their attitude and mind sunk low, the lazy
do not find the path by discernment.

**281** Guarding speech, well restrained in mind,
doing nothing unwholesome with the body:
keep these three paths of action clean,
and you will win the path the seers made known.

**282** From practice, wisdom grows;
without practice, wisdom wastes away.
Knowing this two-way road
to growth and to decline,
set yourself on the way
by which wisdom grows.

**283** Cut down the forest, not just a tree:
danger is born from the forest.
Once you have cut down the forest and the undergrowth,
be free of the forest, bhikkhus.

**284** As long as the undergrowth of a man's desire for women
is not cut down, even the smallest bit of it,
so long his mind stays bound,
like a suckling calf to its mother.

**285** Cut out your fondness for yourself,
as you would pull an autumn lily with your hand.
Develop only the path to peace,
the nibbāna the Sugata taught.

**286** 'Here I will spend the rains,
here the winter and the summer':
so the fool thinks,
and does not see what may cut it short.

**287** That man, intoxicated with children and cattle,
his mind attached,
death seizes and carries off
as a great flood a sleeping village.

**288** Children are no shelter,
nor a father, nor relatives.
For someone overtaken by the Ender
there is no shelter among kin.

**289** Seeing the force of this,
the wise, restrained by conduct,
should quickly clear the path
that leads to nibbāna.

The twentieth chapter, on the path, is finished.
