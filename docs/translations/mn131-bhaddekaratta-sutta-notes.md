# MN 131 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 131: Bhaddekaratta Sutta](mn131-bhaddekaratta-sutta.md). The main
translation is the primary study surface; this file records debated
translation choices, source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 131 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`mn131_root-pli-ms.json`).
- Control surfaces: the repository's current term policy, especially
  `bhaddekaratta`, `nandī`, `rūpa`, `vedanā`, `saññā`, `saṅkhārā`,
  `viññāṇa`, and `attā`; the existing self-view formula in MN 44; and the
  five-heaps surfaces SN 22.48, SN 22.59, and SN 22.89.
- Working method: every Bilara segment was represented in order. Repetition
  abbreviated by `pe` in the root was expanded only where the repeated member
  is structurally explicit.

## Governing Decisions

- `bhaddekaratta` is rendered `one fine night`. The compound can describe a
  single auspicious night or a person who has lived such a night. The English
  title uses the first form; the refrain uses `someone who has lived one fine
  night`. The public reader receives the descriptive title `Don't Chase the
  Past or Long for the Future`, so a newcomer does not need to understand the
  Pali title before reading.
- `atītaṁ anvāgameti` is rendered `chase after the past`. This keeps the active
  movement of `anvāgameti` and avoids the weaker `think about the past`.
- `anāgataṁ paṭikaṅkhati` is rendered `long for the future`. In the prose,
  `evaṁrūpo siyaṁ` and its parallels become `may be like this`. This preserves
  the imagined possibility without making the thought itself a declaration of
  hope; the repeated `nandī` clause supplies the longing in the positive half.
- `nandī` remains `relishing`. The prose is not objecting to memory or planning
  by themselves; the repeated pivot is finding something to relish in a past
  or imagined future version of the fivefold experiential field.
- The five members remain `form`, `felt experience`, `recognition`, `putting
  things together`, and `knowing`. The personal predication of `saṅkhāro` is
  verbalized as `how I put things together` in the past and future sections,
  following the plain-English rule to prefer a natural verb where the lexical
  distinction remains intact.
- `paccuppannesu dhammesu saṁhīrati` is rendered `being pulled away by what is
  happening now`. This follows the verse's `Don't be pulled away` and keeps the
  prose tied to the summary it explains.
- The twenty self-view positions reuse MN 44's governed sequence: regarding
  each member as self, self as having it, it as in self, or self as in it.
  `Attā` therefore remains the direct `self`; the passage is not softened into
  generic identity language.
- `paccuppannañca yo dhammaṁ tattha tattha vipassati` is rendered `See clearly
  what is happening now, right where it is`. The phrase is direct and audible
  without importing the later technical English noun `insight` into the verse.
- `asaṁhīraṁ asaṅkuppaṁ` is rendered `Don't be pulled away or shaken`. The
  compact Pali permits more than one syntactic construal; the English reads the
  pair as the stable way of seeing the present that the following prose then
  defines by contrast.
- `ajjeva kiccamātappaṁ` becomes `The work must be done today`, and
  `na hi no saṅgaraṁ ... maccunā` becomes `There is no bargaining with death
  and its great army`. These preserve the urgency and military image without
  archaic diction.

## Source-Audit Calls

- The opening and close are complete in the root and are translated rather
  than reduced to the compact setting used by some shorter repository
  surfaces.
- The past section states all five members. The future section abbreviates the
  middle members but plainly marks the same fivefold sequence; the translation
  expands them so the positive and negative halves can be heard and compared.
- The present section abbreviates the middle three self-view sets. The
  translation expands all twenty positions because the explanation depends on
  seeing the same fourfold error applied to each member.
- The verse occurs twice in full. Both occurrences remain in full. Its
  repetition frames the prose explanation and is not disposable duplication.
- MN 131 does not say that memory, preparation, or attention to the present is
  inherently a problem. The past and future sections name `nandī`; the present
  section names self-identification. The translation does not add a blanket
  command to ignore the past or future.
- The companion Bhaddekaratta discourses, MN 132 through MN 134, repeat the
  verse but present different narrative settings or analyses. They were
  checked for formula scope, but this edition does not silently translate or
  import their additional material.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse's practical point is narrower and sharper than the modern
  slogan `live in the present`. Someone can be preoccupied with the present by
  taking form, felt experience, recognition, putting things together, or
  knowing as self.
- The alternative is not passivity. The verse calls for clear seeing and work
  today, sustained without tiring through day and night.
- Death supplies urgency, not speculation. The text says that no bargain can
  be made with death; it does not predict when death will come.
- The past, future, and present sections form one argument. Relishing imagined
  versions of experience and identifying present experience as self are three
  temporal forms of the same appropriative pressure.

## Editorial Presentation

- The headings `Chasing After the Past`, `Longing for the Future`, and `Being
  Pulled Away by the Present` reuse the translation's own control lines. They
  are editorial navigation, not headings in the Pali root.
- The verse is set as four plain four-line units. It is not forced into rhyme
  or a ceremonial register; the repetition and images provide its cadence.
- The reader title describes the teaching rather than asking an unfamiliar
  Pali compound to orient a newcomer. The canonical document keeps the Pali
  title visible, and these notes preserve the title decision.
