# MN 119 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[MN 119: Kāyagatāsati Sutta](mn119-kayagatasati-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `mn119:1.1`–`mn119:43.4`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/mn119/pli/ms>.
- Control surfaces: MN 10 for body practice, MN 118 for the shared breathing
  lines, and DN 2 for the four mental themes and higher knowledges.

## Governing Decisions

- `kāyagatā sati` remains in Pali, following the stable major record. The first
  occurrence supplies the record's gloss: `sati directed to the body`.
- The body exercises are not described as a generic meditation technique.
  Each is followed by the text's result: household-linked thoughts are given
  up and the heart settles, unifies, and becomes composed.
- The body-parts and elements passages reuse MN 10 wording where the Pali is
  the same. MN 119's different refrain remains different.
- The charnel-ground stages are collected into one complete, scannable list.
  The application—this body has the same nature and cannot escape it—is stated
  once across the sequence rather than falsely presented as new content.
- `jhāna` remains `mental theme`; `pīti` is `rejoicing`, `sukha` is
  `satisfaction` or `ease` as grammar requires, and `upekkhā` is `dynamic
  balance`.
- The six similes contrasting vulnerability and protection are kept. Their
  point is practical availability: whether Māra finds an opening and foothold.
- The ten benefits are fully numbered. The Pali's peyyāla abbreviations in the
  higher-knowledge formulas are expanded from the same governed formulas in
  DN 2, without importing DN 2's additional similes.
- Extraordinary claims are translated as claims of the text. The reader
  introduction does not require a newcomer to settle how they should be
  interpreted before following the sutta's practical structure.
