## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

### The Four Nutriments

"Bhikkhus, there are four nutriments that keep beings going once they have come
to be, and that support those looking to come to be.

What four?

Edible-food nutriment, whether coarse or fine; contact nutriment is the second;
mental-intention nutriment is the third; knowing nutriment is the fourth.

These, bhikkhus, are the four nutriments that keep beings going once they have
come to be, and that support those looking to come to be.

### Where They Come From

And these four nutriments, bhikkhus — what is their source? What is their
origin? What are they born from? What produces them?

Ignorant wanting is their source, ignorant wanting is their origin; they are
born from ignorant wanting, and ignorant wanting produces them.

And this ignorant wanting, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin?
What is it born from? What produces it?

Felt experience is its source, felt experience is its origin; it is born from
felt experience, and felt experience produces it.

And this felt experience, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin?
What is it born from? What produces it?

Contact is its source, contact is its origin; it is born from contact, and
contact produces it.

And this contact, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What is it
born from? What produces it?

The six fields of experience are its source, the six fields of experience are
its origin; it is born from the six fields of experience, and the six fields of
experience produce it.

And these six fields of experience, bhikkhus — what is their source? What is
their origin? What are they born from? What produces them?

Name-and-form is their source, name-and-form is their origin; they are born
from name-and-form, and name-and-form produces them.

And this name-and-form, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What
is it born from? What produces it?

Knowing is its source, knowing is its origin; it is born from knowing, and
knowing produces it.

And this knowing, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its origin? What is it
born from? What produces it?

Putting things together is its source, putting things together is its origin;
it is born from putting things together, and putting things together produces
it.

And this putting things together, bhikkhus — what is its source? What is its
origin? What is it born from? What produces it?

Ignorance is its source, ignorance is its origin; it is born from ignorance,
and ignorance produces it.

### The Chain

"And so, bhikkhus, with ignorance as condition, putting things together;
with putting things together as condition, knowing;
with knowing as condition, name-and-form;
with name-and-form as condition, the six fields of experience;
with the six fields of experience as condition, contact;
with contact as condition, felt experience;
with felt experience as condition, ignorant wanting;
with ignorant wanting as condition, taking personally;
with taking personally as condition, becoming;
with becoming as condition, birth;
with birth as condition, ageing and dying, sorrow, lamentation,
dissatisfaction, distress, and despair come to be.

That is how this whole heap of dissatisfaction comes to arise.

But with the complete fading and quenching of ignorance, the quenching of
putting things together;
with the quenching of putting things together, the quenching of knowing;
with the quenching of knowing, the quenching of name-and-form;
with the quenching of name-and-form, the quenching of the six fields of
experience;
with the quenching of the six fields of experience, the quenching of contact;
with the quenching of contact, the quenching of felt experience;
with the quenching of felt experience, the quenching of ignorant wanting;
with the quenching of ignorant wanting, the quenching of taking personally;
with the quenching of taking personally, the quenching of becoming;
with the quenching of becoming, the quenching of birth;
with the quenching of birth, the quenching of ageing and dying, sorrow,
lamentation, dissatisfaction, distress, and despair.

That is how this whole heap of dissatisfaction quenches."

The first discourse is finished.
