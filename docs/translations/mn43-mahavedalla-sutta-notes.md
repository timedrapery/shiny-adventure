# MN 43 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 43: Mahāvedalla Sutta](mn43-mahavedalla-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Governing Decisions

- The catechism frame `'X X'ti vuccati. Kittāvatā nu kho ... vuccatī`ti?` is
  rendered `people say, 'X, X.' In what way is ... said to be X?` throughout.
  The doubling is kept because it is how the question is posed, not an
  accident of transmission.
- `duppañña` and `paññavā` are rendered `poor in discernment` and
  `discerning` rather than as noun phrases. The sutta defines them by what a
  person does or fails to do, and the verbal forms `nappajānāti` / `pajānāti`
  are the definition, so the English keeps the same verb visible.
- `paññā` is `discernment` and `viññāṇa` is `knowing`, per the headwords. This
  matters more here than on most surfaces: the discourse turns on the two
  being mixed together but distinguishable in function, so the two renderings
  must stay separable in English without being made to sound unrelated.
- `paññā bhāvetabbā, viññāṇaṁ pariññeyyaṁ` is rendered `discernment is to be
  developed, and knowing is to be fully understood`, keeping the governed
  `pariññā` default rather than softening it to `understood`.
- `vedanā` keeps `felt experience` and the verb `vedeti` is rendered `it
  feels`. The three-term series is `satisfying`, `dissatisfying`, and
  `neither dissatisfying nor satisfying`, matching the governed `sukha` and
  `dukkha` defaults rather than importing `pleasant` and `painful`.
- Where the subject of the definition is the faculty rather than a person, the
  English uses `it` rather than `one`. `viññāṇa`, `vedanā`, and `saññā` are
  defined here by what they do, and a personal subject would import an agent
  the passage does not have. The register standard's warning about the
  generic `one` is honoured by naming the thing, not by promoting it to a
  person.
- `manoviññāṇa` is `mind-knowing` and `mano` is `thinking mind`, per the
  headwords. The distinction carries weight at 21.6, where `mano` is what the
  five faculties resort to.
- `āyu` and `usmā` are rendered `vitality` and `warmth`, and `āyusaṅkhārā` is
  rendered `vitality conditioners`, keeping the governed `conditioner` family
  audible rather than introducing `life-formations`. All three were ungoverned
  when this surface was drafted and were given records in the same pass; see
  Re-audit Calls.
- `saññāvedayitanirodha` keeps the governed `cessation of recognition and felt
  experience`, matching MN 44.
- The threefold set at 25.2 and 25.3 is rendered `body conditioner`, `verbal
  conditioner`, and `mind conditioner`. The middle member previously rendered
  as `verbal process`; the record was harmonised with its two siblings in the
  same pass as this surface, so MN 43 and MN 44 now read alike.
- `cetovimutti` is `release of mind` and `paññāvimutti` is `release through
  discernment`, per the headwords. The four qualified releases are rendered
  `measureless release of mind`, `release of mind through nothingness`,
  `release of mind through emptiness`, and `signless release of mind`.
- `pariyāya` keeps the governed `mode of exposition`. It is the hinge of the
  final section, which turns entirely on there being two ways of putting the
  same set of terms.
- `pamāṇakaraṇa`, `kiñcana`, and `nimittakaraṇa` are rendered `maker of
  measurement`, `a something`, and `maker of signs`. Each is built to answer
  the corresponding release by name, so the English keeps the echo:
  measureless answers the maker of measurement, nothingness answers the
  something, signless answers the maker of signs.
- `akuppā cetovimutti` is rendered `unshakable release of mind`.
- `khīṇāsava` is rendered `a bhikkhu whose outflows are exhausted`, keeping
  the governed `āsava` default rather than compressing to a title.
- The peyyala at 31.3 and 31.4 is expanded only as far as the source marks it.
  The brahmavihāra sweep is written in full for `mettā` and `upekkhā` and
  elided for `karuṇā` and `muditā`, as the Pali does.
- `suññamidaṁ attena vā attaniyena vā` is rendered `this is empty of a self or
  of what belongs to a self`, keeping `attaniya` as a relation rather than
  flattening it to `mine`.

## Re-audit Calls

- **`āyu`, `usmā`, and `āyusaṅkhāra` were ungoverned. Resolved 2026-08-20.**
  The vitality-and-warmth exchange at 22.7 through 22.19, with the oil-lamp
  simile, is the repository's clearest control passage for all three, and none
  had a record. They are also load-bearing at 24.2 and 25.2, where the three
  dhammas that leave the body are vitality, warmth, and knowing. Minor entries
  were written in the same pass as this surface: `āyu` is `vitality` with
  `lifespan` as the alternate for duration contexts, `usmā` is `warmth`, and
  `āyusaṅkhāra` is `vitality conditioner`, kept inside the governed conditioner
  family so the cessation argument stays legible. `āyu` and `usmā` are governed
  as a pair, because the formula is reciprocal and solving either alone invites
  the other to drift.
- **`pamāṇakaraṇa`, `kiñcana`, and `nimittakaraṇa` were ungoverned as a set.
  Resolved 2026-08-20.** They only make sense together: each names what
  passion, aversion, and delusion do, and each answers one of the qualified
  releases of mind by name. Passion makes measurement, so the measureless
  release answers it; passion is a something, so nothingness answers it;
  passion makes signs, so the signless release answers it. Three minor entries
  were written together, each recording that the set is governed as a unit so a
  later surface cannot solve one member at a time and lose the echo.
- `paññācakkhu` is ungoverned. `eye of discernment` follows the `paññā`
  headword, but the compound is used elsewhere in the canon and would benefit
  from a record before a second surface renders it.
- `akuppa` is ungoverned. It recurs across release contexts and is a candidate
  for a minor entry tied to `vimutti`.
- `parato ghosa` is rendered `the word of another` and is ungoverned. It is
  half of the standard two-condition formula for right view, so it belongs
  with `yoniso-manasikāra` rather than being solved locally.
- The five supports for right view -- `sīla`, `suta`, `sākacchā`, `samatha`,
  `vipassanā` -- are a governed-adjacent set of which `suta` and `sākacchā`
  have no records. `suta` is already flagged elsewhere in the rollout plan
  through `assutava` and `sutava`.
- **The ten emptiness-family supporting compounds remain uncited.**
  `sunnata-samadhi`, `animitta-samadhi`, `appanihita-samadhi`, the three
  `-cetosamadhi` forms, the three `-vimokkha` forms, and `vimokkhamukha` have
  no `sutta_references` at all. This surface anchors `animitta` and
  `suññatā` but does not reach them; they need citations written, not a
  further translation. See the Wave 7 entry in
  [next-suttas-roadmap.md](../next-suttas-roadmap.md).
- `appanihita` is not used in MN 43. The wishless member of the interface is
  governed but this surface does not exercise it, so the emptiness cluster is
  anchored by two of its three headwords rather than all three.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse is a question-and-answer session between two senior disciples,
  not a teaching addressed to a general audience. `āvuso` is rendered `friend`
  in both directions, since the two are peers.
- The point of the discernment-and-knowing exchange is not that the two are
  identical. They are inseparable in occurrence and distinguishable in what is
  to be done with them: one is developed, the other is fully understood.
- The vitality-and-warmth answer is deliberately circular, and Mahākoṭṭhika
  says so. The oil-lamp simile does not break the circle; it offers a case
  where mutual dependence is ordinary rather than paradoxical.
- The difference between a corpse and a bhikkhu in the attainment of cessation
  is not that less has stopped. The same three conditioners have ceased in
  both. What differs is that vitality, warmth, and the faculties are intact in
  the second case.
- The final section is the sutta's most compressed argument. Four releases
  that are genuinely different as attainments turn out to name one thing when
  taken as descriptions of a mind free of passion, aversion, and delusion.
  The English keeps both readings available rather than resolving the tension
  toward either.

## Editorial Presentation

- Section headings are editorial and do not correspond to divisions in the
  Pali.
- The five faculties are set as lists where the Pali repeats them as a run,
  because the repetition is structural rather than rhetorical and reads as
  clutter in English prose.
- Prose is hard-wrapped at 79 columns, per the register standard.

## Edition Status

- This is the first stable study edition of the MN 43 surface.
- MN 43 is the Wave 7 lead candidate. It was chosen because the emptiness /
  signless / wishless interface cluster was the only governed cluster with no
  running text behind any of its thirteen terms.
