# MN 36 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[MN 36: Mahāsaccaka Sutta](mn36-mahasaccaka-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `mn36:1.1`–`mn36:48.18`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/mn36/pli/ms>.
- Source boundary: the whole discourse is translated. `mn36:48.18`
  (`Mahāsaccakasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ chaṭṭhaṁ`) is rendered `The sixth discourse is
  finished.`, matching how MN 26 closes.
- The discourse has about 3,940 Pali words, which makes it one of the longer
  surfaces in the repository. Most of its length is structural repetition:
  the two teachers, the three fire-stick similes, the five breath-stopping
  episodes, and the refrain after every stage of the awakening account.
- Control surfaces: MN 26 for the two teachers, the going forth, and the
  arrival at Uruvelā; MN 19 for the four mental themes and the three
  knowledges in the first person; AN 8.6 for `cittaṁ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati`;
  SN 36.6 and the MN 148 phrase records for the untrained and trained
  responses to painful feeling; DN 2 for the going-forth reflection and the
  six rival teachers.

## Governing Decisions

- **The refrain is the discourse, and it is kept every time.** After each
  breath-stopping episode the source says painful feeling `cittaṁ na
  pariyādāya tiṭṭhati`; after each mental theme and each knowledge it says
  the same of pleasant feeling. That is the Buddha's answer to Saccaka's
  question, told as a story rather than stated as a claim. It appears here as
  `did not take over my heart`, following AN 8.6, at every point where the
  source prints it. None is collapsed, and none is added.
- **`citta` splits between `heart` and `mind` by what the sentence is doing.**
  Where citta is what feeling takes over, it is `heart`, the headword default
  and the AN 8.6 control. Where citta is the thing being developed
  (`cittabhāvanā`, `bhāvitacitta`, `abhāvitattā cittassa`), it follows the
  governed `development of mind`, so that `body` and `mind` stay a pair
  through the whole development argument. The result reads naturally:
  painful feeling takes over the heart because the mind is undeveloped. The
  split is deliberate and applies only to this surface's two families; it is
  not a proposal to change either record. `cetasā cittaṁ abhiniggaṇheyyaṁ` is
  `subdue ... my mind with my mind`, which uses the `mind` alternate on the
  `citta` record because `heart with heart` would obscure a plain physical
  description of forcing thought down.
- **`vedanā` is `feeling` inside `pleasant feeling` and `painful feeling`.**
  These two phrases have their own minor records and are the corpus norm in
  SN 36.6 and AN 8.6. The headword default `felt experience` is not used
  because this discourse never names vedanā in the abstract; it always names
  a pleasant or a painful one.
- **`sukhasārāgī` is `infatuated with pleasure`.** In a sentence about
  pleasant feeling, `pleasure` is the plain English and is an alternate on the
  `sukha` record. Inside the mental themes, and in the Buddha's question
  `Why am I afraid of that satisfaction?`, `sukha` keeps the governed
  `satisfaction`. The two uses are different in the source too: one is
  sensual pleasure a person gets hooked on, the other is the satisfaction the
  Buddha realizes has nothing to do with sensuality. Using `satisfaction`
  throughout the mental themes also keeps the fear question audibly tied to
  the first mental theme it asks about.
- **The four mental themes follow the MN 19 first-person wording with
  `satisfaction` for `sukha`,** as SN 45.8 does. The corpus is not uniform
  here; see the source-audit findings below. This surface does not settle
  that drift, and it does not add a fifth version of the formula either.
- **`sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati` is `realized for myself
  with direct knowledge, and entered and remain in it`.** The `abhiññā`
  record has a context rule for exactly this formula, which gives `direct
  knowledge`. `upasampajja viharati` uses the corpus's stock `enter and
  remain in`, the same words the mental-theme formula uses, so the reader
  hears the teachers' attainment and the mental themes described in the same
  terms. MN 26 renders the teacher passages with `higher knowing` and `live
  having entered into it`; this surface follows the record's context rule
  instead.
- **The lip-service line is translated.** `mn36:13.8` and `mn36:15.7` say that
  the young Gotama at first only repeated what he had learned and claimed `I
  know, I see` on that basis. It is rendered `As far as lip-service and
  repeating what I had been told, I spoke the language of knowledge and the
  language of the elders`. See the MN 26 finding below.
- **`nibbijja` is `became disenchanted`,** following `nibbidā`. The verb
  appears twice: when the Bodhisatta leaves each teacher, and when the five
  bhikkhus leave him. Keeping one English verb lets the reader hear that the
  same word is used both times.
- **`padhāna` is `exertion`,** following its record, including
  `dukkhappadhāna`, `painful exertion`, and `padhānavibbhanto`, `given up
  exertion`. MN 26 renders the parallel Uruvelā line with `striving`; this
  surface follows the record.
- **`appāṇakaṁ jhānaṁ jhāyeyyaṁ` is `hold a mental theme without breathing
  at all`.** `jhāna` keeps `mental theme`, and `jhāyati` is realized as `hold`
  rather than the record's instructional `think like this`, which fits a
  teacher's direction to students and not a description of a self-inflicted
  austerity. The record discourages `meditate`, and `breathless meditation`
  would also make this sound like a recommended practice. The source's point
  is the opposite: it is one of the practices that did not lead to awakening.
- **`opakkamikā ... vedanā` is `feelings that are self-inflicted`.** The
  adjective names pain brought on by one's own assault on oneself. `Effort`
  and `exertion` both already belong to governed terms.
- **`satānusāri viññāṇaṁ ahosi` is `following that memory, came the
  knowing`.** `viññāṇa` keeps the governed `knowing`. The compound's first
  member is from `sarati`, to remember, so the English says `memory` rather
  than importing a technical term.
- **`abbhaññāsiṁ` is `I directly knew`,** following `abhijānāti`. In the four
  truths, the outflows, and the closing declaration, MN 36 uses this verb in
  the first person. It is kept distinct from `pajānāti`, `discern`, which is
  the verb of the third-person version of the same declaration in DN 2.
  `Abhijānāmi` in the idiomatic sense of `I remember` (`mn36:31.2`,
  `mn36:45.1`, `mn36:46.1`, `mn36:48.4`) is the ordinary Pali idiom for
  recalling something and is translated as such.
- **`vijjā` is `clear knowledge`** in `This was the first clear knowledge I
  reached`, and `avijjā` is `ignorance` in the stock line that follows.
- **`sammoha`, `sammūḷha`, and `sammohavihāra` are handled as one family.**
  In the painful-feeling formula, `sammohaṁ āpajjati` keeps the MN 148 phrase
  record's `lose their bearings`. In the exchange about daytime sleep, where
  the word is itself the subject of discussion, it is `confused` and `living
  in confusion`, using the `confusion` alternate on the `moha` record, because
  the Buddha redefines the word and the English needs one adjective he can
  redefine.
- **`āsavā saṅkilesikā ponobbhavikā sadarā dukkhavipākā āyatiṁ
  jātijarāmaraṇiyā` is kept as five clauses.** `ponobbhavikā` keeps the
  governed `lead to renewed becoming` from the taṇhā formula. `dukkhavipākā`
  is `result in dissatisfaction`.
- **Saccaka's epithet `nigaṇṭhaputta` is `a son of the Jains`** on first
  mention and in Ānanda's warning, then shortened to `Saccaka` in narration.
  The rival teacher `Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta` keeps the spelling DN 2 uses.
- **The vocative `Aggivessana` is kept at the start of a turn, at section
  openings, and wherever it marks who is being answered.** The source repeats
  it in almost every sentence of the Buddha's account. Keeping every instance
  would fail the read-aloud test without adding information, and MN 26
  thins `bhikkhave` the same way. No sentence of content was removed with it.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source abbreviates four times.

- **Ascetic practices (`mn36:5.6`–`5.8`).** The source prints the first and
  second step and the seventh, and abbreviates the ones between. The English
  says `and so on up to seven`, which states the source's range without
  printing numbers it does not print.
- **Past lives (`mn36:38.2`).** The source prints `ekampi jātiṁ` and then
  jumps to the closing `iti sākāraṁ sauddesaṁ`. The English keeps both ends:
  `beginning with one birth` and `with their aspects and particulars`. The
  full list of past-life details from DN 2 is not imported.
- **The divine eye (`mn36:40.2`).** The source abbreviates after `yathākammūpage
  satte pajānāmi`. The English ends the description there, at `according to
  their actions`, as MN 19 does. The longer account of good and bad conduct
  is not imported.
- **Rival teachers (`mn36:48.7`–`48.11`).** The source gives Pūraṇa Kassapa
  in full, abbreviates Makkhali Gosāla, Ajita Kesakambala, Pakudha Kaccāyana,
  and Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta, and gives Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta in full again. The
  English keeps both full statements where the source puts them and names
  each abbreviated teacher once between them, the method used in SN 12.20.

## Source-Audit Findings

Checking the records that cite MN 36 turned up one false citation pattern,
now repaired, and two findings about other surfaces, recorded here and not
fixed.

- **MN 36 does not contain `nāparaṃ itthattāyāti pajānāti`.** Three records
  quoted that form and cited MN 36: the phrase record
  `naparam-itthattayati-pajanati`, the major `pajanati`, and an example on the
  major `asava`. MN 36 closes the declaration with `abbhaññāsiṁ`, the aorist of
  `abhijānāti`, because the Buddha is speaking in the first person about his
  own awakening. The strict verifier scored all three `inflected`, since
  `itthattāyā` is present, and `inflected` is documented as usually not an
  error. This is the same shape as the `dhammatā`, `ariyapuggala`, and
  `upasamānussati` findings. Repairs:
  - `naparam-itthattayati-pajanati` and `pajanati` now cite SN 22.86
    (`sn22.86:7.16`), which has the exact form and whose governed surface
    already renders the line `They discern: ...`. The phrase record also
    lists DN 2, where the declaration occurs with the quotation particle
    written separately (`itthattāyā’ti`).
  - `asava` keeps MN 36 as a source, but its example now quotes what MN 36
    actually says about the outflows: the heart released from the sensual
    outflow, the outflow of becoming, and the outflow of ignorance.
  - `abhijanati` gains MN 36 as a real running-text example of the verb the
    discourse does use.
  - The two examples touched on `pajanati` and `naparam-itthattayati-pajanati`
    also drop the generic `one discerns`, which contradicted their own
    preferred `they discern`.
- **MN 26 leaves out the lip-service line.** The MN 26 Pali has the same line
  as MN 36 at `mn26:15.7` and `mn26:16.7`, and the MN 26 surface does not
  translate it: it goes straight from `I quickly mastered that teaching` to
  the reflection about the teacher. That is an omission in a governed surface,
  not a matter of wording. It is not fixed here because it changes MN 26's
  body and needs its own readability pass and hash update.
- **The mental-theme formula has drifted across the corpus.** DN 2, MN 19,
  MN 141, and MN 119 use `ease` for `sukha`; SN 45.8 uses `satisfaction`;
  MN 26 and MN 38 use `equanimity` and `gladness and grief`; MN 43 and MN 44
  have further variants, and SN 45.8 has `joy and sadness` where the others
  have `gladness and sadness`. No formula record governs the sequence, so the
  consistency checker cannot see it. It wants a formula record, decided once
  and applied to every surface together, the same way the fourfold source
  question was settled.

## Practice Clarifications

- Saccaka's picture is that some people train the body and others train the
  mind. The Buddha does not answer by picking a side. He redefines both
  terms around one test: whether pleasant or painful feeling, once it has
  arisen, takes over the heart.
- The austerities are not told as a failure of effort. The refrain after each
  one says the opposite: energy did not flag, remembering did not slip, and
  even that pain did not take over the heart. What failed was the method. It
  exhausted the body without leading to knowledge.
- The turn comes from a memory, not from a new technique. The Buddha recalls
  a satisfaction he once had that had nothing to do with sensuality, asks
  why he is afraid of it, and decides he is not. The text presents that
  decision, and eating again, as the step that made the mental themes and
  the three knowledges possible.
- The closing exchange about sleep is a practical definition. Being confused
  is not a matter of whether someone naps. It is a matter of whether the
  outflows have been abandoned.

## Role in the Corpus

MN 36 was requested directly rather than drawn from a wave audit. It is the
corpus's fullest first-person account of the Buddha's own practice before
awakening: the two teachers it shares with MN 26, the austerities no other
surface describes, the childhood memory of the first mental theme, and the
three knowledges it shares with MN 19. It is also the control surface for
the distinction between being developed in body and developed in mind, and
for the refrain that neither painful nor pleasant feeling took over the
Buddha's heart.
