# MN 61 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 61: Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovāda Sutta](mn61-ambalatthikarahulovada-sutta.md).
The main translation is the primary study surface; this file records debated
translation choices, source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 61 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially `kamma`,
  `musāvāda`, `dukkha`, `kusala`, `akusala`, `sacca`, and `Rāhula`.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as the repository's control
  surface for the threefold before/during/after reflection formula applied
  to bodily, verbal, and mental action, and for the water-vessel and
  war-elephant similes on the gravity of deliberate lying.

## Governing Decisions

- `kamma` is rendered `action` (major lexicon entry). `Kāyakamma`,
  `vacīkamma`, and `manokamma` are rendered `bodily action`, `verbal
  action`, and `mental action`, inheriting the headword default rather than
  introducing a separate compound rendering.
- `musāvāda` is rendered `false speech` (minor lexicon entry). The specific
  phrase `sampajānamusā bhāsati` (knowingly speaking false speech) is
  rendered "speaking a deliberate lie" in the water-vessel and war-elephant
  similes, since the passage is about the act of lying rather than the
  abstract category of false speech; "false speech" is reserved for where
  the sutta names the category directly.
- `dukkha` is rendered `dissatisfaction` in the reflection formula's
  `dukkhudrayaṁ dukkhavipākaṁ` ("with dissatisfaction as its outcome,
  dissatisfaction as its result"), matching the house four-truths default
  rather than a looser "painful consequences" gloss. The positive mirror
  `sukhudrayaṁ sukhavipākaṁ` is rendered "with ease as its outcome, ease as
  its result," keeping `sukha` distinct from `dukkha` rather than simply
  negating the same noun.
- `kusala` / `akusala` are rendered `wholesome` / `unwholesome` (major
  lexicon entries), consistent with the house default established across
  AN 3.65 and other surfaces.
- `Rāhula` remains untranslated as a proper name (minor lexicon entry). The
  sutta is a direct father-to-son teaching; the translation keeps the
  address plain and warm rather than importing devotional register.
- The setting formula "near Rājagaha, in the Bamboo Grove, at the
  Squirrels' Feeding Ground" (Rājagahe viharati Veḷuvane Kalandakanivāpe) is
  the standard recurring Rājagaha setting used across many suttas in the
  corpus; it is rendered identically here for consistency, without a
  separate lexicon entry since it functions as a fixed narrative formula
  rather than a doctrinal term.

## Re-audit Calls

- `byābādha` (affliction) has no lexicon entry. This sutta is the natural
  governing surface for one: the reflection formula's key question —
  whether an action leads to one's own affliction, the affliction of
  others, or the affliction of both — recurs nine times across the three
  action-types and three time-phases (before/during/after) in this single
  discourse, and is very likely to recur in other ethical-reflection
  surfaces. A minor entry would fix "affliction" as the governed rendering
  before it drifts into "harm," "trouble," or "distress" in a future
  surface.
- `paccavekkhati` (reflect, review) has no lexicon entry. The entire
  discourse is organized around this verb; a minor entry would be
  appropriate, distinguishing it from `manasikāra` (attention) and from
  `sati` (remembering) as a distinct, deliberate act of reviewing a past,
  present, or intended action against a stated standard.
- The threefold before/during/after reflection formula itself (applied
  identically to bodily, verbal, and mental action, with only the
  after-the-fact response differing between bodily/verbal action —
  "confess it, reveal it, lay it open" — and mental action — "feel
  disturbed, ashamed, and disgusted by it") has no formula record. This is
  a strong candidate: it is long, exactly repeated across three action-types
  in this sutta alone, and is the kind of stock passage the repository's
  formula-record mechanism exists to stabilize. A future pass should
  consider whether to govern the bodily/verbal branch and the mental-action
  branch as one record with a documented variance, or as two related
  records.
- The water-vessel simile's four-step structure (a little water left → the
  water thrown away → the vessel turned upside down → the vessel empty and
  void) has no formula record. It recurs only once in this sutta, so a
  record is not urgent, but should be considered if a parallel simile
  surfaces elsewhere in a future translation pass.

## Editorial Presentation

- The main translation is divided into eight sections: `The Setting`, `The
  Water Vessel`, `The War Elephant`, `The Mirror`, `Reflecting On Bodily
  Action`, `Reflecting On Verbal Action`, `Reflecting On Mental Action`, and
  `The Purification Of Every Contemplative And Brahmin`.
- The three reflection sections (bodily, verbal, mental action) are each
  given in full rather than abbreviated after the first, consistent with
  the repository's policy of preserving repetition for study readability.
  This is a long discourse as a result, but the repetition is structurally
  load-bearing: the sutta's point is that the same reflection applies
  identically across all three doors of action, and collapsing the
  repetition would obscure that.
- The mental-action reflection section deliberately preserves the source's
  divergence from the bodily/verbal pattern in its after-the-fact response:
  "feel disturbed, ashamed, and disgusted by it, and guard against it in
  the future," rather than "confess it, reveal it, lay it open" — a private
  mental action cannot be confessed to a teacher or companion the way a
  bodily or verbal action can, and the translation keeps this distinction
  visible rather than flattening all three action-types into one identical
  closing line.

## Edition Status

- This companion supports the first stable study edition of the
  repository's MN 61 translation surface.
- Future revisions should focus on: adding minor entries for `byābādha`
  (affliction) and `paccavekkhati` (reflect); and adding a formula record
  for the threefold before/during/after reflection formula, the discourse's
  most reusable structural element.
