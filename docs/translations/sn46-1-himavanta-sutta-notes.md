# SN 46.1 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[SN 46.1: Himavanta Sutta](sn46-1-himavanta-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `sn46.1:1.1`–`sn46.1:1.13`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/sn46.1/pli/ms>.
- Source boundary: the discourse ends at `sn46.1:1.13`. The segment after it,
  `1.14` (`Paṭhamaṁ`), is collection apparatus marking the first discourse of
  the Pabbatavagga. The closing line `The first discourse is finished.`
  renders that marker only, as on every other surface in this repository.
- The discourse has 125 Pali words once the front matter is excluded. It is
  the shortest surface in the corpus after AN 2.9.
- Control surfaces: SN 46.51 and MN 118 for the seven awakening factors and
  their four-part tail, and AN 3.88 for the place of conduct in training.

## Governing Decisions

- **`sīlaṁ nissāya sīle patiṭṭhāya` is `relies on conduct and is established
  in conduct`.** `sīla` keeps its governed default `conduct` rather than the
  compact-list alternate `virtue`, because this sentence is practical rather
  than a doctrinal list. The English keeps `relying on` for `nissāya` in both
  halves of the simile — the nāgas rely on the mountain, the bhikkhu relies on
  conduct — because that repeated word is what makes the comparison work in
  Pali and it should do the same work in English.
- **`mahantattaṁ vepullattaṁ` is `greatness and fullness` on both sides.**
  The two abstract nouns are the hinge of the whole discourse: the same pair
  is predicated of the nāgas' bodies and of the bhikkhu's qualities. Any
  rendering that varies between the halves would break the comparison the
  discourse is built on. The Pali does change verbs across the halves
  (`āpajjanti`, `pāpuṇāti`), and the English follows with `come to` and
  `reaches`.
- **`dhammesu` is `in qualities`, unqualified.** The Pali does not say
  `kusala`, and adding `wholesome` or `good` would be supplying a word the
  source leaves out. The seven factors listed immediately afterwards are the
  qualities in view, so the sentence does not need the help.
- **`nāga` stays in Pali.** The word covers great serpents and elephants
  alike, and this discourse gives no way to tell which is meant: the
  commentarial tradition reads elephants, while the migration down through
  pools and rivers to the ocean reads more naturally as serpents. Any English
  animal resolves an ambiguity the source leaves open, which the plain-English
  standard forbids. The word is kept, glossed in the reader layer, and the
  `nāga` record now carries that decision instead of the auto-scaffolded stub
  it had before.
- **`himavantaṁ pabbatarājānaṁ` is `the Himalaya, the king of mountains`.**
  The apposition is the source's own and is not compressed into an epithet.
- **The four descending waters keep their four sizes.** `kusobbha`,
  `mahāsobbha`, `kunnadī`, and `mahānadī` become `little pools`, `big pools`,
  `small rivers`, and `great rivers`. The stepwise enlargement is the point of
  the sequence, so the English uses two plain size words rather than four
  unrelated nouns. `mahāsamuddasāgara` doubles two words for the ocean and is
  rendered `the sea, the great ocean`.
- **The seven factors follow SN 46.51.** Its attributive pattern — `the
  remembering awakening factor`, `the discernment-of-qualities awakening
  factor`, and so on — is used unchanged, so the two SN 46 surfaces read
  identically. `pīti` is `rejoicing` and `passaddhi` is `relaxation`, each
  following its headword's explicit awakening-factor context rule.
- **The four-part tail follows MN 118** word for word: `based on seclusion,
  based on fading, based on quenching, culminating in relinquishment`.
- **`bhāvento … bahulīkaronto` is `developing … and cultivating`**, matching
  the `bhāvitā bahulīkatā` pair already rendered `developed and cultivated` in
  MN 118.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source writes the four-part tail in full for the first factor
(`satisambojjhaṅgaṁ`) and again for the last (`upekkhāsambojjhaṅgaṁ`), and
abbreviates the five between them with `…pe…`.

This edition writes all seven out. That is a different choice from SN 12.20
and AN 8.39, where naming the elided items once was enough, and the reason is
that here the abbreviation stands for nothing but the identical tail: each
elided segment is one factor name plus the four words the source has already
printed twice. Expanding it adds no content and loses none, and the
list is the reader's only statement of what the seven factors are.

No factor is added, removed, or reordered.

## Source-Audit Findings

- **`bojjhaṅgabhāvanā` is not in SN 46.1.** Two records quoted that compound
  to this discourse: `bojjhaṅga-bhāvanā`, whose only citation it was, and the
  `bojjhaṅga` major entry. The discourse contains no form of `bhāvanā` at all.
  What it has is `satta bojjhaṅge bhāvento satta bojjhaṅge bahulīkaronto`, and
  both records now quote text that is actually there.
- **This one hid behind `inconclusive`, not `inflected`.** The five earlier
  findings of this shape — `ariyapuggala` at SN 55.30, `upasamānussati` at
  AN 11.12, `dhammatā` at SN 12.20, the MN 36 declaration line, and
  `veramaṇī` at AN 8.39 — were all scored `inflected`, so the standing advice
  was to open `inflected` verdicts by hand. SN 46.1 uses peyyāla, so the
  strict verifier returns `inconclusive` for anything not found in it and
  proves nothing either way. An `inconclusive` verdict on a short discourse is
  now worth the same manual check: the elided material here expands to a tail
  the source prints twice, so absence really was decidable by reading.
- The `bojjhaṅga` major entry already carried notes recording that SN 46.1 has
  only the accusative `satta bojjhaṅge` and not the nominative or participial
  forms two of its other examples quote. Those notes were right about the
  discourse and did not go on to check the third example against it.

## Re-audit Calls

- **The seven awakening factors are named two different ways across the
  corpus. Found here, not fixed.** SN 46.51 uses one attributive pattern for
  all seven (`the discernment-of-qualities awakening factor`, `the composure
  awakening factor`, `the dynamic-balance awakening factor`). MN 118 switches
  to a genitive for three of them (`the awakening factor of discernment of
  qualities`, `the awakening factor of composure`) and drops the hyphens. Both
  are defensible English and neither contradicts a term record, so nothing
  catches it. This surface follows SN 46.51, its neighbour in the same
  saṁyutta. A single pass across MN 118 would settle the corpus on one
  pattern.
- **Two compound records contradicted their own headwords, and are fixed
  here.** `pītisambojjhaṅga` read `delight awakening factor` while the `pīti`
  entry records an explicit rule that bojjhaṅga contexts take `rejoicing`, and
  `passaddhisambojjhaṅga` read `tranquility awakening factor` while
  `passaddhi` makes `relaxation` the default and scopes `tranquility` to prose
  needing a more state-like noun. Both existing translation surfaces already
  followed the headwords, so the records were the outliers. Nothing caught
  this either: `check_translation_drift.py` compares renderings declared
  across related entries, not a compound against the context rule of its own
  head.

## Practice Clarifications

- The argument is about a base, not about a sequence of merit. The nāgas do
  not earn the ocean by living in the mountains; the mountain is simply what
  they have to grow on before a body that size is possible. Conduct is put in
  the same position with respect to the awakening factors.
- `Greatness and fullness` is deliberately not a claim about attainments or
  stages. The discourse says only that the qualities get big, in the way a
  body gets big.
- The four-part tail is not decoration. It says what the seven factors are
  developed *toward*, and it is the same in every surface that lists them.

## Wave 10 Role

SN 46.1 is the fourth and final Wave 10 queue item, and it completes the wave.

It was ranked for one orphan signal, `bojjhaṅga-bhāvanā`. That signal turned
out to rest on a phrase the discourse does not contain, so the record is
anchored here by repair rather than by the citation that ranked it — the same
outcome as SN 12.20's `dhammatā`, and the reason the plan says to verify a
leverage signal against the source before committing to a queue position.
Corpus orphans go from 91 to 90.

Beyond the lexicon, the discourse gives the reader path a short, concrete
statement of why conduct comes before the meditative factors rather than
alongside them, in one image a newcomer can hold.
