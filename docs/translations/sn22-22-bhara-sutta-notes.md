# SN 22.22 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[SN 22.22: Bhāra Sutta](sn22-22-bhara-sutta.md). The main translation is the
primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of SN 22.22 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`sn22.22:0.1` to `sn22.22:7.5`), fetched from the upstream
  Bilara root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: SN 22.48 for the clung-to heap names, SN 56.11 for the
  origin and quenching formulas, and SN 1.1 for `parinibbuta`.
- Working method: the discourse is a short definition text built on four
  parallel questions, followed by two verses. The four questions are kept
  visibly parallel, and the two quoted formulas are copied from their control
  surfaces rather than retranslated.

## Governing Decisions

- `bhāra` is rendered `burden`; `bhārahāra` is `the carrier of the burden`;
  `bhārādāna` is `the taking up of the burden`; and `bhāranikkhepana` is `the
  putting down of the burden`. The four nouns share one root image, and the
  English keeps that image in all four so the reader can hear the parallel.
  These are document-local renderings; no headword record governs them yet.
- `pañcupādānakkhandhā` remains `five clung-to heaps`, and the five members
  follow SN 22.48's compact compound form (`form clung-to heap` and so on),
  because SN 22.22 uses the same compounds (`rūpupādānakkhandho` and so on).
- `tissa vacanīyaṁ` is rendered `It should be said:`. It introduces the answer
  in both the burden and the carrier sections, and the English keeps the same
  words in both places.
- `puggala` keeps its governed default `individual`. `Yvāyaṁ āyasmā evaṁnāmo
  evaṅgotto` is `this venerable one of such a name and such a clan`.
- `taṇhā ponobhavikā nandīrāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī` and the three
  kinds of wanting are copied from SN 56.11's origin formula. `Yo tassāyeva
  taṇhāya asesavirāganirodho cāgo paṭinissaggo mutti anālayo` is copied from
  SN 56.11's quenching formula. The point of the discourse is that taking up
  and putting down the burden are exactly the second and third truths, so the
  wording must match those truths exactly.
- `Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṁ vatvāna sugato athāparaṁ etadavoca satthā` is `That
  is what the Buddha said. Having said this, the Sugata, the Teacher, went on to
  say:`. `sugata` keeps its governed untranslated form.
- The verse's first line, `Bhārā have pañcakkhandhā`, follows its governed
  record `bhāro have pañcakkhandhā`: `The five heaps are indeed a burden`. The
  English singular reads the plural predicate as one load, which is how the
  prose has just described it. The verse names the plain `pañcakkhandhā`
  where the prose said `pañcupādānakkhandhā`; each keeps its own rendering.
- In the verses, `dukha` and `sukha` keep `dissatisfaction` and `satisfaction`,
  so the verse closes the same pairing the prose opened. `nicchāta` is `free of
  hunger`, a document-local rendering of the image of hunger ended.
  `parinibbuta` is `has reached complete quenching`, following SN 1.1.
- `Paṭhamaṁ` is rendered `The first discourse is finished.`, as in the other
  Saṁyutta surfaces.

## The Carrier Crux

`Puggalo tissa vacanīyaṁ` is a long-standing interpretive crux. Some later
schools read the carrier as a person existing apart from the five heaps; the
mainstream commentarial reading takes `puggala` as a conventional designation
for the named individual, who is not a self over and above what is carried.

The translation does not settle this. It renders the line plainly, keeps the
discourse's own gloss (`this venerable one of such a name and such a clan`),
and adds nothing that would argue for either reading. Readers who want the
question should meet it here, in the notes, and not in the translation.

## Practice Clarifications

- The burden is not life or the body. It is the five clung-to heaps: the
  fivefold field when it is taken personally.
- Picking up the burden is ignorant wanting, and putting it down is the
  complete fading and quenching of that wanting. The image therefore restates
  the second and third noble truths in terms a newcomer can hold.
- The verses add one thing the prose does not say: the burden is not swapped
  for another (`aññaṁ bhāraṁ anādiya`), and wanting is pulled out with its
  root, not merely set aside.

## Editorial Presentation

- The source abbreviates the setting (`Sāvatthiyaṁ … tatra kho …`). It is
  rendered `At Sāvatthī, the Buddha said:`, following the other abbreviated
  Saṁyutta openings in the corpus.
- The two verses are set as quoted stanzas under a `Verses` heading, as in
  SN 36.6. They are part of the discourse, not a later addition.
