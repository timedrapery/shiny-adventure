# Snp 1.8: Metta Sutta (Friendliness)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of Snp 1.8, the discourse widely
  known as the Karaṇīyamettā Sutta, designed to be readable as a single
  Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [Snp 1.8 Translation Notes](snp1-8-metta-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of Snp 1.8 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- House choices that most affect this discourse: `mettā` is `friendliness`,
  as in MN 7; `sukha` is `happy`, `khema` is `secure`, and `sati` is
  `remembering`; and `brahmavihāra` is `the divine way of living`.
- The ten verses are numbered within the discourse, since the Sutta Nipāta
  has no running verse numbers in this edition.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's Snp 1.8
  translation surface.

## Translation

### Verses

**1** This is what should be done by someone skilled in the good,
who has understood the state of peace:
let them be able, upright, truly upright,
easy to speak to, gentle, and not proud,

**2** content and easy to support,
with few duties and a light way of living,
calm in their senses and alert,
not brash, and not greedy among families.

**3** Let them not do even the slightest thing
that the wise would criticize them for.
May all be happy and secure;
may all beings be happy at heart.

**4** Whatever living beings there are,
whether frail or firm, without exception,
long or large,
middling, short, fine, or coarse,

**5** seen or unseen,
living far away or near,
those who have come to be and those still to come:
may all beings be happy at heart.

**6** Let no one deceive another
or look down on anyone anywhere.
Let no one, out of anger or resentment,
wish pain on another.

**7** As a mother would protect with her life
her child, her only child,
so toward all beings
develop a boundless heart.

**8** Develop a boundless heart
of friendliness toward the whole world,
above, below, and all around,
unobstructed, without hatred, without enmity.

**9** Standing or walking, sitting
or lying down, as long as you are awake,
resolve on this remembering.
This, they say, is the divine way of living here.

**10** Not taking up views,
virtuous and accomplished in vision,
having removed greed for sensuality,
they do not come again to lie in a womb.

The eighth discourse, on friendliness, is finished.
