# AN 4.5 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[AN 4.5: Anusota Sutta](an4-5-anusota-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `an4.5:1.1`–`an4.5:8.5`, stored in
  `.bilara-cache/an4.5_root-pli-ms.json`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/an4.5/pli/ms>.
- Comparison reference: Bhikkhu Sujato's published English translation in
  SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The source contains 196 Pali words.
- The title `Anusotasutta` means "The Discourse on Going With the Stream."

## Governing Decisions

- `anusotagāmī`, `paṭisotagāmī`, `ṭhitatto`, and `tiṇṇo pāraṅgato`
  organize the whole text as stages in a river crossing: going with the
  stream, going against it, standing firm, and having crossed over to the far
  shore. The translation keeps those images rather than replacing the last
  two with attainment labels.
- `kāme paṭisevati` is `indulges in sensual pleasures`. The object-side
  alternate is appropriate because the passage is about taking part in and
  enjoying sensual pleasures, not defining the whole domain of `kāma`.
- `pāpa kamma` is `harmful actions`. This makes the ethical consequence clear
  without importing sin-language.
- `brahmacariya` is `spiritual life`, referring to the complete life of
  practice rather than celibacy alone.
- The description of going upstream keeps the source's emotional force:
  `sahāpi dukkhena ... assumukhopi rudamāno` is `Even while suffering and
  distressed—weeping, with tears on their face.` The text does not claim that
  every stage of practice is comfortable.
- The third person is described rather than named as a `non-returner`:
  wearing away the five lower fetters, being spontaneously reborn, reaching
  final nibbāna there, and not returning from that world. The translation
  preserves the description and lets the newcomer note supply the map.
- `parinibbāyī` is `reach final nibbāna there`. Unlike the explicitly
  present-life verbal use in AN 3.69, this line describes final completion
  following another birth and therefore uses the governed final frame.
- `āsavānaṃ khayā` is `with the wearing away of the outflows`, keeping
  `khaya` distinct from quenching and `āsava` distinct from generic
  defilement.
- `cetovimutti` and `paññāvimutti` remain two audible members: `release of
  mind and release through discernment`. The pair is not collapsed into one
  vague reference to freedom.
- `sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā` is stated plainly as `realizes for
  themselves`. It is direct realization, not an appeal to authority or an
  occult power.
- `upaṭṭhitassatī` is `keeps remembering established`, following the
  project's `sati` policy rather than returning to "mindfulness."
- The verse's `kilesāni ... pañca` refers back to the five lower fetters in
  the prose. It becomes `five stains`, the governed plain and image-bearing
  alternate for `kilesa`. The source deliberately changes families here; this
  note prevents that broader word from erasing the more specific fetter
  category.
- `samāhitindriyo` is `with their faculties composed`. This preserves the
  `samādhi` family's sense of collected accord rather than suggesting forced
  concentration.
- `lokantagū` is `gone to the world's end`. In the completed-crossing verse,
  this is the end of the experienced world as a field of bondage, not a
  geographic journey to an edge of the earth.
