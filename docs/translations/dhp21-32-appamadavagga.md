# Dhp 21-32: Appamādavagga (Heedfulness)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of the second chapter of the
  Dhammapada, verses 21 to 32, designed to be readable as a single Markdown
  document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [Dhp 21-32 Translation Notes](dhp21-32-appamadavagga-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of Dhp 21-32 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data. The verse titles from the later commentary are not translated.
- House choices that most affect this chapter: `appamāda` is rendered
  `heedfulness` and `pamāda` `heedlessness`; `amatapada` is `the path to the
  deathless`; `jhāyin` is `those who practice the mental themes`, following
  `jhāna` as `mental theme`; `yogakkhema` is `security from the yoke`;
  `sukha` is `satisfaction`; `paññā` is `discernment`; and `saṁyojana` is
  `fetter`.
- Each verse carries its Dhammapada number.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's Dhp 21-32
  translation surface.

## Translation

### Verses

**21** Heedfulness is the path to the deathless;
heedlessness is the path to death.
The heedful do not die;
the heedless are as if already dead.

**22** Knowing this difference clearly,
the wise, established in heedfulness,
rejoice in heedfulness,
delighting in the range of the noble ones.

**23** Those who practice the mental themes, persevering,
always firm in their effort,
the steadfast touch nibbāna,
the unsurpassed security from the yoke.

**24** For someone who is energetic and remembers,
whose actions are clean, who acts with care,
who is restrained, living by the Dhamma,
and heedful, fame grows.

**25** By effort and heedfulness,
by restraint and self-control,
the intelligent should make an island
that no flood can overwhelm.

**26** Fools, people of poor understanding,
give themselves to heedlessness;
the intelligent guard heedfulness
as their greatest treasure.

**27** Do not give yourselves to heedlessness,
nor to intimacy with sensual delight.
Whoever is heedful and practices the mental themes
reaches abundant satisfaction.

**28** When the wise drive out heedlessness
by heedfulness,
they climb the tower of discernment,
and free of sorrow, they look out over a sorrowing people,
as one standing on a mountain looks out over those on the plain:
the steadfast look out over fools.

**29** Heedful among the heedless,
wide awake among those asleep,
the person of good understanding goes on ahead,
as a swift horse leaves a weak one behind.

**30** By heedfulness Maghavā
rose to be chief of the devas.
Heedfulness is praised;
heedlessness is always blamed.

**31** A bhikkhu who delights in heedfulness,
or who sees fear in heedlessness,
goes on like a fire,
burning up every fetter, fine or coarse.

**32** A bhikkhu who delights in heedfulness,
or who sees fear in heedlessness,
cannot fall away:
they are close to nibbāna.

The second chapter, on heedfulness, is finished.
