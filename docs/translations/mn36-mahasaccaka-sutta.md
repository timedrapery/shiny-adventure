# MN 36: Mahāsaccaka Sutta (The Longer Discourse with Saccaka)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [MN 36 Translation Notes](mn36-mahasaccaka-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data,
  segments `mn36:1.1`–`mn36:48.18`.
- Saccaka, a skilled debater, claims that some practitioners train the body
  and neglect the mind, and that the Buddha's disciples train the mind and
  neglect the body. The Buddha answers with a different account of what it
  means to be developed in body and in mind, and then with his own story:
  the two teachers, the self-torment that nearly killed him, and an old
  memory that showed him another way.
- The refrain that holds the discourse together is kept at every place the
  source gives it: painful feeling, however severe, did not take over his
  heart, and pleasant feeling, however refined, did not take over his heart
  either.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `vedanā` is `feeling` in the
  fixed phrases `pleasant feeling` and `painful feeling`; `bhāvanā` is
  `development`; `jhāna` is `mental theme`; `sukha` inside the mental themes
  is `satisfaction`; `upekkhā` is `dynamic balance`; `sati` is `remembering`;
  `samādhi` is `composure`; `āsava` is `outflow`; `padhāna` is `exertion`;
  `sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā` is `realized for myself with direct knowledge`.
- The source abbreviates the lists of ascetic practices, the recollection of
  past lives, the passing away and reappearance of beings, and Saccaka's list
  of rival teachers. Each abbreviation is marked where it falls; see the
  notes for how each is handled.

## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Vesālī, at the Great
Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof.

That morning the Buddha had dressed and, taking his bowl and robe, was about
to go into Vesālī for alms.

Then Saccaka, a son of the Jains, was going for a walk, wandering about to
stretch his legs, and he came toward the hall with the peaked roof in the
Great Wood. Venerable Ānanda saw him coming in the distance and said to the
Buddha:

"Bhante, here comes Saccaka, the son of the Jains. He is a debater and a
clever speaker, and many people think highly of him. He wants to discredit
the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha. It would be good if the Buddha sat
down for a while, out of compassion."

The Buddha sat down on the seat that had been prepared.

Saccaka went up to the Buddha and exchanged greetings with him. When the
polite and friendly talk was over, he sat down to one side and said to the
Buddha:

### Saccaka's Claim

"Master Gotama, there are some recluses and brahmins who live committed to
development of body, but not to development of mind. They are struck by
painful bodily feeling. It has happened before that someone struck by
painful bodily feeling had their thighs seize up, or their heart burst, or
hot blood gush from their mouth, or they went mad, their mind unhinged. For
them, Master Gotama, the mind follows the body and is ruled by the body. Why
is that? Because the mind is undeveloped.

"And there are some recluses and brahmins who live committed to development
of mind, but not to development of body. They are struck by painful mental
feeling. It has happened before that someone struck by painful mental
feeling had their thighs seize up, or their heart burst, or hot blood gush
from their mouth, or they went mad, their mind unhinged. For them, Master
Gotama, the body follows the mind and is ruled by the mind. Why is that?
Because the body is undeveloped.

"And it seems to me, Master Gotama, that Master Gotama's disciples surely
live committed to development of mind, but not to development of body."

"But, Aggivessana, what have you heard development of body is?"

"Take Nanda Vaccha, Kisa Saṅkicca, and Makkhali Gosāla, Master Gotama. They
go naked and reject conventions. They lick their hands. They don't come when
asked, and they don't wait when asked. They don't accept food brought to
them, food prepared for them, or invitations to a meal. They don't accept
food from the mouth of a pot or from the mouth of a bowl. They don't accept
it across a threshold, across a stick, or across a pestle. They don't accept
it from two people eating together, from a pregnant woman, from a woman
breastfeeding, or from a woman who has gone off with a man. They don't accept
food collected in a famine, or food where a dog is waiting, or where flies
are swarming. They take no fish or meat, and they drink no liquor, wine, or
fermented grain drink.

"They go to one house and take one mouthful, or to two houses and take two
mouthfuls, and so on up to seven houses and seven mouthfuls. They live on one
small dish a day, or two, and so on up to seven. They eat once a day, or once
every two days, and so on up to once every seven days. In this way they live
committed to eating at set intervals, even as long as once a fortnight."

"But, Aggivessana, do they keep themselves going on only that much?"

"No, Master Gotama. Sometimes they eat plenty of rich snacks, eat plenty of
rich meals, taste plenty of rich delicacies, and drink plenty of rich drinks.
With these they give the body back its strength, build it up, and fatten
it."

"What they gave up earlier, Aggivessana, they gather up again later. That is
how this body grows and wastes away.

"And what have you heard development of mind is, Aggivessana?"

When the Buddha asked Saccaka about development of mind, he could not
answer.

### Undeveloped and Developed in Body and Mind

Then the Buddha said to Saccaka:

"Aggivessana, the development of body you described first is not the
development of body that is in line with the teaching in the training of the
noble one. You do not even understand development of body, Aggivessana. How
could you understand development of mind?

"But I will tell you how someone is undeveloped in body and undeveloped in
mind, and how someone is developed in body and developed in mind. Listen
carefully, attend well, and I will speak."

"Yes, sir," Saccaka replied.

The Buddha said this:

"And how, Aggivessana, is someone undeveloped in body and undeveloped in
mind?

"Take an unlearned ordinary person in whom pleasant feeling arises. When
pleasant feeling touches them, they are infatuated with pleasure and give
themselves over to that infatuation. That pleasant feeling ends. When it
ends, painful feeling arises. When painful feeling touches them, they
grieve, they wear themselves out, they wail, they beat their chest and cry,
and they lose their bearings.

"For them, Aggivessana, pleasant feeling that has arisen takes over the heart
because the body is undeveloped, and painful feeling that has arisen takes
over the heart because the mind is undeveloped. Whenever anyone has it both
ways like this, with pleasant feeling taking over the heart because the body
is undeveloped and painful feeling taking over the heart because the mind is
undeveloped, that is how someone is undeveloped in body and undeveloped in
mind.

"And how, Aggivessana, is someone developed in body and developed in mind?

"Take a learned noble disciple in whom pleasant feeling arises. When
pleasant feeling touches them, they are not infatuated with pleasure and do
not give themselves over to that infatuation. That pleasant feeling ends.
When it ends, painful feeling arises. When painful feeling touches them,
they do not grieve, they do not wear themselves out, they do not wail, they
do not beat their chest and cry, and they do not lose their bearings.

"For them, Aggivessana, pleasant feeling that has arisen does not take over
the heart because the body is developed, and painful feeling that has arisen
does not take over the heart because the mind is developed. Whenever anyone
has it both ways like this, with pleasant feeling not taking over the heart
because the body is developed and painful feeling not taking over the heart
because the mind is developed, that is how someone is developed in body and
developed in mind."

### Saccaka's Challenge

"I have confidence in Master Gotama, because Master Gotama is developed in
body and developed in mind."

"Certainly, Aggivessana, you said that to needle me and to provoke me. Still,
I will answer you.

"Ever since I shaved off my hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and went
forth from home into homelessness, it has been impossible for pleasant
feeling that arose to take over my heart, or for painful feeling that arose
to take over my heart."

"Surely, then, Master Gotama never has pleasant feeling of the kind that
could take over the heart once it arose. Surely Master Gotama never has
painful feeling of the kind that could take over the heart once it arose."

"How could I not have, Aggivessana?

### Going Forth

"Before my awakening, Aggivessana, when I was still unawakened but intent on
awakening, I thought: 'Life at home is crowded and dusty. Going forth is
wide open. It is not easy for someone living at home to lead the spiritual
life utterly full and utterly pure, as if polished like a shell. What if I
were to shave off my hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from
home into homelessness?'

"Some time later, while I was still young, with jet-black hair, blessed with
youth, in the prime of life, I shaved off my hair and beard, put on ochre
robes, and went forth from home into homelessness, though my mother and
father did not want it and were weeping, their faces wet with tears.

### Āḷāra Kālāma

"Once I had gone forth, I was searching for what is wholesome, seeking the
unsurpassed state of peace. I went to Āḷāra Kālāma and said to him:
'Reverend Kālāma, I want to live the spiritual life in this teaching and
training.'

"When I said this, Aggivessana, Āḷāra Kālāma said to me: 'Stay, venerable.
This teaching is such that a sensible person can soon realize their own
teacher's doctrine for themselves with direct knowledge, and enter and
remain in it.'

"I quickly learned that teaching. As far as lip-service and repeating what I
had been told, I spoke the language of knowledge and the language of the
elders, and I claimed, 'I know, I see.' So did others.

"Then I thought: 'Āḷāra Kālāma does not declare that he has realized this
teaching for himself with direct knowledge, and entered and remains in it,
on faith alone. Surely Āḷāra Kālāma lives knowing and seeing this teaching.'

"So I went to Āḷāra Kālāma and said: 'Reverend Kālāma, how far do you
declare that you have realized this teaching for yourself with direct
knowledge, and entered and remain in it?'

"When I said this, Āḷāra Kālāma declared the sphere of nothingness.

"Then I thought: 'It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has confidence. I have
confidence too. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has energy. I have energy
too. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has remembering. I have remembering too.
It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has composure. I have composure too. It is
not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has discernment. I have discernment too. What if I
were to exert myself to realize the teaching that Āḷāra Kālāma declares he
has realized for himself with direct knowledge, and entered and remains in?'

"I quickly realized that teaching for myself with direct knowledge, and I
entered and remained in it. Then I went to Āḷāra Kālāma and said: 'Reverend
Kālāma, is this as far as you declare that you have realized this teaching
for yourself with direct knowledge, and entered it?'

"'This is as far as I declare it, reverend.'

"'I too, reverend, have realized this teaching this far for myself with
direct knowledge, and I have entered and remain in it.'

"'We are fortunate, reverend, very fortunate, to see such a venerable as a
fellow practitioner. So the teaching I declare I have realized for myself
with direct knowledge and entered is the teaching you have realized for
yourself with direct knowledge and have entered and remain in. And the
teaching you have realized for yourself with direct knowledge and have
entered and remain in is the teaching I declare I have realized and entered.
So the teaching I know is the teaching you know, and the teaching you know is
the teaching I know. As I am, so are you, and as you are, so am I. Come now,
reverend, let us lead this community together.'

"So Āḷāra Kālāma, my teacher, placed me, his student, on an equal footing
with himself, and honored me with high honor.

"Then I thought: 'This teaching does not lead to disenchantment, fading,
cessation, calming, higher knowing, awakening, or nibbāna, but only as far as
rebirth in the sphere of nothingness.'

"Not satisfied with that teaching, I became disenchanted with it and left.

### Uddaka Rāmaputta

"Still searching for what is wholesome, seeking the unsurpassed state of
peace, I went to Uddaka, son of Rāma, and said to him: 'Reverend, I want to
live the spiritual life in this teaching and training.'

"When I said this, Uddaka, son of Rāma, said to me: 'Stay, venerable. This
teaching is such that a sensible person can soon realize their own teacher's
doctrine for themselves with direct knowledge, and enter and remain in it.'

"I quickly learned that teaching. As far as lip-service and repeating what I
had been told, I spoke the language of knowledge and the language of the
elders, and I claimed, 'I know, I see.' So did others.

"Then I thought: 'Rāma did not declare that he had realized this teaching for
himself with direct knowledge, and entered and remained in it, on faith
alone. Surely Rāma lived knowing and seeing this teaching.'

"So I went to Uddaka, son of Rāma, and said: 'Reverend, how far did Rāma
declare that he had realized this teaching for himself with direct
knowledge, and entered and remained in it?'

"When I said this, Uddaka, son of Rāma, declared the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition.

"Then I thought: 'It was not only Rāma who had confidence. I have confidence
too. It was not only Rāma who had energy. I have energy too. It was not only
Rāma who had remembering. I have remembering too. It was not only Rāma who
had composure. I have composure too. It was not only Rāma who had
discernment. I have discernment too. What if I were to exert myself to
realize the teaching that Rāma declared he had realized for himself with
direct knowledge, and entered and remained in?'

"I quickly realized that teaching for myself with direct knowledge, and I
entered and remained in it. Then I went to Uddaka, son of Rāma, and said:
'Reverend, is this as far as Rāma declared that he had realized this teaching
for himself with direct knowledge, and entered it?'

"'This is as far as Rāma declared it, reverend.'

"'I too, reverend, have realized this teaching this far for myself with
direct knowledge, and I have entered and remain in it.'

"'We are fortunate, reverend, very fortunate, to see such a venerable as a
fellow practitioner. So the teaching Rāma declared he had realized for
himself with direct knowledge and entered is the teaching you have realized
for yourself with direct knowledge and have entered and remain in. And the
teaching you have realized for yourself with direct knowledge and have
entered and remain in is the teaching Rāma declared he had realized and
entered. So the teaching Rāma directly knew is the teaching you know, and the
teaching you know is the teaching Rāma directly knew. As Rāma was, so are
you, and as you are, so was Rāma. Come now, reverend, you lead this
community.'

"So Uddaka, son of Rāma, my fellow practitioner, placed me in the position of
teacher, and honored me with high honor.

"Then I thought: 'This teaching does not lead to disenchantment, fading,
cessation, calming, higher knowing, awakening, or nibbāna, but only as far as
rebirth in the sphere of neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition.'

"Not satisfied with that teaching, I became disenchanted with it and left.

### Uruvelā

"Still searching for what is wholesome, seeking the unsurpassed state of
peace, I traveled stage by stage through the Magadhan country until I came
to Senānigama near Uruvelā. There I saw a delightful piece of land, a lovely
grove, a flowing river with clear water and pleasant, smooth banks, and a
village nearby for alms.

"Then I thought: 'What a delightful piece of land this is, with its lovely
grove, its flowing river with clear water and pleasant, smooth banks, and a
village nearby for alms. This is good enough for the exertion of a young man
of good family who wants to exert himself.'

"So I sat down right there, thinking: 'This is good enough for exertion.'

### The Fire-Stick Similes

"Then, Aggivessana, three similes occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard
before.

"Suppose there were a green, sappy piece of wood lying in water. Then a man
came along with an upper fire-stick, thinking: 'I will light a fire and make
heat.' What do you think, Aggivessana? Could that man light a fire and make
heat by rubbing an upper fire-stick against that green, sappy wood lying in
water?"

"No, Master Gotama."

"Why not?"

"Because that wood is green and sappy, Master Gotama, and on top of that it
is lying in water. That man would get nothing but weariness and
frustration."

"In the same way, Aggivessana, there are recluses and brahmins who do not
live withdrawn from sensual pleasures in body and mind. Their sensual
desire, sensual affection, sensual infatuation, sensual thirst, and sensual
fever for sensual pleasures has not been well abandoned and well stilled
within them. Even if those good recluses and brahmins feel painful, sharp,
harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted, they are incapable of
knowledge and vision, of unsurpassed awakening. And even if they do not feel
painful, sharp, harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted, they are
incapable of knowledge and vision, of unsurpassed awakening. This was the
first simile that occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard before.

"Then a second simile occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard before.
Suppose there were a green, sappy piece of wood lying on dry land far from
water. Then a man came along with an upper fire-stick, thinking: 'I will
light a fire and make heat.' What do you think, Aggivessana? Could that man
light a fire and make heat by rubbing an upper fire-stick against that
green, sappy wood lying on dry land far from water?"

"No, Master Gotama."

"Why not?"

"Because that wood is green and sappy, Master Gotama, even though it is
lying on dry land far from water. That man would get nothing but weariness
and frustration."

"In the same way, Aggivessana, there are recluses and brahmins who live
withdrawn from sensual pleasures in body and mind. But their sensual desire,
sensual affection, sensual infatuation, sensual thirst, and sensual fever for
sensual pleasures has not been well abandoned and well stilled within them.
Even if those good recluses and brahmins feel painful, sharp, harsh, bitter
feelings that are self-inflicted, they are incapable of knowledge and
vision, of unsurpassed awakening. And even if they do not feel painful,
sharp, harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted, they are incapable of
knowledge and vision, of unsurpassed awakening. This was the second simile
that occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard before.

"Then a third simile occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard before.
Suppose there were a dry, sapless piece of wood lying on dry land far from
water. Then a man came along with an upper fire-stick, thinking: 'I will
light a fire and make heat.' What do you think, Aggivessana? Could that man
light a fire and make heat by rubbing an upper fire-stick against that dry,
sapless wood lying on dry land far from water?"

"Yes, Master Gotama."

"Why is that?"

"Because that wood is dry and sapless, Master Gotama, and on top of that it
is lying on dry land far from water."

"In the same way, Aggivessana, there are recluses and brahmins who live
withdrawn from sensual pleasures in body and mind, and their sensual desire,
sensual affection, sensual infatuation, sensual thirst, and sensual fever for
sensual pleasures has been well abandoned and well stilled within them. Even
if those good recluses and brahmins feel painful, sharp, harsh, bitter
feelings that are self-inflicted, they are capable of knowledge and vision,
of unsurpassed awakening. And even if they do not feel painful, sharp,
harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted, they are capable of
knowledge and vision, of unsurpassed awakening. This was the third simile
that occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard before.

"These were the three similes that occurred to me, spontaneous, never heard
before.

### Crushing the Mind with the Mind

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to clench my teeth, press my tongue against
the roof of my mouth, and subdue, squeeze, and torment my mind with my
mind?'

"So I clenched my teeth, pressed my tongue against the roof of my mouth, and
subdued, squeezed, and tormented my mind with my mind. As I did, sweat poured
from my armpits. It was like a strong man grabbing a weaker man by the head
or by the shoulders and subduing, squeezing, and tormenting him. In the same
way, as I clenched my teeth, pressed my tongue against the roof of my mouth,
and subdued, squeezed, and tormented my mind with my mind, sweat poured from
my armpits.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

### Holding a Mental Theme Without Breathing

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to hold a mental theme without breathing at
all?'

"So I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth and nose. When I did,
there was a tremendous noise of winds coming out through my ears, like the
tremendous noise of a blacksmith's bellows being pumped. In the same way,
when I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth and nose, there was a
tremendous noise of winds coming out through my ears.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to hold a mental theme without breathing at
all?'

"So I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears. When I
did, tremendous winds pounded against my head. It was like a strong man
grinding my head with a sharp sword-point. In the same way, when I stopped
breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears, tremendous winds
pounded against my head.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to hold a mental theme without breathing at
all?'

"So I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears. When I
did, I had tremendous pains in my head. It was like a strong man tightening a
tough leather strap around my head. In the same way, when I stopped
breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears, I had tremendous pains
in my head.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to hold a mental theme without breathing at
all?'

"So I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears. When I
did, tremendous winds carved through my belly. It was like a skilled butcher
or his apprentice carving through a belly with a sharp meat cleaver. In the
same way, when I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and
ears, tremendous winds carved through my belly.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to hold a mental theme without breathing at
all?'

"So I stopped breathing in and out through my mouth, nose, and ears. When I
did, there was a tremendous burning in my body. It was like two strong men
grabbing a weaker man by the arms and roasting and scorching him over a pit
of glowing coals. In the same way, when I stopped breathing in and out
through my mouth, nose, and ears, there was a tremendous burning in my body.

"My energy was roused and did not flag, and remembering was established and
did not slip. But my body was strained and not at rest, because I was worn
down by that painful exertion. Even painful feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"And some deities who saw me said: 'The recluse Gotama is dead.' Other
deities said: 'The recluse Gotama is not dead, but he is dying.' Other deities
said: 'The recluse Gotama is not dead, and he is not dying. The recluse
Gotama is an arahant. This is simply how an arahant lives.'

### Starving the Body

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to stop eating altogether?'

"Then deities came to me and said: 'Dear sir, do not stop eating altogether.
If you do, we will feed divine nourishment into you through the pores of your
skin, and you will keep going on that.'

"Then I thought: 'If I claimed to be fasting completely, while these deities
fed divine nourishment into me through the pores of my skin and I kept going
on that, it would be a lie on my part.' So I refused those deities, saying,
'Enough.'

"Then I thought: 'What if I were to eat only a little food at a time, a
handful at a time, whether mung bean soup, horse gram soup, chickpea soup, or
pea soup?'

"So I ate only a little food at a time, a handful at a time, whether mung
bean soup, horse gram soup, chickpea soup, or pea soup. As I did, my body
became extremely thin.

"My limbs became like the joints of a vine or of a bamboo, because I ate so
little. My backside became like a camel's hoof, because I ate so little. My
spine stood out like a string of beads, because I ate so little. My ribs
jutted out like the broken rafters of an old shed, because I ate so little.
The gleam of my eyes sank deep into their sockets, like the gleam of water
sunk deep in a well, because I ate so little. My scalp shriveled and
withered, like a green bitter gourd cut off and shriveled and withered in
the wind and sun, because I ate so little.

"If I tried to touch the skin of my belly, I took hold of my spine. If I
tried to touch my spine, I took hold of the skin of my belly. That is how
close the skin of my belly had come to my spine, because I ate so little.

"When I tried to pass stool or urine, I fell over on my face right there,
because I ate so little.

"To soothe my body, I rubbed my limbs with my hands. As I did, the hair,
rotted at the roots, fell from my body, because I ate so little.

"And some people who saw me said: 'The recluse Gotama is black.' Other people
said: 'The recluse Gotama is not black. He is brown.' Other people said:
'The recluse Gotama is not black, and he is not brown either. His skin is the
color of a maṅgura fish.' That is how far the pure, bright color of my skin
had been ruined, because I ate so little.

### Could There Be Another Path?

"Then I thought: 'Whatever recluses and brahmins in the past have felt
painful, sharp, harsh, bitter feelings that were self-inflicted, this is the
utmost. There is none beyond it. Whatever recluses and brahmins in the future
will feel painful, sharp, harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted,
this is the utmost. There is none beyond it. Whatever recluses and brahmins
now feel painful, sharp, harsh, bitter feelings that are self-inflicted, this
is the utmost. There is none beyond it. But with this bitter, grueling
practice I have not reached any superhuman distinction in knowledge and
vision worthy of the noble ones. Could there be another path to awakening?'

"Then I thought: 'I remember sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree
while my father the Sakyan was working. Quite secluded from sensuality,
secluded from unwholesome qualities, I entered and remained in the first
mental theme, with thinking and pondering, and with rejoicing and
satisfaction born of seclusion. Could that be the path to awakening?'

"Then, following that memory, came the knowing: 'That is the path to
awakening.'

"Then I thought: 'Why am I afraid of that satisfaction, a satisfaction that
has nothing to do with sensuality or with unwholesome qualities?'

"Then I thought: 'I am not afraid of that satisfaction, a satisfaction that
has nothing to do with sensuality or with unwholesome qualities.'

### Eating Again

"Then I thought: 'It is not easy to reach that satisfaction with a body this
badly emaciated. What if I were to eat some solid food, some rice and
porridge?'

"So I ate some solid food, some rice and porridge.

"At that time five bhikkhus were attending on me, thinking: 'Whatever
teaching the recluse Gotama reaches, he will tell us.' But when I ate some
solid food, some rice and porridge, those five bhikkhus became disenchanted
with me and left, saying: 'The recluse Gotama lives in luxury. He has given
up exertion and gone back to luxury.'

### The Four Mental Themes

"So I ate solid food and got my strength back. Then, quite secluded from
sensuality, secluded from unwholesome qualities, I entered and remained in
the first mental theme, with thinking and pondering, and with rejoicing and
satisfaction born of seclusion. But even pleasant feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"With thinking and pondering subsiding, with inner clarity and unification of
mind, I entered and remained in the second mental theme, without thinking and
pondering, with rejoicing and satisfaction born of composure. But even
pleasant feeling like that, once it arose, did not take over my heart.

"With the fading of rejoicing, I remained in dynamic balance, remembering and
clearly knowing, and I experienced satisfaction in the body. I entered and
remained in the third mental theme, of which the noble ones say: 'Someone
with dynamic balance and remembering lives in satisfaction.' But even
pleasant feeling like that, once it arose, did not take over my heart.

"With the abandoning of satisfaction and discomfort, and with the earlier
ending of gladness and sadness, I entered and remained in the fourth mental
theme, without discomfort and without satisfaction, purified by dynamic
balance and remembering. But even pleasant feeling like that, once it arose,
did not take over my heart.

### The Three Knowledges

"With my heart composed like this, purified, bright, stainless, free from
corruption, pliable, workable, steady, and unshakable, I inclined it toward
the knowledge of recollecting past lives. I recollected many kinds of past
lives, beginning with one birth. In this way I recollected many kinds of
past lives, with their aspects and particulars.

"This was the first clear knowledge I reached, Aggivessana, in the first
watch of the night. Ignorance was dispelled and clear knowledge arose;
darkness was dispelled and light arose, as happens for someone who lives
diligent, ardent, and resolved. But even pleasant feeling like that, once it
arose, did not take over my heart.

"With my heart composed like this, purified, bright, stainless, free from
corruption, pliable, workable, steady, and unshakable, I inclined it toward
the knowledge of the passing away and reappearance of beings. With the divine
eye, purified and surpassing the human, I saw beings passing away and
reappearing, low and high, beautiful and ugly, in good places and bad places.
I understood how beings move on according to their actions.

"This was the second clear knowledge I reached in the middle watch of the
night. Ignorance was dispelled and clear knowledge arose; darkness was
dispelled and light arose, as happens for someone who lives diligent,
ardent, and resolved. But even pleasant feeling like that, once it arose,
did not take over my heart.

"With my heart composed like this, purified, bright, stainless, free from
corruption, pliable, workable, steady, and unshakable, I inclined it toward
the knowledge of the wearing away of the outflows.

"I directly knew as it has come to be: 'This is dissatisfaction.' I directly
knew as it has come to be: 'This is the origin of dissatisfaction.' I
directly knew as it has come to be: 'This is the quenching of
dissatisfaction.' I directly knew as it has come to be: 'This is the way of
practice leading to the quenching of dissatisfaction.'

"I directly knew as it has come to be: 'These are the outflows.' I directly
knew as it has come to be: 'This is the origin of the outflows.' I directly
knew as it has come to be: 'This is the quenching of the outflows.' I
directly knew as it has come to be: 'This is the way of practice leading to
the quenching of the outflows.'

"As I knew and saw like this, my heart was released from the sensual
outflow, from the outflow of becoming, and from the outflow of ignorance.
When it was released, there was the knowledge: 'It is released.' I directly
knew: 'Birth is worn away, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be
done has been done, there is no more of this state of being.'

"This was the third clear knowledge I reached in the last watch of the
night. Ignorance was dispelled and clear knowledge arose; darkness was
dispelled and light arose, as happens for someone who lives diligent,
ardent, and resolved. But even pleasant feeling like that, once it arose,
did not take over my heart.

### Teaching and Resting

"I remember, Aggivessana, teaching the Dhamma to an assembly of many
hundreds. Each person there thinks: 'The recluse Gotama is teaching the
Dhamma just for me.' But it should not be seen like that. The Tathāgata
teaches others the Dhamma only so that they will understand.

"When that talk is over, I steady my heart inwardly on that same sign of
composure as before. I settle it, make it direct, and bring it into
composure. That is how I live all the time."

"That is believable of Master Gotama, as it would be of an arahant, a fully
awakened Buddha. But does Master Gotama remember sleeping during the day?"

"I remember, Aggivessana, in the last month of the hot season, after the
meal, back from alms-round, spreading out my outer robe folded in four,
lying down on my right side, and falling asleep remembering and clearly
knowing."

"Some recluses and brahmins call that living in confusion, Master Gotama."

"That is not what makes someone confused or not confused, Aggivessana. But I
will tell you how someone is confused and how someone is not confused.
Listen carefully, attend well, and I will speak."

"Yes, sir," Saccaka replied.

The Buddha said this:

### Who Is Confused

"Aggivessana, whoever has not abandoned the outflows that corrupt, that lead
to renewed becoming, that bring trouble, that result in dissatisfaction, and
that lead to birth, ageing, and death in the future, I call confused. It is
by not abandoning the outflows that someone is confused.

"Whoever has abandoned the outflows that corrupt, that lead to renewed
becoming, that bring trouble, that result in dissatisfaction, and that lead
to birth, ageing, and death in the future, I call not confused. It is by
abandoning the outflows that someone is not confused.

"The Tathāgata has abandoned the outflows that corrupt, that lead to renewed
becoming, that bring trouble, that result in dissatisfaction, and that lead
to birth, ageing, and death in the future. He has cut them off at the root,
made them like a palm stump, and done away with them, so that they cannot
arise in the future.

"Just as a palm tree with its crown cut off cannot grow again, in the same
way the Tathāgata has abandoned the outflows that corrupt, that lead to
renewed becoming, that bring trouble, that result in dissatisfaction, and
that lead to birth, ageing, and death in the future. He has cut them off at
the root, made them like a palm stump, and done away with them, so that they
cannot arise in the future."

### Saccaka's Response

When this was said, Saccaka said to the Buddha:

"It is amazing, Master Gotama, it is astonishing. Master Gotama has been
spoken to so provocatively, again and again, and assailed with such pointed
words. Yet the color of his skin brightens and his face becomes clear, as
you would expect of an arahant, a fully awakened Buddha.

"I remember debating Pūraṇa Kassapa, Master Gotama. When I debated him, he
dodged the question by bringing up something else, pulled the talk off
topic, and showed irritation, aversion, and resentment. But Master Gotama has
been spoken to so provocatively, again and again, and assailed with such
pointed words. Yet the color of his skin brightens and his face becomes
clear, as you would expect of an arahant, a fully awakened Buddha.

"I remember debating Makkhali Gosāla, Ajita Kesakambala, Pakudha Kaccāyana,
and Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta in the same way. And I remember debating Nigaṇṭha
Nātaputta, Master Gotama. When I debated him, he too dodged the question by
bringing up something else, pulled the talk off topic, and showed
irritation, aversion, and resentment. But Master Gotama has been spoken to
so provocatively, again and again, and assailed with such pointed words. Yet
the color of his skin brightens and his face becomes clear, as you would
expect of an arahant, a fully awakened Buddha.

"Well now, Master Gotama, we must go. We have many duties and much to do."

"Please, Aggivessana, go when you are ready."

Then Saccaka approved and agreed with what the Buddha had said, got up from
his seat, and left.

The sixth discourse is finished.
