# MN 22 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 22: Alagaddūpama Sutta](mn22-alagaddupama-sutta.md). The main translation
is the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 22 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially `viññāṇa`,
  `vedanā`, `saññā`, `saṅkhārā`, `nibbidā`, `virāga`, `vimutti`, `āsava`,
  `avijjā`, `taṇhā`, `saṁyojana`, `asmimāna`, `upadhi`, `paññā`.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as the repository's control
  surface for: (1) the wrong-grasp / right-grasp distinction via the cobra
  simile; (2) the raft simile — the teaching is for crossing, not for holding;
  (3) the `netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā` refrain across the five
  heaps; and (4) the five liberation epithets.

## Governing Decisions

- The `netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā` refrain is rendered "not mine,
  I am not this, this is not my self." The three-part formula:
  `netaṁ mama` = not mine; `nesohamasmi` = I am not this (literally "not-he-I-am");
  `na meso attā` = this is not my self (literally "not this my self"). The
  positive form: `etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā` = "this is mine, I am
  this, this is my self." The triple is used throughout the six-grounds section
  and the full-sweep section. This is the central doctrinal formula of MN 22
  and should be treated as a governing rendering for all future surfaces where
  the formula appears.
- `alagadda` is rendered `cobra` (Sujato: "cobra"). The sutta title
  `Alagaddūpama` = "cobra simile." The Pali `alagadda` names a venomous snake
  — the rendering "cobra" is conventional and clear.
- `duggahita` / `suggahita` are rendered `wrongly grasped` / `correctly
  grasped`. These two terms are the axis of the sutta's central teaching: the
  same teaching can be grasped in a way that causes harm (wrongly) or in a way
  that produces welfare (correctly). The cobra simile makes the contrast
  concrete: grasping a cobra by the coil causes a fatal bite; pinning it and
  grasping it by the neck is safe.
- `kullūpama` is rendered "the teaching as like a raft." `Kulla` = raft;
  `upama` = simile. The full formula: `Kullūpamaṁ vo, bhikkhave, dhammaṁ
  desessāmi nittharaṇatthāya, no gahaṇatthāya` = "I will teach you the
  teaching as like a raft — for crossing over, not for holding on." This is
  the source sutta for the raft simile referenced in MN 38. The rendering
  "for crossing over, not for holding on" is used here and should be used
  consistently in any future surface referencing this simile.
- `antarāyika dhamma` is rendered "obstructing quality." `Antarāyika` =
  making an obstruction, causing an obstacle. Ariṭṭha's wrong view is that the
  qualities the Blessed One describes as obstructing do not actually obstruct
  when one engages in them. The rendering "obstructing quality" preserves the
  Pali's directness without importing "hindrance" (which is reserved for
  `nīvaraṇa`).
- `diṭṭhiṭṭhāna` is rendered "ground for views." The sutta names six grounds:
  the five heaps (individually) + whatever is seen, heard, sensed, known,
  reached, sought, and explored by the feeling mind + the eternalist view. On
  each of these grounds, the untrained person makes the "mine / I am / my self"
  claim. No standalone lexicon entry; future revisions should add a minor entry
  for this compound.
- `paritassati / paritassanā` are rendered "is anxious / anxiety." The
  dialogue in mn22:18-21 explores four combinations of presence / absence of
  this anxiety with respect to external things and internal self-view. No
  standalone lexicon entry; the natural English "anxiety" is used.
- `svākkhāta` = "well explained." The closing formula `Evaṁ svākkhāto mayā
  dhammo uttāno vivaṭo pakāsito chinnapilotiko` is rendered "The teaching thus
  well explained by me is open, laid bare, illuminated, and without patchwork."
  `Uttāna` = open/on the surface; `vivaṭa` = laid bare/uncovered; `pakāsita` =
  illuminated/made clear; `chinnapilotika` = literally "cut-patch-cloth" — the
  teaching is not a patched garment full of hidden joins. "Without patchwork"
  captures this metaphor.
- The five liberation epithets (mn22:30-35) are rendered:
  1. `ukkhittapaligha` = "one who has lifted the crossbar" — `paligha`
     (crossbar/bolt) = ignorance; when ignorance is abandoned, the crossbar is
     lifted.
  2. `saṅkiṇṇaparikkha` = "one who has filled in the moat" — `parikkha`
     (moat/ditch) = transmigration through births in future becoming.
  3. `abbūḷhesika` = "one who has pulled up the stake" — `esika` (post/stake)
     = ignorant wanting; when ignorant wanting is abandoned, the stake that
     holds the being to existence is pulled up.
  4. `niraggaḷa` = "one who is unlatched" — `aggaḷa` (bolt/latch) = five
     lower fetters; when these are abandoned, the door can no longer be
     latched shut.
  5. `ariyo pannaddhaja pannabhāra visaṁyutta` = "a noble one with banner
     lowered, burden dropped, and fully detached" — `panndhaja` = banner
     lowered (no longer fighting); `pannabhāra` = burden dropped; `visaṁyutta`
     = fully detached. The root condition: the conceit 'I am' (`asmimāna`).
- The five levels of practitioners in the closing section (mn22:42-47) are
  rendered using established house terms:
  - `arahanto khīṇāsavā` = "arahants with outflows exhausted"
  - `pañcorambhāgiyāni saṁyojanāni pahīnāni` → five lower fetters abandoned;
    `opapātikā` = spontaneously reborn (non-returners in the pure abodes)
  - `tīṇi saṁyojanāni pahīnāni, rāgadosamohā tanubhūtā` → three fetters
    abandoned, passion/aversion/delusion weakened: once-returners
    (`sakadāgāmino`)
  - `tīṇi saṁyojanāni pahīnāni` → three fetters abandoned: stream-enterers
    (`sotāpannā`)
  - `dhammānusārino saddhānusārino` = "followers of the teaching and followers
    of confidence" → heading for awakening
  - `saddhāmattaṁ pemamattaṁ` = "a measure of confidence and affection" →
    heading for the higher realms
- `mn22:36.1-4`: "Even in this very life, bhikkhus, the Tathāgata is not to
  be traced" (`ananuvijja`). This is the key counter to the annihilationism
  charge that follows: the Tathāgata does not teach the destruction of an
  existing being, because even now the Tathāgata is not traceable as a fixed
  entity. This passage directly supports the not-self teaching that precedes
  it.

## Re-audit Calls

- The `netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā` refrain has no standalone
  formula record. This governing usage in MN 22 should be the basis for
  creating one. The refrain recurs in the Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta (SN 22.59)
  and in the full-heap analysis sections of many surfaces. A formula record
  would prevent the three-part structure from being rendered inconsistently
  across future surfaces.
- `diṭṭhiṭṭhāna` (ground for views) has no lexicon entry. It appears in
  the six-grounds analysis and in the anxiety dialogues. A minor entry
  should be added when the views-cluster is next governed.
- `opapātika` (spontaneously reborn / non-returner) has no lexicon entry.
  It is used in the five-levels closing section. A minor entry should be
  added when the awakening-stage family is next governed.
- `abbūḷhesika` (stake pulled up = craving abandoned) — the "stake" metaphor
  names the structural post that holds the person in place. In the Pali,
  `esika` can be a pillar or a stake. Rendering as "stake" rather than
  "pillar" better captures its function: it is the driven post that anchors
  the being to continued becoming. The root condition is ignorant wanting
  (`taṇhā`), not craving in a generic sense.
- The Bilara abbreviates the ten kāma similes with `…pe…` throughout the
  Ariṭṭha episode. The translation expands the list in full at the first
  occurrence (in the bhikkhus' argument with Ariṭṭha) and thereafter uses
  "a skeleton of bones; a scrap of meat; a grass torch; a pit of glowing
  coals; a dream; borrowed goods; fruit on a tree; a butcher's knife and
  chopping board; swords and spears; and a snake's head" in subsequent
  repetitions rather than spelling out each in full.
- The Bilara abbreviates the five-heap formula across the six-grounds section
  and the full-sweep section with `…pe…` for felt experience, recognition,
  and what is put together. These are expanded in the translation consistent
  with the repository's policy of preserving repetition for study readability.

## Editorial Presentation

- The main translation is divided into eleven sections: `The Setting`,
  `Ariṭṭha's Wrong View`, `Wrong Grasp and Right Grasp`, `The Teaching as a
  Raft`, `Six Grounds for Views`, `Anxiety About What Doesn't Exist`, `No
  Permanent Possession, No Safe Self-View`, `Impermanent, Suffering, Not
  Self`, `The Full Sweep`, `The Five Epithets of Liberation`, `The Liberated
  One Is Not Found`, `What I Describe Is Suffering and Its Cessation`, `Give
  Up What Isn't Yours`, and `The Teaching Well Explained`.
- The Ariṭṭha episode (mn22:2-9) is parallel in structure to the Sāti episode
  in MN 38: a monk holds a wrong view, other monks cannot dissuade him, they
  report to the Blessed One, the Blessed One summons him, refutes him publicly,
  and then addresses the broader community. The shared formula — abashed,
  shoulders drooping, downcast, dejected, unable to respond — is used
  identically in both surfaces.

## Edition Status

- This companion supports the first stable study edition of the repo's
  MN 22 translation surface.
- Future revisions should focus on: creating a formula record for the
  `netaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā` refrain; adding minor entries
  for `diṭṭhiṭṭhāna`, `opapātika`, and `paritassanā`; and confirming
  whether the raft-simile formula should gain its own formula record
  cross-linking MN 22 and MN 38.
