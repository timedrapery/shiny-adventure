# MN 122 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 122: Mahāsuññata Sutta](mn122-mahasunnata-sutta.md). The main translation
is the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 122 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`mn122:0.1` to `mn122:27.7`), fetched from the upstream Bilara
  root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: MN 18 for the setting and the closing formula; MN 19 for
  the four mental themes and the three kinds of thinking; MN 119 for
  `saṇṭhapeti sannisādeti ekodiṁ karoti samādahati`; SN 56.11 for `hīna gamma
  pothujjanika anariya anatthasaṁhita` and for `ponobbhavika`; MN 26 for the
  disenchantment formula; DN 2 for the Tathāgata's epithets, the list of low
  talk, and the knowing and seeing of release; MN 99 for the five kinds of
  sensual stimulation; SN 22.89 for observing arising and passing away in the
  five clung-to heaps and for `asmimāna`.
- Working method: the discourse has three movements. First, why a bhikkhu who
  loves company cannot reach the satisfactions of seclusion. Second, the
  Tathāgata's own way of dwelling in emptiness and how a bhikkhu trains toward
  it, through the posture, talk, thinking, sensual-stimulation, and heaps
  checks. Third, why a disciple follows the teacher, the three kinds of ruin,
  and the call to treat the teacher as a friend.

## Governing Decisions

- `suññatā` is `emptiness`, its governed default. `ajjhattaṁ suññataṁ`,
  `bahiddhā suññataṁ`, and `ajjhattabahiddhā suññataṁ` are `emptiness
  internally`, `emptiness externally`, and `emptiness internally and
  externally`, following the governed `ajjhatta` and `bahiddhā`.
- `sabbanimittānaṁ amanasikārā` is `by not attending to any signs`. `nimitta`
  keeps its governed `sign`; the record discourages `meditation object`.
- `āneñja` is `the imperturbable`, following `aneñjābhisaṅkhāra`
  (`imperturbable constructing`). The text does not say which attainment it
  means, and the translation does not supply one.
- `vihāra` has two senses in this text. Kāḷakhemaka's and Ghaṭāya's `vihāra`
  is a building, rendered `dwelling` (the record's alternate; `monastery`
  would mislead for a lay Sakyan's residence). The Tathāgata's `vihāra` is a
  way of practicing, rendered `way of dwelling`, and the verb `viharati` is
  `dwells` in that passage. Both are document-local.
- `citta` is `heart`, its governed default and the MN 19 and MN 119 wording.
  `cetaso samudācāro` is `my mind becomes involved`: `cetas` is the
  repository's `mind`, and `samudācāra` names the mind's getting busy with
  the object.
- `ajjhattameva cittaṁ saṇṭhapeti sannisādeti ekodiṁ karoti samādahati` is
  `steadies the heart within, settles it, makes it single, and unifies it`.
  MN 119 has the intransitive form of the same four verbs (`the heart settles
  within, grows quiet, becomes single, and is mentally unified`). Here the
  verbs are causative, so they are active. `samādahati` keeps its governed
  `unifies` and `ekodi` its governed `single`.
- `samādhinimitta` is `sign of unification`, combining `samādhi`'s compact
  form with `nimitta`.
- `pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati vimuccati` is `leaps forward, becomes
  clear, settles, and is released`. `pasīdati` follows `pasāda`'s default
  `clarity`: the subject is the heart, not a person's trust in a teacher.
  `vimuccati` follows `vimutti` (`release`).
- `sampajāno hoti` is `they clearly know it`, following `sampajañña`
  (`clear knowing`). The refrain `itiha tattha sampajāno hoti` is kept every
  time it occurs.
- The four mental themes are copied from MN 19 with the person changed from
  first to third. `kāma` is `sensuality`; `sukha` is `satisfaction` in the
  first two themes and `ease` in the third and fourth, as in MN 19.
- `nekkhammasukhaṁ pavivekasukhaṁ upasamasukhaṁ sambodhisukhaṁ` is `the
  satisfaction of renunciation, the satisfaction of deep seclusion, the
  satisfaction of calming, the satisfaction of awakening`. `paviveka` keeps
  its governed `deep seclusion`, distinct from `viveka` (`seclusion`), which
  the text uses separately (`vivekaninna`, `vivekamanubrūhayamāno`).
- `sāmāyikaṁ vā kantaṁ cetovimuttiṁ ... asāmāyikaṁ vā akuppaṁ` is `a release
  of mind that is temporary and delightful, or one that is not temporary and
  is unshakable`. `cetovimutti` and `akuppā cetovimutti` keep their governed
  renderings.
- `saṅgaṇikārāma saṅgaṇikarata saṅgaṇikārāmataṁ anuyutta gaṇārāma gaṇarata
  gaṇasammudita` is `delights in company, loves company, and is given to
  delighting in company, delights in a group, loves a group, and is pleased
  with a group`. The repetition is the point and is kept whole each time.
- The ten topics of talk keep their record wording: `fewness of wishes`
  (`appicchatā`), `contentment` (`santuṭṭhi`), `deep seclusion` (`paviveka`),
  `non-entanglement` (`asaṁsagga`), `arousing energy` (`vīriyārambha`),
  `conduct` (`sīla`), `unification` (`samādhi`, compact form in a list),
  `discernment` (`paññā`), `release` (`vimutti`), and `the knowing and seeing
  of release` (`vimutti-ñāṇadassana`).
- `abhisallekhikā cetovinīvaraṇasappāyā` is `wholly self-effacing, that helps
  the heart come free of hindrances`. `sallekha` keeps its governed
  `self-effacement`, and `nīvaraṇa` its governed `hindrance`.
- The disenchantment formula (`nibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya
  abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya`) follows its formula record and MN 26: `to
  disenchantment, fading, cessation, calming, higher knowing, awakening, and
  nibbāna`. In its negative form it ends with `or nibbāna`, as in MN 26.
- `hīnā gammā pothujjanikā anariyā anatthasaṁhitā` is `low, vulgar, ordinary,
  ignoble, and unbeneficial`, copied from SN 56.11.
- The three kinds of thinking keep their records: `thinking of sensuality`,
  `thinking of ill will`, `thinking of harming`, and their opposites
  `thinking of renunciation`, `thinking of non-ill-will`, `thinking of
  non-harming`, as in MN 19.
- `niyyānikā niyyanti takkarassa sammādukkhakkhayāya` is `lead out, and ...
  lead whoever acts on them to the complete ending of dissatisfaction`.
  `dukkha` keeps its governed `dissatisfaction`. MN 11's `leading out` is the
  control for `niyyānika`.
- `pañca kāmaguṇā` is `five kinds of sensual stimulation`, with the
  description of each copied from MN 99. `āyatana` is `field`, its governed
  default.
- `chandarāga` is `desire and passion`, its record.
- The heaps passage is copied from SN 22.89: `remain observing arising and
  passing away`, `Such is form. Such is the arising of form. Such is the
  vanishing of form.` `asmimāna` is `conceit "I am"`.
- `ekantakusalā kusalāyātikā ariyā lokuttarā anavakkantā pāpimatā` is
  `entirely wholesome, with wholesome outcomes, noble, beyond the world, and
  out of the Wicked One's reach`. `lokuttara` keeps its governed
  `beyond-the-world`, unhyphenated here because it stands as a predicate.
  `pāpimā` is `the Wicked One`, as in MN 26.
- The Tathāgata's epithets follow DN 2 word for word.
- `ācariyūpaddava`, `antevāsūpaddava`, and `brahmacārūpaddava` are `the ruin
  of a teacher`, `the ruin of a pupil`, and `the ruin of someone who lives the
  spiritual life`. `upaddava` has no record; `ruin` names what happens to the
  person, which is what the three definitions describe. `antevāsin` keeps its
  record's sense of a pupil who lives with the teacher, shortened to `pupil`
  because the passage pairs it with `teacher`. `brahmacariya` is `spiritual
  life`.
- `saṅkilesikā ponobbhavikā sadarā dukkhavipākā āyatiṁ jātijarāmaraṇiyā` is
  `defiling, leading to renewed becoming, troubling, ripening in
  dissatisfaction, and leading to future birth, aging, and death`.
  `ponobbhavika` follows SN 56.11.
- `vinipāta` is `a downfall state`, its record.
- `mittavatāya samudācaratha, mā sapattavatāya` is `treat me as a friend, not
  as an enemy`. `mitta` keeps its governed `friend`. `hitāya sukhāya` is `for
  your welfare and satisfaction`, keeping `sukha` as `satisfaction`;
  `anukampā` is `sympathy`.
- `suttaṁ geyyaṁ veyyākaraṇaṁ` is `discourses, songs, and explanations`: the
  first three of the traditional nine divisions of the teaching, kept plain.
- `sutā dhātā vacasā paricitā manasānupekkhitā diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā` is
  `heard ..., remembered ..., recited ..., examined ... with the mind, and
  penetrated ... well by view`.

## Re-audit Calls

- The source abbreviates the four mental themes (`mn122:8.1` to `8.4`), the
  external, internal-and-external, and imperturbable passages in both the
  failing and the succeeding sequence (`mn122:9.6`, `9.7`, `10.7`, `10.8`),
  the five kinds of sensual stimulation (`mn122:14.4` to `14.6`), and the
  middle three heaps (`mn122:16.3` to `16.5`). The study edition writes all of
  them out, as SN 22.48 and SN 22.26 do for their abbreviated heaps, so that a
  reader sees each step the text intends. In the emptiness passages the
  source keeps `Ānanda` only in the internal and imperturbable steps; the
  expansion keeps that pattern.
- `surākathā`: the Mahāsaṅgīti root spells the item `surā-`, which would mean
  liquor. The same list of low talk appears in DN 2, where this repository
  renders `heroes`, following the reading `sūra-`. The study edition keeps
  `heroes` for consistency across the corpus and records the spelling here.
- `pavayha pavayha` (`mn122:27.3`) is disputed. It is paired with `niggayha
  niggayha`, `restraining` or `correcting`. Some translators read `pavayha` as
  `urging on`, some as `testing`, some as `having purified`. This edition uses
  `pressing you again and again`, which keeps the contrast with a potter
  handling raw clay gently and does not settle the dispute.
- `api paṇujjamāno` is `even when told to go away`. The verb is passive: the
  disciple keeps following even when sent off.
- `uyyojanikapaṭisaṁyuttaṁ kathaṁ` is `speaks to them in a way that is
  concerned with sending them on their way`: the Tathāgata answers visitors
  so that they leave satisfied, not so that they stay.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse does not say company is wrong. It says that a bhikkhu who
  delights in company will not gain the satisfactions of seclusion at will,
  and it is spoken when many bhikkhus are gathered for robe-making.
- Emptiness here is a way of dwelling: not attending to signs, entering and
  remaining in emptiness internally. The text does not explain it as a view
  or a metaphysical claim, and the translation does not add one.
- The instruction expects failure first. The bhikkhu attends to emptiness and
  the heart does not go there; the bhikkhu clearly knows that, returns to
  steadying the heart in the earlier sign of unification, and tries again.
  Knowing clearly what is happening is the practice at each step.
- The posture, talk, thinking, and sensual-stimulation checks carry the
  dwelling into the rest of the day. The heaps passage ends the training: the
  conceit "I am" is abandoned, and the bhikkhu clearly knows that it has been.
- The ruin of someone who lives the spiritual life is worse than the other two
  because it happens under a fully awakened teacher who did not fall.
