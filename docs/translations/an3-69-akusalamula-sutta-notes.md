# AN 3.69 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[AN 3.69: Akusalamūla Sutta](an3-69-akusalamula-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `an3.69:1.1`–`an3.69:16.6`, stored in
  `.bilara-cache/an3.69_root-pli-ms.json`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/an3.69/pli/ms>.
- The source contains 713 Pali words, counting expanded source segments but
  not reconstructing material represented by `…pe…`.
- The Pali title `Akusalamūlasutta` means "The Discourse on the Roots of What
  Is Unwholesome."

## Governing Decisions

- `lobha`, `dosa`, and `moha` are `greed`, `aversion`, and `delusion`, the
  project's governed three-root vocabulary.
- `alobha`, `adosa`, and `amoha` are `non-greed`, `non-aversion`, and
  `clarity`. `Amoha` literally has the negative form "non-delusion." The
  translation uses the governed default `clarity` because it names the
  positive ability to see without confusion in ordinary English. The first
  occurrence makes the contrast explicit through "a person free of
  delusion."
- `kusala` and `akusala` are `wholesome` and `unwholesome`: qualities and
  actions that lead respectively toward well-being or harm. These are ethical
  and practical judgments, not claims about social respectability.
- `pariyādinnacitto` is `with the heart taken over by it`. The Pali says that
  `citta`, the affective or feeling mind, has been overrun. `Heart` keeps that
  distinct from `mano`, the thinking mind, without introducing technical
  jargon into the story.
- `vadhena vā bandhanena vā jāniyā vā garahāya vā pabbājanāya vā` is
  rendered concretely as attacking, imprisoning, taking property, condemning,
  or driving someone away. The list shows power being used to inflict
  undeserved suffering; `unjustly` carries the force of `asatā` in this
  setting.
- `balavamhi balattho` is `I have power, and I want power.` The repetition
  links the ability to dominate with the desire to exercise that power.
- The five speech descriptions are stated in direct contemporary English:
  speaking at the right or wrong time, truthfully or falsely, beneficially or
  harmfully, in line with or against the Dhamma and the training.
- `nibbeṭhana` is `sort it out`. The surrounding lines concern admitting
  what is true and actively correcting what is false, not winning an argument.
- The source names three species of large tree—`sāla`, `dhava`, and
  `phandana`. The translation compresses these unfamiliar alternatives to `a
  large forest tree`; the point of the comparison is what three vines do to
  the tree.
- `diṭṭheva dhamme parinibbāyati` is `They reach complete quenching here
  and now.` The explicit present-life phrase rules out the final-at-death frame
  that `parinibbāna` often has elsewhere. `Complete quenching` preserves the
  word family's force without suggesting death or annihilation.
- Repetitions represented by `…pe…` in the source are expanded or summarized
  transparently. The parallel roots and consequences remain visible, while a
  modern reader is not forced through every duplicated sentence.
