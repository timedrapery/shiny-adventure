# SN 12.23 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[SN 12.23: Upanisa Sutta](sn12-23-upanisa-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of SN 12.23 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially `upanisa`,
  `saddha`, `pamojja`, `piti`, `passaddhi`, `sukha`, `samadhi`, `nibbida`,
  `viraga`, `vimutti`, `asava`, `tanha`, `vedana`, `vinnana`, `sankhara`,
  and `avijja`.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as the repository's control
  surface for the positive dependent-arising sequence, which runs from
  dissatisfaction through confidence → actively happy state → rejoicing →
  relaxation → satisfaction → composure → seeing things as they are →
  disenchantment → fading → release → knowledge of outflow destruction.

## Governing Decisions

- `upanisā` is rendered `vital condition`. The compound `saupanisā` (having a
  vital condition) and `anupanisā` (lacking a vital condition) are rendered
  consistently: `has a vital condition` / `does not lack a vital condition`.
  The term names the enabling, near-supporting condition — distinct from
  `paccaya` (condition in the standard dependent-arising formula). `Vital
  condition` is used throughout both the descending-chain question format and
  the full-sequence restatement.
- The positive sequence follows the current house defaults updated for this
  governance pass: `saddhā` → `confidence`; `pāmojja` → `actively happy
  state`; `pīti` → `rejoicing`; `passaddhi` → `relaxation`; `sukha` →
  `satisfaction`; `samādhi` → `composure`.
- `Composure` is used as the short form of `samādhi` here rather than the
  full `mental composure`. In the condensed vertical sequence — where each
  link is named as a single noun — `composure` reads more cleanly. The
  full `mental composure` remains the default outside sequence contexts.
- `yathābhūtañāṇadassana` is rendered `seeing things as they are`. The
  compound: `yathābhūta` (as things actually are) + `ñāṇa` (knowing) +
  `dassana` (seeing). The rendering keeps it as a single practical phrase
  rather than splitting into two nouns, which suits the sequence context
  where it appears as a single link.
- `nibbidā` is rendered `disenchantment` (major, house default).
- `virāga` is rendered `fading` (major, house default).
- `vimutti` is rendered `release` (major, house default).
- `āsavānaṁ khaye ñāṇa` is rendered `knowledge of the destruction of
  outflows`. `Āsava` → `outflow` (major, house default); `khaya` →
  `destruction`; `ñāṇa` → `knowledge`.
- The conventional dependent-arising chain (ignorance through birth)
  uses the established house renderings: `avijjā` → `ignorance`;
  `saṅkhārā` → `putting things together`; `viññāṇa` → `knowing`;
  `nāmarūpa` → `name-and-form`; `saḷāyatana` → `six sense fields`;
  `phassa` → `contact`; `vedanā` → `felt experience`; `taṇhā` →
  `ignorant wanting`; `upādāna` → `appropriation`; `bhava` → `becoming`;
  `jāti` → `birth`; `dukkha` → `dissatisfaction`.
- The five heaps in the opening are rendered: `rūpa` → `form`; `vedanā` →
  `felt experience`; `saññā` → `recognition`; `saṅkhārā` → `what is put
  together`; `viññāṇa` → `knowing`.

## Re-audit Calls

- `upanisā` is rendered `vital condition` rather than `condition` or `support`.
  The Pali `upanisā` is not the same as `paccaya` (the standard condition term
  in dependent arising). `Upanisā` names the near enabling condition — what
  makes the next link genuinely possible. `Vital condition` conveys this
  weight without importing technical jargon.
- The refrain `saupanisaṁ vadāmi, no anupanisaṁ` is rendered `has a vital
  condition — it does not lack a vital condition`. The double phrasing
  (positive then negative) is preserved rather than collapsed into one
  phrase, because the emphatic structure is the teaching point: each link
  in this chain genuinely supports the next.
- In the descending-chain section, each link's vital condition is introduced
  as `You should say: 'X.'` rather than `The answer is: 'X.'` This follows
  the Pali `'X'tissa vacanīyaṁ` (it should be said to be X) — a directive
  to the listener to hold this understanding, not a passive statement of fact.
- The full sequence in sections 6 and 8 is presented as a list of single-line
  statements rather than continuous prose. The Pali naturally falls into this
  list structure (each link stated as one clause), and the list format makes
  the chain visually scannable.
- The `…pe…` abbreviation marks in the Bilara (segments 1.5–1.7 for the
  middle heaps, and 5.2–5.6 for the middle DA links) are expanded in full,
  consistent with the repository's policy of preserving repetition for study
  readability.
- `Tatiyaṁ.` is rendered `The third discourse is finished.`

## Editorial Presentation

- The main translation is divided into five sections: `The Setting`, `Knowing
  and Seeing What?`, `Each Link Has Its Condition`, `The Full Sequence`, and
  `The Rain Simile`.
- The full sequence is stated twice: once in section 6 (after the descending
  chain), and once in section 8 (with the rain simile). Both statements are
  preserved in full rather than abbreviated. The repetition is deliberate and
  pedagogically important — the chain is heard as a whole unit after being
  built link by link.
- The rain simile precedes the final full-chain restatement. In the Pali,
  section 7 gives the simile and section 8 immediately states the chain again
  using `evameva kho` (in the same way). The editorial flow preserves this
  pairing: simile → chain restatement within the same section.
