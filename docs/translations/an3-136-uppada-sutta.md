## Translation

### Whether Tathāgatas Arise or Not

"Bhikkhus, whether Tathāgatas arise or not, that element still stands: the
stability of the pattern, the regularity of the pattern. All that has been put
together is impermanent. A Tathāgata awakens to this and breaks through to it.
Once he has, he tells it, teaches it, makes it known, sets it out, reveals it,
breaks it down, and makes it plain: 'All that has been put together is
impermanent.'

Whether Tathāgatas arise or not, that element still stands: the stability of
the pattern, the regularity of the pattern. All that has been put together is
unsatisfactory. A Tathāgata awakens to this and breaks through to it. Once he
has, he tells it, teaches it, makes it known, sets it out, reveals it, breaks
it down, and makes it plain: 'All that has been put together is
unsatisfactory.'

Whether Tathāgatas arise or not, that element still stands: the stability of
the pattern, the regularity of the pattern. All phenomena are not-self. A
Tathāgata awakens to this and breaks through to it. Once he has, he tells it,
teaches it, makes it known, sets it out, reveals it, breaks it down, and makes
it plain: 'All phenomena are not-self.'"

The fourth discourse is finished.
