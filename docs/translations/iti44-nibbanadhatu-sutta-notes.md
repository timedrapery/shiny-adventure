# Iti 44 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[Iti 44: Nibbānadhātu Sutta](iti44-nibbanadhatu-sutta.md). The main
translation is the primary study surface; this file records debated
translation choices, source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of Iti 44 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially
  `nibbāna-dhātu`, `sa-upādisesa-nibbāna-dhātu`, `anupādisesa-nibbāna-dhātu`,
  `parinibbāna-dhātu`, `āsava`, `khaya`, `rāga`, `dosa`, `moha`, `indriya`,
  `asaṅkhata`, `nirodha`, `bhava`, `vimutti`, and `citta`.
- The discourse is 135 Pali words: a two-item definition stated once, followed
  by a summary verse.
- The arahant formula at iti44:3.2 and iti44:4.2 is word-for-word the formula
  already governed in MN 1 (mn1:51.1 and three parallels), and is rendered
  here exactly as MN 1 renders it. It was not re-solved locally.

## Governing Decisions

- **`saupādisesā` and `anupādisesā` keep `with remainder` and `without
  remainder`.** Both compounds already have major entries recording those
  defaults, and this discourse is the text they were written for. The
  temptation with this pair is to explain the compounds inside the
  translation — `with the substrate of life remaining`, `with nothing of
  clinging left over` — which imports a reading of `upādisesa` that the sutta
  never states. The sutta defines each element by what it says, not by what
  the remainder consists of, and the translation keeps that reticence.
- **`upādisesa` itself is left ungoverned and is not rendered in isolation.**
  It appears here only inside the two compounds, which are governed. Giving
  the bare stem a default would force a decision the discourse does not make:
  it never says what the remainder is.
- **`pañcindriyāni` is rendered `five sense faculties`, not `five
  faculties`.** `indriya` keeps its `faculty` default, but a bare `five
  faculties` in this repository already names the spiritual set — faith,
  energy, remembering, composure, discernment — which is a different list
  governed under the awakening-factors cluster. The five meant here are the
  senses, as the rest of the sentence makes plain: they are what meets the
  agreeable and the disagreeable. `sense` is doing disambiguating work, not
  translating a word that is present.
- `avighātattā` is rendered `because these are unimpaired`, keeping the causal
  force. The point of the clause is that the arahant's senses have not been
  damaged or shut down — experience continues, and continues to arrive as
  pleasant and painful.
- `sukhadukkhaṁ paṭisaṁvedeti` is rendered `feel the pleasant and the
  painful`, following SN 36.6's handling of the same felt registers, rather
  than importing the doctrinal defaults `satisfaction` and `dissatisfaction`.
  What is at stake here is what the arahant still feels, not the first noble
  truth.
- `rāgakkhayo, dosakkhayo, mohakkhayo` is rendered as three separate wearings
  away rather than compressed to `the wearing away of passion, aversion, and
  delusion`. The Pali repeats `khaya` three times, and that repetition is what
  makes this element a description of a living person's mind rather than an
  event.
- `sabbavedayitāni anabhinanditāni sīti bhavissanti` is rendered `everything
  felt — being not relished — will become cool`. Three decisions inside one
  clause: `vedayita` is `felt` rather than `feelings`, because the Pali is the
  past participle and names what is felt rather than the aggregate;
  `anabhinandita` is `not relished`, tying it to the governed `nandī` family
  rather than to a fresh `not delighted in`; and `sīti bhavissanti` is `will
  become cool`, which keeps the cooling image the consummation cluster map
  assigns to `nibbuta` without asserting the word `nibbuta`, which is not in
  this text.
- The future tense of `bhavissanti` is preserved. Any rendering that flattens
  it to the present makes the second element sound like a state the arahant is
  already in, which is exactly the distinction the discourse exists to draw.
- `idheva` is rendered `right here`. It is the discourse's sharpest word and
  the easiest to drop: the cooling of everything felt is located here, not
  elsewhere and not after.
- `bhagavā` is rendered `the Buddha`, the repository default across all
  surfaces.

## Verse Handling

The closing verse is rendered as verse, in a `Verses` section, following the
precedent set by SN 36.6. Line breaks follow the Pali lines where the English
allows it; the last stanza runs long because `dhammasārādhigamā khaye ratā`
does not fit an English line of the same length.

- `padaṁ asaṅkhataṁ` is rendered `this unconditioned state`. `asaṅkhata` keeps
  its governed `unconditioned` default; `pada` is ungoverned and is rendered
  `state` here, following the local verse rendering already used in SN 36.6
  for `padaṁ virajaṁ asokaṁ`. This is a local verse rendering, not a new
  default for `pada`.
- `cakkhumatā` is rendered `the one with vision`. The epithet is for the
  Buddha, and the verse is already speaking about him in the third person.
- `anissitena tādinā` is rendered `independent, unshaken`. `tādi` is
  ungoverned; `unshaken` renders its settled sense without importing `such`,
  which would read as a title rather than a description.
- `bhavanettisaṅkhayā` is rendered `the conduit to becoming worn away`, in
  both places the verse uses it. `bhavanetti` is ungoverned; `conduit to
  becoming` keeps the image of a channel that leads onward, and `saṅkhaya`
  follows the governed `khaya` family into `worn away`.
- `nirujjhanti` is rendered `quenches`, from the governed `nirodha` default.
  `bhavāni sabbaso` is `every becoming`, keeping `bhava` on its headword
  default.
- `dhammasārādhigamā` is rendered `reached the heart of the Dhamma`. `sāra` is
  ungoverned; `heart` renders the pith-of-the-tree image without the
  wood-working metaphor that `heartwood` would carry into a verse that is not
  otherwise figurative.

## Itivuttaka Framing

This is the repository's first Itivuttaka surface, so the collection's framing
formulas are settled here for the ones that follow:

- `Vuttañhetaṁ bhagavatā vuttamarahatāti me sutaṁ` — `This was said by the
  Buddha, said by the arahant — so I heard:`. `arahaṁ` keeps the governed
  `arahant` rather than becoming an epithet such as `the Perfected One`.
- `Etamatthaṁ bhagavā avoca. Tatthetaṁ iti vuccati:` — `This is what the
  Buddha said. And in reference to this, it was said:`.
- `Ayampi attho vutto bhagavatā, iti me sutanti.` — `This too was said by the
  Buddha — so I heard.`
- `Sattamaṁ.` — `The seventh discourse is finished.`, following the closing
  line already used in SN 36.6.

The framing sits outside the headed sections rather than getting a section of
its own, so the reader meets the discourse the way the collection presents it.

## Re-audit Calls

- **`Iti` is in the `UNSUPPORTED` set in
  `scripts/verify_example_sources.py`.** Every citation this surface adds is
  therefore unverifiable by machine. All citations added in this pass were
  checked by hand against the cached Bilara root text
  (`.bilara-cache/iti44_root-pli-ms.json`), and that check has to be repeated
  by hand if they are edited. This is the first surface in the repository
  whose citations no script can confirm; see the workflow plan's standing note
  on the verse-collection gap.
- `bhavanetti`, `tādi`, `pada`, `sāra`, `cakkhumant`, `diṭṭhadhammika`, and
  `samparāyika` are all ungoverned and all appear in the verse. None is
  load-bearing enough on its own to justify a record from this surface alone,
  but `bhavanetti` is the closest: it carries the verse's whole argument, it
  occurs twice here, and it also appears in DN 1 and DN 16. It is the
  candidate to promote if a second surface needs it.
- `upādisesa` is deliberately left ungoverned; see Governing Decisions above.
  If a later surface uses the bare stem outside these two compounds, that
  decision has to be reopened rather than inherited.
- `parinibbāna-dhātu` cites this discourse. That citation was repaired to
  Iti 44 on 2026-08-19 during the citation-integrity pass, and this surface is
  the first to exercise the repair. The Pali it quotes,
  `anupādisesā nibbānadhātu`, is present at iti44:2.3 and iti44:4.4.

## Practice Clarifications

- The two elements are not two nibbānas. They are one thing described at two
  moments: what has already happened to a living arahant's mind, and what
  happens to everything that person feels.
- The first element is defined entirely by subtraction — passion, aversion,
  and delusion worn away — and the discourse is careful to say that the senses
  keep working. Nothing is added to the arahant, and nothing is withdrawn from
  experience.
- The verse's contrast between `visible here and now` and `what comes after`
  is the discourse's own framing and should not be read as a claim about what
  the second element is like. The prose says only that everything felt becomes
  cool.
