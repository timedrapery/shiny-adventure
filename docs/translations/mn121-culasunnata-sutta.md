## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in the Eastern Monastery, in
Migāra's Mother's mansion.

Then in the late afternoon Venerable Ānanda came out of retreat, went to the
Buddha, bowed, and sat down to one side. Seated there, he said to the Buddha:

"Bhante, at one time the Buddha was staying among the Sakyans, at a Sakyan town
named Nagaraka. There I heard and learned from the Buddha's own mouth: 'Ānanda,
these days I mostly dwell in emptiness.' I hope I heard that correctly, learned
it correctly, attended to it correctly, and remembered it correctly?"

"Yes, Ānanda, you heard it correctly, learned it correctly, attended to it
correctly, and remembered it correctly. Both before and now, I mostly dwell in
emptiness.

### The Recognition of the Forest

This mansion of Migāra's Mother is empty of elephants, cattle, horses, and
mares, empty of gold and silver, and empty of gatherings of men and women.
There is only this that is not empty: the oneness that depends on the Saṅgha of
bhikkhus. In the same way, Ānanda, a bhikkhu does not attend to the recognition
of the village or to the recognition of people. They attend to the oneness that
depends on the recognition of the forest. Their heart leaps forward into the
recognition of the forest, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of the village are not present here. Whatever disturbances there
would be dependent on the recognition of people are not present here. There is
only this much disturbance: the oneness that depends on the recognition of the
forest.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
village.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition
of people. There is only this that is not empty: the oneness that depends on
the recognition of the forest.' So they regard it as empty of whatever is not
there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

### The Recognition of Earth

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of people, not attending
to the recognition of the forest, a bhikkhu attends to the oneness that depends
on the recognition of earth. Their heart leaps forward into the recognition of
earth, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

Just as a bull's hide is stretched out with a hundred pegs until it is free of
folds, in the same way, Ānanda, not attending to whatever on this earth is high
or low, its rivers and ravines, its stumps and thorns, its mountains and uneven
ground, a bhikkhu attends to the oneness that depends on the recognition of
earth. Their heart leaps forward into the recognition of earth, becomes clear,
settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of people are not present here. Whatever disturbances there would
be dependent on the recognition of the forest are not present here. There is
only this much disturbance: the oneness that depends on the recognition of
earth.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of
people.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition
of the forest. There is only this that is not empty: the oneness that depends
on the recognition of earth.' So they regard it as empty of whatever is not
there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

### The Formless Spheres

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of the forest, not
attending to the recognition of earth, a bhikkhu attends to the oneness that
depends on the recognition of the sphere of boundless space. Their heart leaps
forward into the recognition of the sphere of boundless space, becomes clear,
settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of the forest are not present here. Whatever disturbances there
would be dependent on the recognition of earth are not present here. There is
only this much disturbance: the oneness that depends on the recognition of the
sphere of boundless space.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
forest.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition
of earth. There is only this that is not empty: the oneness that depends on the
recognition of the sphere of boundless space.' So they regard it as empty of
whatever is not there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of earth, not attending to
the recognition of the sphere of boundless space, a bhikkhu attends to the
oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing.
Their heart leaps forward into the recognition of the sphere of boundless
knowing, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of earth are not present here. Whatever disturbances there would be
dependent on the recognition of the sphere of boundless space are not present
here. There is only this much disturbance: the oneness that depends on the
recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of earth.'
They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
sphere of boundless space. There is only this that is not empty: the oneness
that depends on the recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing.' So they
regard it as empty of whatever is not there, and of what remains they discern:
'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of boundless
space, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing, a
bhikkhu attends to the oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness. Their heart leaps forward into the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of the sphere of boundless space are not present here. Whatever
disturbances there would be dependent on the recognition of the sphere of
boundless knowing are not present here. There is only this much disturbance:
the oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of nothingness.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
sphere of boundless space.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty
of the recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing. There is only this that
is not empty: the oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness.' So they regard it as empty of whatever is not there, and of what
remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of boundless
knowing, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of nothingness, a
bhikkhu attends to the oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition. Their heart leaps forward into the
recognition of the sphere of neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition, becomes
clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of the sphere of boundless knowing are not present here. Whatever
disturbances there would be dependent on the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness are not present here. There is only this much disturbance: the
oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
sphere of boundless knowing.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty
of the recognition of the sphere of nothingness. There is only this that is not
empty: the oneness that depends on the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition.' So they regard it as empty of
whatever is not there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

### The Signless Unification of Mind

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition, a bhikkhu attends to the oneness that
depends on the signless unification of mind. Their heart leaps forward into the
signless unification of mind, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the
recognition of the sphere of nothingness are not present here. Whatever
disturbances there would be dependent on the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition are not present here. There is only
this much disturbance: the six fields of experience that depend on this very
body and are conditioned by life.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the recognition of the
sphere of nothingness.' They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of
the recognition of the sphere of neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition. There
is only this that is not empty: the six fields of experience that depend on
this very body and are conditioned by life.' So they regard it as empty of
whatever is not there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
and purely.

### The Release of the Heart

Then again, Ānanda, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of
nothingness, not attending to the recognition of the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition, a bhikkhu attends to the oneness that
depends on the signless unification of mind. Their heart leaps forward into the
signless unification of mind, becomes clear, settles, and commits to it.

They discern: 'This signless unification of mind too is constructed and
intended.' They discern: 'Whatever is constructed and intended is impermanent
and subject to cessation.'

As they know and see like this, the heart is released from the sensual outflow,
from the outflow of becoming, and from the outflow of ignorance. When it is
released, there is the knowledge: 'It is released.' They discern: 'Birth is
worn away, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been
done, there is no more of this state of being.'

They discern: 'Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on the sensual
outflow are not present here. Whatever disturbances there would be dependent on
the outflow of becoming are not present here. Whatever disturbances there would
be dependent on the outflow of ignorance are not present here. There is only
this much disturbance: the six fields of experience that depend on this very
body and are conditioned by life.'

They discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the sensual outflow.' They
discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the outflow of becoming.' They
discern: 'This field of recognition is empty of the outflow of ignorance. There
is only this that is not empty: the six fields of experience that depend on
this very body and are conditioned by life.' So they regard it as empty of
whatever is not there, and of what remains they discern: 'This is present.'

Ānanda, this too is how they come into emptiness genuinely, without distortion,
purely, and in the highest and unsurpassed way.

### The Highest Emptiness

Ānanda, whatever samaṇas or brahmins in the past entered and remained in the
pure, highest, unsurpassed emptiness, all of them entered and remained in this
same pure, highest, unsurpassed emptiness.

Whatever samaṇas or brahmins in the future will enter and remain in the pure,
highest, unsurpassed emptiness, all of them will enter and remain in this same
pure, highest, unsurpassed emptiness.

Whatever samaṇas or brahmins at present enter and remain in the pure, highest,
unsurpassed emptiness, all of them enter and remain in this same pure, highest,
unsurpassed emptiness.

So, Ānanda, you should train like this: 'We will enter and remain in the pure,
highest, unsurpassed emptiness.'"

That is what the Buddha said. Venerable Ānanda was glad and welcomed what the
Buddha had said.

The first discourse is finished.
