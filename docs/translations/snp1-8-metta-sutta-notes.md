# Snp 1.8 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[Snp 1.8: Metta Sutta](snp1-8-metta-sutta.md). The main translation is the
primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of Snp 1.8 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`snp1.8:0.1` to `snp1.8:10.5`), fetched from the upstream
  Bilara root.
- Control surfaces: MN 7 for `mettā` as `friendliness` and for boundless
  pervasion; Dhp 21-32 for the verse register; the `mettā`, `sati`, `sīla`,
  `diṭṭhi`, and `kāma` records.
- Working method: ten four-line verses, each translated as one stanza and
  kept line for line where English allows.

## Sutta Nipāta Framing

This is the repository's first Sutta Nipāta surface. These decisions hold for
later Sutta Nipāta discourses unless their notes record a reason to differ.

- **The surface is the discourse, and the label is the SuttaCentral number.**
  The label is `Snp 1.8`, which is also its SuttaCentral identifier
  (`snp1.8`). The Mahāsaṅgīti root titles it `Mettasutta`; the name
  `Karaṇīyamettā Sutta`, from its first word, is the one most readers know,
  and the Editorial Note gives both.
- **Verses are numbered within the discourse.** The Sutta Nipāta has running
  verse numbers in some editions, but the Bilara segments are numbered by
  verse within the discourse (`snp1.8:1` to `snp1.8:10`), and those are the
  numbers shown.
- **The close is kept.** `Mettasuttaṁ aṭṭhamaṁ` is rendered `The eighth
  discourse, on friendliness, is finished.`
- **Verse display** follows the Dhp 21-32 rule: one stanza per paragraph,
  one line per verse line, under a `Verses` heading.

## Governing Decisions

- `mettā` is `friendliness`, its governed default and MN 7's wording.
  `mānasaṁ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṁ` is `develop a boundless heart`; `aparimāṇa` is
  `boundless`, as in MN 7's pervasion.
- `karaṇīyamatthakusalena` is `this is what should be done by someone skilled
  in the good`. `attha` here is the good, or one's true welfare.
- `santaṁ padaṁ abhisamecca` is `who has understood the state of peace`: the
  commentarial reading is nibbāna, but the translation stays with the verse.
- `sukhino va khemino hontu ... bhavantu sukhitattā` is `may all be happy and
  secure; may all beings be happy at heart`. `sukha` is `happy` here, not the
  governed `satisfaction`: it is a wish for others' wellbeing, and
  `satisfied` would mislead. This is document-local, as in Dhp 179-196's
  verse 194.
- `tasā vā thāvarā` is `frail or firm`: those that tremble and those that
  stand firm.
- `bhūtā va sambhavesī va` is `those who have come to be and those still to
  come`.
- `byārosanā paṭighasañña` is `out of anger or resentment`. `paṭighasaññā` is
  literally `the recognition of aversion`; the translation keeps the sense
  and not the technical form.
- `mātā yathā niyaṁ puttam āyusā ekaputtamanurakkhe` is `as a mother would
  protect with her life her child, her only child`.
- `asambādhaṁ averamasapattaṁ` is `unobstructed, without hatred, without
  enmity`.
- `etaṁ satiṁ adhiṭṭheyya` is `resolve on this remembering`. `sati` keeps its
  governed `remembering`, and `adhiṭṭhāti` is `resolve on`.
- `brahmametaṁ vihāramidhamāhu` is `this, they say, is the divine way of
  living here`. The record `brahmavihāra` prefers `divine abiding`; the
  plain-English standard asks for `living` or `staying` over `abiding` in
  running English, and the verse describes a way of living rather than naming
  the four-part list, so the surface uses `the divine way of living`.
- `diṭṭhiñca anupaggamma` is `not taking up views`; `sīlavā dassanena
  sampanno` is `virtuous and accomplished in vision`.
- `na hi jātuggabbhaseyya punareti` is `they do not come again to lie in a
  womb`.

## Practice Clarifications

- The first three verses describe the kind of person who practices
  friendliness: easy to speak to, content, not greedy, careful of what the
  wise would criticize. The wish for others comes after the character.
- The heart of the discourse is the mother's protection of her only child,
  turned toward all beings without limit.
- The last verse turns the practice toward release: friendliness done with
  remembering, without clinging to views, leads beyond rebirth.
