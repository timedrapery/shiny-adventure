# Iti 44: Nibbānadhātu Sutta (The Two Nibbāna Elements)

## Editorial Note

  as a single Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:

  data.

  `nibbāna element`; `saupādisesā nibbānadhātu` is `the nibbāna element with
  remainder` and `anupādisesā nibbānadhātu` is `the nibbāna element without
  remainder`; `āsava` is rendered `outflow`; `khaya` is rendered `wearing
  away`; `rāga`, `dosa`, and `moha` are `passion`, `aversion`, and `delusion`;
  `indriya` is rendered `faculty`; `asaṅkhata` is rendered `unconditioned`;
  and `nirodha` is rendered `quenching`.
- This edition treats Iti 44 as the control passage for the two nibbāna
  elements, and for the distinction between the wearing away of passion,
  aversion, and delusion in a living arahant and the cooling of everything
  felt.
- This is the repository's first Itivuttaka surface, so it also sets the
  handling of the collection's framing formulas and of its closing verse.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's Iti 44
  translation surface.

## Translation

### The Two Elements

This was said by the Buddha, said by the arahant — so I heard:

"Bhikkhus, there are these two nibbāna elements. What two? The nibbāna element
with remainder, and the nibbāna element without remainder.

### The Element With Remainder

And what, bhikkhus, is the nibbāna element with remainder?

Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is an arahant, whose outflows are worn away, who has
lived the spiritual life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden,
reached the goal, worn away the fetters of becoming, and is rightly released
through final knowledge.

Their five sense faculties still stand, and because these are unimpaired they
still meet what is agreeable and what is disagreeable, and still feel the
pleasant and the painful.

It is their wearing away of passion, their wearing away of aversion, their
wearing away of delusion — this, bhikkhus, is called the nibbāna element with
remainder.

### The Element Without Remainder

And what, bhikkhus, is the nibbāna element without remainder?

Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is an arahant, whose outflows are worn away, who has
lived the spiritual life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden,
reached the goal, worn away the fetters of becoming, and is rightly released
through final knowledge.

For them, bhikkhus, right here, everything felt — being not relished — will
become cool.

This, bhikkhus, is called the nibbāna element without remainder.

These, bhikkhus, are the two nibbāna elements."

This is what the Buddha said. And in reference to this, it was said:

### Verses

"These two nibbāna elements were made plain
by the one with vision, independent, unshaken.

One element belongs to what is visible here and now:
with remainder, the conduit to becoming worn away.

The element without remainder belongs to what comes after,
in which every becoming quenches.

Those who know this unconditioned state,
whose heart is released and whose conduit to becoming is worn away,
have reached the heart of the Dhamma and delight in the wearing away.
They have given up every becoming."

This too was said by the Buddha — so I heard.

The seventh discourse is finished.
