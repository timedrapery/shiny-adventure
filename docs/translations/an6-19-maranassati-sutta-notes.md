# AN 6.19 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[AN 6.19: Maraṇassati Sutta](an6-19-maranassati-sutta.md). The main
translation is the primary study surface; this file records debated
translation choices, source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of AN 6.19 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`an6.19:1.1` to `an6.19:16.4`), fetched from the upstream
  Bilara root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: MN 118 for `bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti
  mahānisaṁsā`; Dhp 21-32 for `pamatta` and `appamatta`; MN 2 for
  `āsavānaṁ khayāya`; MN 122 for `sāsana` as `instruction`; MN 11 for the
  bhikkhus' reply `Bhadante`.
- Working method: six bhikkhus each say how they recollect death, each with a
  shorter span of time. The Buddha then repeats all six and sorts them into
  two groups. Every statement is kept whole, because the shrinking span is
  the teaching.

## Governing Decisions

- `maraṇassati` is `recollection of death`, its record. `sati` alone is
  `remembering` in this repository; in the compound the record's
  `recollection` is the established English and is kept.
- `bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṁsā` is `when ... developed
  and cultivated, it bears great fruit and great benefit`, as in MN 118.
- `amatogadhā amatapariyosānā` is `it plunges into the deathless and ends in
  the deathless`. `amata` keeps its governed `deathless`.
- `bhagavato sāsanaṁ manasi kareyyaṁ` is `I would attend to the Buddha's
  instruction`. `manasi karoti` is `attend to`, as in MN 122.
- `bahu vata me kataṁ assā` is `I would have done a great deal`.
- The six spans are `a night and a day` (`rattindivaṁ`), `a day`
  (`divasaṁ`), `as long as it takes to eat one meal of alms food`
  (`ekapiṇḍapātaṁ bhuñjāmi`), `as long as it takes to chew and swallow four or
  five mouthfuls`, `... one mouthful`, and `as long as it takes to breathe in
  after breathing out, or to breathe out after breathing in`.
- `pamattā viharanti` and `appamattā viharanti` are `live heedlessly` and
  `live heedfully`, following `appamāda` (`heedfulness`).
- `dandhaṁ` and `tikkhaṁ` are `slowly` and `keenly`. `tikkha` is literally
  `sharp`; `keenly` keeps the edge and reads as English.
- `āsavānaṁ khayāya` is `for the wearing away of the outflows`, following
  `āsavakkhaya`.
- `Nātike viharati giñjakāvasathe` is `staying at Nātika, in the brick
  house`. `giñjakāvasatha` is a guest house built of bricks.

## Re-audit Calls

- The source writes out every statement in the Buddha's sorting of the six
  answers (`an6.19:8.2` to `14.2`). It does not abbreviate, and neither does
  the translation.
- `Navamaṁ` is rendered `The ninth discourse is finished.`

## Practice Clarifications

- The first four bhikkhus are not wrong to recollect death. The Buddha calls
  them heedless because the span they give themselves is still long enough to
  put things off.
- The heedful two leave no room for putting off: a mouthful, or one breath.
  The recollection is not morbid; its aim is to attend to the instruction now,
  for the wearing away of the outflows.
- This is the practical side of Dhp 21: heedfulness is the path to the
  deathless.
