# MN 113: Sappurisa Sutta (The Good Person)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of MN 113, designed to be readable
  as a single Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [MN 113 Translation Notes](mn113-sappurisa-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of MN 113 as segmented in SuttaCentral's Bilara
  data. The passages the source abbreviates are written out in full.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `sappurisa` is `good person` and
  `asappurisa` `bad person`; `atammayatā` is `non-identification`; `maññati`
  is `thinks of`; the mental themes follow MN 19 and the formless spheres and
  cessation follow MN 26; `lobha`, `dosa`, and `moha` are `greed`,
  `aversion`, and `delusion`.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's MN 113
  translation surface.

## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery. There the Buddha addressed the bhikkhus:
"Bhikkhus."

"Bhadante," the bhikkhus replied. The Buddha said this:

"Bhikkhus, I will teach you the qualities of a good person and the qualities
of a bad person. Listen and pay close attention. I will speak."

"Yes, Bhante," the bhikkhus replied. The Buddha said this:

### Family

"And what, bhikkhus, are the qualities of a bad person? Here, bhikkhus, a bad
person has gone forth from a high family. They reflect: 'I have gone forth from
a high family, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of their high
family, they praise themselves and put others down. This, bhikkhus, is a
quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of a high family that qualities
of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone has not gone
forth from a high family, if they practice in line with the Dhamma, practice
properly, and live in line with the Dhamma, they are honored and praised for
that.' Putting the practice first, they do not praise themselves or put others
down because of their high family. This, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good
person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person has gone forth from a great family. They
reflect: 'I have gone forth from a great family, but these other bhikkhus have
not.' Because of their great family, they praise themselves and put others
down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of a great family that qualities
of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone has not gone
forth from a great family, if they practice in line with the Dhamma, practice
properly, and live in line with the Dhamma, they are honored and praised for
that.' Putting the practice first, they do not praise themselves or put others
down because of their great family. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good
person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person has gone forth from a very wealthy family.
They reflect: 'I have gone forth from a very wealthy family, but these other
bhikkhus have not.' Because of their very wealthy family, they praise
themselves and put others down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad
person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of a very wealthy family that
qualities of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone has
not gone forth from a very wealthy family, if they practice in line with the
Dhamma, practice properly, and live in line with the Dhamma, they are honored
and praised for that.' Putting the practice first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of their very wealthy family. This too,
bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person has gone forth from an eminently wealthy
family. They reflect: 'I have gone forth from an eminently wealthy family, but
these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of their eminently wealthy family, they
praise themselves and put others down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a
bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of an eminently wealthy family
that qualities of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone
has not gone forth from an eminently wealthy family, if they practice in line
with the Dhamma, practice properly, and live in line with the Dhamma, they are
honored and praised for that.' Putting the practice first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of their eminently wealthy family. This
too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

### Reputation and Gains

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person is well known and famous. They reflect: 'I
am well known and famous, but these other bhikkhus are unknown and of little
account.' Because of being well known, they praise themselves and put others
down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of being well known that
qualities of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone is
not well known and famous, if they practice in line with the Dhamma, practice
properly, and live in line with the Dhamma, they are honored and praised for
that.' Putting the practice first, they do not praise themselves or put others
down because of being well known. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good
person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person receives robes, alms food, lodgings, and
medicines and supplies for the sick. They reflect: 'I receive robes, alms food,
lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick, but these other bhikkhus do
not.' Because of what they receive, they praise themselves and put others down.
This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'It is not because of what someone receives that
qualities of greed, aversion, or delusion come to an end. Even if someone does

sick, if they practice in line with the Dhamma, practice properly, and live in
line with the Dhamma, they are honored and praised for that.' Putting the
practice first, they do not praise themselves or put others down because of
what they receive. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

### Learning and Skill

### Mental Themes and Formless Spheres

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, quite secluded from sensuality, secluded
from unwholesome dhammas, enters and remains in the first mental theme, with
thinking and pondering, with rejoicing and satisfaction born of seclusion. They
reflect: 'I have attained the first mental theme, but these other bhikkhus have
not.' Because of attaining the first mental theme, they praise themselves and
put others down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the first mental theme. For whatever they think it is,
it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of attaining the first mental theme. This
too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, with the subsiding of thinking and
pondering, with inner clarity and singleness of mind, enters and remains in the
second mental theme, without thinking and pondering, with rejoicing and
satisfaction born of unification. They reflect: 'I have attained the second
mental theme, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of attaining the
second mental theme, they praise themselves and put others down. This too,
bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the second mental theme. For whatever they think it is,
it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of attaining the second mental theme.
This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, with the fading of rejoicing, remains in
dynamic balance, remembering and clearly knowing, experiences ease in the body,
and enters and remains in the third mental theme, of which the noble ones say:
"One of dynamic balance and remembering stays at ease". They reflect: 'I have
attained the third mental theme, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of
attaining the third mental theme, they praise themselves and put others down.
This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the third mental theme. For whatever they think it is,
it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of attaining the third mental theme. This
too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, with the abandoning of ease and
discomfort, and with the earlier ending of gladness and sadness, enters and
remains in the fourth mental theme, without discomfort and without ease,
purified by dynamic balance and remembering. They reflect: 'I have attained the
fourth mental theme, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of attaining
the fourth mental theme, they praise themselves and put others down. This too,
bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the fourth mental theme. For whatever they think it is,
it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not praise
themselves or put others down because of attaining the fourth mental theme.
This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, with the complete transcending of
recognitions of form, with the passing away of recognitions of resistance, with
non-attention to recognitions of diversity, aware that "space is boundless,"
enters and remains in the sphere of boundless space. They reflect: 'I have
attained the sphere of boundless space, but these other bhikkhus have not.'
Because of attaining the sphere of boundless space, they praise themselves and
put others down. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the sphere of boundless space. For whatever they think
it is, it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not
praise themselves or put others down because of attaining the sphere of
boundless space. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, going completely beyond the sphere of
boundless space, aware that "knowing is boundless," enters and remains in the
sphere of boundless knowing. They reflect: 'I have attained the sphere of
boundless knowing, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of attaining the
sphere of boundless knowing, they praise themselves and put others down. This
too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even
with the attainment of the sphere of boundless knowing. For whatever they think
it is, it turns out otherwise.' Putting non-identification first, they do not
praise themselves or put others down because of attaining the sphere of
boundless knowing. This too, bhikkhus, is a quality of a good person.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, going completely beyond the sphere of
boundless knowing, aware that "there is nothing at all," enters and remains in
the sphere of nothingness. They reflect: 'I have attained the sphere of
nothingness, but these other bhikkhus have not.' Because of attaining the
sphere of nothingness, they praise themselves and put others down. This too,
bhikkhus, is a quality of a bad person.

But a good person reflects: 'The Buddha has spoken of non-identification even

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bad person, going completely beyond the sphere of

### Beyond All Conceiving

Furthermore, bhikkhus, a good person, going completely beyond the sphere of
neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition, enters and remains in the cessation of
recognition and felt experience. When they see this with discernment, their
outflows are exhausted.

This bhikkhu does not think of anything, anywhere, or in any way."

That is what the Buddha said. The bhikkhus were glad and welcomed what the
Buddha had said.

The third discourse is finished.
