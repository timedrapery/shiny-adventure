# Iti 49 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[Iti 49: Diṭṭhigata Sutta](iti49-ditthigata-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `iti49:1.1`–`iti49:8.2`, stored locally in
  `.bilara-cache/iti49_root-pli-ms.json`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/iti49/pli/ms>.
- The source has about 173 Pali words after collection and chapter headings
  are excluded. The chapter summary after `iti49:8.2` is not part of the
  discourse.
- The title `Diṭṭhigatasutta` means "The Discourse on What Is Bound Up with
  Views." The reader title foregrounds the active takeover described in the
  first sentence.

## Term-Family Inventory

- `diṭṭhigata`: a view that has been taken up; rendered simply as `view`
- `pariyuṭṭhita`: actively overtaken; rendered `taken over`
- `bhava` and `vibhava`: `becoming` and `non-becoming`
- `citta`: `heart`, the affective or feeling mind
- `bhūta`: `what has come to be` in this process-oriented context
- `nibbidā`, `virāga`, and `nirodha`: `disenchantment`, `fading`, and
  `quenching`
- `bhavataṇhā`: `ignorant wanting for becoming`
- `parikkhaya`: `completely worn away`

The passage depends on keeping several endings distinct. `Vibhava` names the
non-becoming welcomed by the annihilationist view. It is not silently equated
with `nirodha`, the quenching toward which the practice is directed, or with
`parikkhaya`, the complete wearing away of ignorant wanting.

## Governing Decisions

- **`pariyuṭṭhitā` is `taken over`.** The major record keeps
  `manifestation` as the noun's source-facing default, while explicitly
  allowing `taking over` when the overtaking force matters. Here the verb
  describes people in the grip of two views, so `taken over` is the clearest
  ordinary English.
- **`olīyanti` and `atidhāvanti` are `hold back` and `go too far`.** The
  source contrasts falling short with running beyond the mark. The headings
  repeat those verbs so the structure stays audible without technical labels.
- **The first extreme is attachment to becoming.** `Bhavārāmā`, `bhavaratā`,
  and `bhavasammuditā` become `delight in`, `enjoy`, and `celebrate`
  becoming. The three verbs are retained because the source emphasizes how
  thoroughly becoming is welcomed.
- **The heart's four refusals remain four movements.** When the Dhamma for
  quenching becoming is taught, the heart does not incline, become confident,
  settle, or commit. `Does not take to it, grow confident in it, settle on it,
  or commit to it` keeps the progression in speakable English.
- **The second extreme remains a quoted view.** The claim that the self is
  destroyed after death is put in the mouth of those welcoming non-becoming.
  The translation does not turn that claim into the Buddha's position or use
  `quenching` for their hoped-for annihilation.
- **`bhūtaṁ bhūtato` is process language.** `What has come to be simply as
  what has come to be` follows the governed `bhūta` alternate and avoids
  making the line assert a permanent metaphysical `being`.
- **The three-part practice sequence stays distinct.** `Nibbidā`, `virāga`,
  and `nirodha` are respectively disenchantment, fading, and quenching. None
  is flattened into a generic `ending`.
- **The verse shortens `bhavataṇhā` only once.** Its first occurrence uses the
  governed `ignorant wanting for becoming`. The next line's
  `bhavābhave vītataṇho` is rendered `free of wanting for becoming or
  non-becoming`, which makes the two-sided freedom audible without repeating
  a long doctrinal label.
- **`bhūtassa vibhavā` is `when what has come to be is gone`.** This line
  preserves the verse's play between coming-to-be and being gone. The notes,
  rather than an overloaded verse line, record why this is not a synonym for
  annihilating a self.

## Itivuttaka Framing

The opening, prose-to-verse bridge, closing, and discourse number follow the
Iti 44 control. The em dash is closed up in the generated reading style, and
the chapter summary after the twelfth discourse is excluded.

## Reader Placement

Iti 49 follows SN 12.15 in Stage 4. SN 12.15 identifies the middle between
`it exists` and `it does not`; this discourse shows how those two positions
feel from the inside. One clings to becoming, the other mistakes destruction
for peace, and the alternative is to see what has come to be without turning
it into a permanent self.
