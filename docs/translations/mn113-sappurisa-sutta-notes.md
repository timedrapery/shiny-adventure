# MN 113 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 113: Sappurisa Sutta](mn113-sappurisa-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 113 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`mn113:1.1` to `mn113:29.5`), fetched from the upstream Bilara
  root and matching its hash in `reviews/source-pins.json`.
- Control surfaces: MN 19 for the four mental themes; MN 26 for the formless
  spheres and the cessation of recognition and felt experience; MN 1 for
  `maññati`; MN 122 for the secluded lodgings; MN 11 and MN 117 for the
  setting.
- Working method: one pattern applied to twenty-six grounds for pride. For
  each, the bad person praises themselves and puts others down, and the good
  person sees that the ground does not end greed, aversion, or delusion, or,
  for the meditative attainments, that the Buddha taught non-identification
  even with them. The source abbreviates two families, five ascetic practices,
  and the second to fourth mental themes (`mn113:4-6`, `16-20`, `22-24`); the
  study edition writes every one out.

## Governing Decisions

- `sappurisa` is `good person` and `asappurisa` `bad person`, their records.
  `sappurisadhamma` and `asappurisadhamma` are `the qualities of a good
  person` and `the qualities of a bad person`; each instance is `a quality`.
- `attānukkaṁseti paraṁ vambheti` is `they praise themselves and put others
  down`.
- `lobhadhammā dosadhammā mohadhammā` is `qualities of greed, aversion, or
  delusion`, and `parikkhayaṁ gacchanti` is `come to an end`.
- `dhammānudhammappaṭipanno sāmīcippaṭipanno anudhammacārī` is `practice in
  line with the Dhamma, practice properly, and live in line with the Dhamma`;
  `pujjo pāsaṁso` is `honored and praised`.
- `paṭipadaṁyeva antaraṁ karitvā` is `putting the practice first`, and
  `atammayataññeva antaraṁ karitvā` is `putting non-identification first`.
  `atammayatā` keeps its governed `non-identification`.
- `yena yena hi maññanti tato taṁ hoti aññathā` is `for whatever they think it
  is, it turns out otherwise`. `maññati` keeps its governed `thinks of`.
- The four families are `a high family` (`uccākula`), `a great family`
  (`mahākula`), `a very wealthy family` (`mahābhogakula`), and `an eminently
  wealthy family` (`uḷārabhogakula`).
- `ñāto yasassī` is `well known and famous`; `appaññātā appesakkhā` is
  `unknown and of little account`.
- The four requisites are `robes, alms food, lodgings, and medicines and
  supplies for the sick`.
- The ascetic practices are given in plain English: `lives in the forest`
  (`āraññika`), `wears rag robes` (`paṁsukūlika`), `eats only alms food`
  (`piṇḍapātika`), `lives at the root of a tree` (`rukkhamūlika`), `lives in a
  charnel ground` (`sosānika`), `lives in the open air` (`abbhokāsika`),
  `never lies down` (`nesajjika`), `sleeps wherever a bed is set out`
  (`yathāsanthatika`), and `eats in one sitting` (`ekāsanika`). These are
  later counted among the `dhutaṅga` practices; the discourse does not use
  that word.
- The mental themes follow MN 19 and the formless spheres and cessation follow
  MN 26, with `paṭhamajjhānasamāpatti` and the rest rendered `attaining the
  first mental theme` and so on.
- `na kiñci maññati, na kuhiñci maññati, na kenaci maññati` is `does not think
  of anything, anywhere, or in any way`.

## Re-audit Calls

- The last case changes subject: it is the good person (`sappuriso`), not the
  bad person, who enters the cessation of recognition and felt experience, and
  the passage ends with `this bhikkhu`. The translation keeps that shift.
- `Sappurisasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ tatiyaṁ` is rendered `The third discourse is
  finished.`

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse does not criticize family, learning, ascetic practice, or
  meditation. It criticizes using them to rank yourself above others.
- From the mental themes on, the good person's answer changes. It is no
  longer that the attainment does not end greed, aversion, and delusion; it
  is that the Buddha taught non-identification even with these. The highest
  attainment ends in not thinking of anything at all as self.
