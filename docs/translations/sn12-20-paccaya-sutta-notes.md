# SN 12.20 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[SN 12.20: Paccaya Sutta](sn12-20-paccaya-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `sn12.20:1.1`–`sn12.20:5.9`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/sn12.20/pli/ms>.
- Source boundary: the discourse ends at `sn12.20:5.9`. The segments after it
  are collection apparatus, not discourse content: `5.10` (`Dasamaṁ`) marks
  the tenth discourse, `5.11` closes the Āhāravagga, and `6.0`–`6.6` are the
  vagga's mnemonic summary verse covering all ten of its discourses. None of
  that is translated here. The closing line `The tenth discourse is finished.`
  renders `Dasamaṁ` only, matching how other surfaces in this repository close.
- The discourse has about 355 Pali words. A large part of its structure is
  carried by peyyāla abbreviation.
- Control surfaces: the link-by-link definitions in SN 12.2, the
  `idappaccayatā` handling in MN 26 and DN 15, the peyyāla method established
  in SN 12.44, and the past-and-future questions in MN 131.

## Governing Decisions

- **The discourse's whole point is a distinction, and the English has to keep
  it.** `paṭiccasamuppāda` is the pattern; `paṭiccasamuppannā dhammā` are the
  items the pattern runs through. Rendering the second as `dependent arising`
  again, or as an abstract plural like `phenomena`, would collapse exactly
  what the text separates. It is rendered `the things that arise dependently`,
  and the predicate `paṭiccasamuppanna` inside the list stays `dependently
  arisen`, matching the governed example already recorded on `saṅkhata`.
- **`dhammā` here is `things`, not `dhamma`.** This is a context-specific
  rendering of a governed term, of the same kind as SN 12.44's sense-object
  `ideas`. The plural here means the items just enumerated—ageing and dying,
  birth, becoming, and the rest—not the teaching and not mental objects.
- **The twelve links use the governed sequence unchanged**, so this surface
  reads identically to SN 12.2, SN 12.44, and DN 15 at every link.
- **`idappaccayatā` is `conditionality`**, following the major entry and the
  MN 26 pairing, where conditionality names the structural principle and
  dependent arising names its application.
- **`dhammaṭṭhitatā` and `dhammaniyāmatā` are rendered as a pair**: `the
  stability of the pattern` and `the regularity of the pattern`. The first
  follows the default on `dhammatthiti`. The second takes the sanctioned
  alternate on `dhammaniyāmatā`—`regularity of the pattern` rather than the
  headword default `regularity of dhamma`—because the two terms occur here as
  a matched pair in one breath, and pairing them keeps the parallel audible in
  running English. Both entries record this alternate as available when
  smoother English is needed.
- **`ṭhitāva sā dhātu` is `that element still stands`.** `dhātu` keeps its
  governed `element`. The sentence is the anti-drift crux of the discourse: it
  says the pattern is found, not authored. The English deliberately avoids
  `law`, `truth`, and `principle`, all of which would import either legislation
  or a standing metaphysical object.
- **`tathatā avitathatā anaññathatā` is rendered as one plain sequence**: `the
  suchness of it, that it is not otherwise, that it does not become something
  else`. `tathatā` keeps its governed `suchness`. The two negative members are
  given as clauses rather than as the coined nouns `not-unsuchness` and
  `not-otherwiseness`, which are usable as a translator-facing gloss but fail
  the read-aloud test in running text. The controlled explanatory phrase `just
  like that` is deliberately not used here; the `tathatā` entry restricts it to
  explanation and forbids it as a running-text default.
- **The seven verbs of teaching are kept as seven.** `ācikkhati deseti
  paññāpeti paṭṭhapeti vivarati vibhajati uttānīkaroti` becomes `tells it,
  teaches it, makes it known, sets it out, reveals it, breaks it down, and
  makes it plain`. The pile-up is the source's own emphasis and is not
  compressed.
- **The absolutive `abhisambujjhitvā abhisametvā` is `Once he has`.** Repeating
  the two verbs immediately after they were just used, as a `Having awakened to
  it and broken through to it` opener, is Pali sentence shape rather than
  English, and the plain-English standard's second rule rejects it. The
  compression loses nothing: the reader has read both verbs in the previous
  sentence.
- **`netaṁ ṭhānaṁ vijjati` is `that is impossible`**, matching MN 99. The
  three question-sets are held inside one sentence closed by that negation, as
  the Pali does, rather than being negated three separate times.
- **`yathābhūtaṁ sammappaññāya sudiṭṭhā` is `has clearly seen, with right
  discernment as they have come to be`.** `paññā` keeps its governed
  `discernment`, and the established `as they have come to be` phrasing
  follows MN 137.
- **The noble disciple takes `they`**, as on every other surface in this
  repository that renders `ariyasāvaka`.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source abbreviates twice, and the two cases are not the same shape.

In the dependent-arising half, it prints the whole frame for `jāti` →
`jarāmaraṇa`, abbreviates the ten links between, and then prints the whole
frame again for `avijjā` → `saṅkhārā`. This edition keeps both full statements
exactly where the source puts them, and names each of the eleven remaining
conditional statements once in between. Nothing represented by the peyyāla is
dropped, and the reader is not asked to work through the same paragraph twelve
times.

In the dependently-arisen half, the source gives the full sevenfold predicate
for `jarāmaraṇa`, `jāti`, and `bhava`, abbreviates through `saṅkhārā`, and
restates it in full for `avijjā`. This edition states the predicate once,
names all twelve items, and then applies the predicate to the whole list. The
list order follows the source, which runs from `jarāmaraṇa` back to `avijjā`.

No link is added, removed, or reordered in either half.

## Source-Audit Findings

Checking this discourse against the records that already cite it turned up two
citation problems. Both are repaired in this packet.

- **`dhammatā` did not occur in SN 12.20.** The record cited the bare word
  `dhammatā` to this discourse, but the discourse contains `dhammaṭṭhitatā`
  and `dhammaniyāmatā` and never `dhammatā` itself. The strict verifier scored
  it `inflected` rather than `absent`, because the stem is present inside those
  two longer words, and `inflected` is documented as usually not an error. This
  is the third citation of this exact shape found in the project, after
  `ariyapuggala` at SN 55.30 and `upasamānussati` at AN 11.12. The citation is
  moved to DN 14, where `Ayamettha dhammatā` occurs nineteen times in running
  text.
- **`dhammatthiti` cited a form that is not in the text either.** Its example
  read `dhammatthitā`, which is neither the headword nor the source form, and
  passed only as a prefix of the real word. It now cites the exact running-text
  form `dhammaṭṭhitatā`.

The general lesson is the one already recorded for the Wave 7 citation pass: a
`verify_example_sources.py` pass proves that a string occurs somewhere, not
that the governed term occurs. Read the segment.

## Practice Clarifications

- `Whether Tathāgatas arise or not` is not a claim about cosmic ages. It is
  the reason the teaching can be tested rather than believed: the conditioning
  the discourse describes is supposed to be observable now, whoever is or is
  not around to point it out.
- The second half prevents the first half from hardening into a metaphysics.
  The pattern is stable; every single item it runs through—including
  ignorance, and including knowing—is impermanent and bound to wear away,
  pass away, fade, and quench. There is no permanent item anywhere in the
  sequence, and the stability of the pattern is not a thing standing behind
  the sequence.
- The closing section is a practical result, not a rule against remembering.
  The questions listed are all identity questions—what was I, what will I be,
  what am I. Someone who has actually seen experience arising by conditions
  has nothing left for those questions to be about. Compare MN 131, which
  reaches a similar place from a different direction.

## Wave 10 Role

SN 12.20 is the second Wave 10 queue item. It was ranked for five orphan
signals and anchors four of them—`dhammatthiti`, `dhammaniyāmatā`, `tathatā`,
and `dharma`—in running text. The fifth, `dhammatā`, turned out not to be in
this discourse at all; its repaired citation points at DN 14, which is not a
translated surface, so that record stays orphaned. Corpus orphans went from
101 to 97.

Beyond the lexicon, the discourse supplies something the reader path was
missing: a direct statement of what dependent arising *is*, and of how the
pattern differs from the things it runs through, placed after SN 12.2 has
already defined each link.
