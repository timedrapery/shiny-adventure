# SN 1.1 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[SN 1.1: Oghataraṇa Sutta](sn1-1-oghatara-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `sn1.1:1.1`–`sn1.1:3.4`, stored in
  `.bilara-cache/sn1.1_root-pli-ms.json`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/sn1.1/pli/ms>.
- Comparison reference: Bhikkhu Sujato's published English translation in
  SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The source contains 97 Pali words.
- The title `Oghataraṇasutta` means "The Discourse on Crossing the Flood."

## Governing Decisions

- `ogha` is `flood` and `oghaṃ atari` is `crossed over the flood`. The text
  depends on the physical image—sinking, being swept away, and crossing—so it
  is not flattened into generic language about escaping suffering.
- `tarati` and `tiṇṇa` stay distinct. The dialogue uses the crossing verb;
  the closing verse uses the completed form for having crossed over the
  world's entanglement.
- `appatiṭṭha` can suggest not standing, not stopping, or finding no fixed
  foothold. `Without standing still` is selected because the explanation
  immediately says, `When I stood still, I sank.`
- `anāyūha` can suggest not striving, forcing, struggling, or swimming
  forward. `Without struggling` keeps the practical tension audible, and the
  explanation immediately says, `When I struggled, I was swept away.`
- Together, those words do not recommend passivity. The passage rules out
  both freezing in place and forceful struggle. It leaves their relationship
  as a paradox rather than supplying a technical method not stated in the
  source.
- `mārisa` and `āvuso` are both rendered with ordinary friend-language in
  this short exchange. English has no natural way to preserve the difference
  between the deity's respectful address and the Buddha's familiar response
  without making the dialogue sound ceremonial.
- `brāhmaṇa` is `brahmin`. Here it is an ideal title for the Buddha as one
  who has completed the path, not a reference to hereditary caste.
- `parinibbutaṃ` is `has reached complete quenching`. The deity is looking at
  and speaking with the living Buddha, so the participle cannot name final
  nibbāna at death. This extends the present-life context rule already made
  explicit by AN 3.69.
- `tiṇṇaṃ loke visattikaṃ` is `they have crossed over the world's
  entanglement`, matching the governed `visattikā` phrase already used in MN
  26. The sticky entanglement is what has been crossed, not another name for
  the flood itself.
- The source does not define the flood in this discourse. The newcomer
  introduction therefore describes its function—something that overwhelms
  and carries a person along—without reducing it to one later doctrinal list.
- `padakkhiṇaṃ katvā` is `circled him clockwise`, a concise description of
  the ancient gesture of respect before the deity vanishes.
