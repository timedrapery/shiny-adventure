# MN 61: Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovāda Sutta

## Editorial Note

  as a single Markdown document.

  [MN 61 Translation Notes](mn61-ambalatthikarahulovada-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of MN 61 as segmented in SuttaCentral's Bilara
  data. The title names the setting (Ambalaṭṭhikā) and the addressee
  (Rāhula, the Buddha's son) together: the advice given to Rāhula at
  Ambalaṭṭhikā.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `kamma` is rendered `action`
  (`kāyakamma` / `vacīkamma` / `manokamma` = `bodily action` / `verbal
  action` / `mental action`); `musāvāda` is rendered `false speech`;
  `dukkha` is rendered `dissatisfaction`; `kusala` / `akusala` are rendered
  `wholesome` / `unwholesome`; `Rāhula` remains untranslated as a proper
  name.
- This edition treats MN 61 as the repository's control surface for the
  threefold (before / during / after) reflection formula on bodily, verbal,
  and mental action, and for the water-vessel and war-elephant similes on
  the gravity of deliberate lying.

## Translation

### The Setting

So I have heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha, in
the Bamboo Grove, at the Squirrels' Feeding Ground. Now at that time
Venerable Rāhula was staying at Ambalaṭṭhikā.

Then, when it was evening, the Buddha rose from seclusion and went to
Ambalaṭṭhikā, to where Venerable Rāhula was. Venerable Rāhula saw the
Buddha coming in the distance and prepared a seat and water for washing
the feet. The Buddha sat down on the seat prepared for him and washed
his feet. Venerable Rāhula bowed to the Buddha and sat down to one side.

### The Water Vessel

Then the Buddha, leaving a little water in the water vessel, said to
Venerable Rāhula: "Rāhula, do you see this little water left in the water
vessel?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"So little, Rāhula, is the contemplation of those who feel no shame in
speaking a deliberate lie."

Then the Buddha threw away that little water and said to Venerable
Rāhula: "Rāhula, do you see this little water that has been thrown away?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"Rāhula, whoever feels no shame in speaking a deliberate lie throws away
their contemplation just like that."

Then the Buddha turned that water vessel upside down and said to
Venerable Rāhula: "Rāhula, do you see this water vessel turned upside down?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"Rāhula, whoever feels no shame in speaking a deliberate lie turns their
contemplation upside down just like that."

Then the Buddha set that water vessel right side up and said to
Venerable Rāhula: "Rāhula, do you see this water vessel, empty and void?"

"Yes, Bhante."

"Rāhula, whoever feels no shame in speaking a deliberate lie has a
contemplation that is empty and void just like that.

### The War Elephant

Suppose, Rāhula, there were a royal war elephant, with tusks like
chariot-poles, fully grown, of good breed, accustomed to battle. Going into
battle, it would use its forefeet and its hindfeet, its forequarters and its
hindquarters, its head and its ears and its tusks and its tail, but it would
guard its trunk. Then the elephant trainer would think: 'This royal war
elephant has not yet given up its life for the king.' But when that royal
war elephant, going into battle, uses its forefeet and its hindfeet, its
forequarters and its hindquarters, its head and its ears and its tusks and
its tail, and its trunk as well — then the elephant trainer thinks: 'This
royal war elephant has now given up its life for the king. There is nothing
it will not do.'

In the same way, Rāhula, for whoever feels no shame in speaking a deliberate
lie, there is no evil, I say, that they would not do. Therefore, Rāhula, you
should train yourself: 'I will not speak a deliberate lie, even as a joke.'

### The Mirror

What do you think, Rāhula — what is a mirror for?"

"For reflection, Bhante."

"In the same way, Rāhula, a bodily action, a verbal action, or a mental
action should be done only after repeated reflection.

### Reflecting On Bodily Action

Whenever you want to do a bodily action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it:
'This bodily action I want to do — would it lead to my own affliction, or to
the affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Is this an
unwholesome bodily action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome,
dissatisfaction as its result?' If, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily
action I want to do would lead to my own affliction, or to the affliction of
others, or to the affliction of both; it is an unwholesome bodily action,
with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — then a
bodily action of this kind is certainly not to be done.

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily action I want to do would not
lead to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the
affliction of both; it is a wholesome bodily action, with ease as its
outcome, ease as its result' — then a bodily action of this kind may be
done.

While you are doing a bodily action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This
bodily action I am doing — is it leading to my own affliction, or to the
affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Is this an unwholesome
bodily action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its
result?' If, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily action I am doing is
leading to my own affliction, or to the affliction of others, or to the
affliction of both; it is an unwholesome bodily action, with dissatisfaction
as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — you should suspend that
bodily action.

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily action I am doing is not
leading to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the
affliction of both; it is a wholesome bodily action, with ease as its
outcome, ease as its result' — you may continue with that bodily action.

After you have done a bodily action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This
bodily action I have done — did it lead to my own affliction, or to the
affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Was this an unwholesome
bodily action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its
result?' If, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily action I have done led
to my own affliction, or to the affliction of others, or to the affliction of
both; it was an unwholesome bodily action, with dissatisfaction as its
outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — then you should confess it, reveal
it, lay it open to the Teacher or to a wise companion in the spiritual life,
and undertake restraint in the future.

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This bodily action I have done did not
lead to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the
affliction of both; it was a wholesome bodily action, with ease as its
outcome, ease as its result' — then you may live with joy and gladness,
training day and night in wholesome qualities.

### Reflecting On Verbal Action

While you are doing a verbal action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This
verbal action I am doing — is it leading to my own affliction, or to the

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This verbal action I am doing is not

After you have done a verbal action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This

### Reflecting On Mental Action

Whenever you want to do a mental action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it:
'This mental action I want to do — would it lead to my own affliction, or to
the affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Is this an
unwholesome mental action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome,
dissatisfaction as its result?' If, upon reflecting, you know: 'This mental
action I want to do would lead to my own affliction, or to the affliction of
others, or to the affliction of both; it is an unwholesome mental action,
with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — then a
mental action of this kind is certainly not to be done.

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This mental action I want to do would not
lead to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the

outcome, ease as its result' — then a mental action of this kind may be

While you are doing a mental action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This
mental action I am doing — is it leading to my own affliction, or to the
affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Is this an unwholesome
mental action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its
result?' If, upon reflecting, you know: 'This mental action I am doing is
leading to my own affliction, or to the affliction of others, or to the
affliction of both; it is an unwholesome mental action, with dissatisfaction
as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — you should suspend that

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This mental action I am doing is not

outcome, ease as its result' — you may continue with that mental action.

After you have done a mental action, Rāhula, you should reflect on it: 'This
mental action I have done — did it lead to my own affliction, or to the
affliction of others, or to the affliction of both? Was this an unwholesome
mental action, with dissatisfaction as its outcome, dissatisfaction as its

to my own affliction, or to the affliction of others, or to the affliction of
both; it was an unwholesome mental action, with dissatisfaction as its
outcome, dissatisfaction as its result' — then you should feel disturbed,
ashamed, and disgusted by it, and you should guard against it in the future.

But if, upon reflecting, you know: 'This mental action I have done did not
lead to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the
affliction of both; it was a wholesome mental action, with ease as its
outcome, ease as its result' — then you may live with joy and gladness,
training day and night in wholesome qualities.

### The Purification Of Every Contemplative And Brahmin

Rāhula, whatever contemplatives or brahmins in the past purified their
bodily action, their verbal action, and their mental action, all of them did
so by repeatedly reflecting in just this way. Whatever contemplatives or
brahmins in the future will purify their bodily action, their verbal action,
and their mental action, all of them will do so by repeatedly reflecting in
just this way. Whatever contemplatives or brahmins in the present are
purifying their bodily action, their verbal action, and their mental action,
all of them are doing so by repeatedly reflecting in just this way.

Therefore, Rāhula, you should train yourself: 'I will purify my bodily
action by repeatedly reflecting on it. I will purify my verbal action by
repeatedly reflecting on it. I will purify my mental action by repeatedly
reflecting on it.' It is in this way, Rāhula, that you should train
yourself."

That is what the Buddha said. Venerable Rāhula was satisfied and
welcomed what the Buddha had said.

The first discourse is finished.
