# SN 12.61 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[SN 12.61: Assutavā Sutta](sn12-61-assutava-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of SN 12.61 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (33 segments).
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially `citta`,
  `mano`, `viññāṇa`, `puthujjana`, `nibbidā`, `virāga`, `vimutti`, and the four
  conditionality formula records, together with the governed dependent-arising
  chain in SN 12.2 and the governed disenchantment passage in SN 22.59.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as one argument with a
  counter-intuitive shape — the body is the safer thing to identify with, not
  the mind — followed by the standard remedy.

## Why This Surface Was Needed

The Wave 6 audit ranked this discourse for the highest formula density in the
queue: four formula records for the conditionality lines, plus the `paccaya`
major. Those four records existed but no translation surface demonstrated them
in running text.

They are now used verbatim:

- `imasmiṁ sati idaṁ hoti` — when this is, this comes to be
- `imassuppādā idaṁ uppajjati` — from the arising of this, this arises
- `imasmiṁ asati idaṁ na hoti` — when this is not, this does not come to be
- `imassa nirodhā idaṁ nirujjhati` — with the quenching of this, this ceases

## Governing Decisions

- `cittaṁ itipi, mano itipi, viññāṇaṁ itipi` is rendered `what is called the
  feeling mind, and the thinking mind, and knowing`. The repository governs the
  three terms separately as `feeling mind`, `thinking mind`, and `knowing`, and
  keeping them distinct is what makes the sentence work: the discourse is
  stringing together three names for the same thing in order to say that none
  of them is a stable object.
- `assutavā` and `sutavā` are rendered `unlearned` and `learned`, matching the
  corpus norm. Both are ungoverned; see the standing note in
  [plain-english-rollout-plan.md](../plain-english-rollout-plan.md).
- `ācaya` and `apacaya` are rendered `the building up and the wearing down`,
  and `ādāna` and `nikkhepana` as `its being taken up and its being put down`.
  All four are ungoverned. Plain verb phrases were preferred to the noun forms
  under rule 6 of the plain-English standard.
- `ajjhosita mamāyita parāmaṭṭha` is rendered `held to, taken as mine, and
  grasped`. Ungoverned. The three are kept distinct because the sentence is
  building to the governed not-self refrain, and collapsing them would blunt
  the run-up.
- `etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā` uses the governed refrain `this is
  mine, I am this, this is my self` verbatim.
- `varaṁ ... upagaccheyya, na tveva cittaṁ` is rendered `it would be better ...
  to take this body as self than to take the mind as self`. The comparison is
  the point of the section and a literal `rather than the mind` would lose the
  verb.
- The closing disenchantment passage reuses the governed SN 22.59 wording.

## Editorial Presentation

- The source abridges the dependent-arising chain with `…pe…` at both the
  arising and the quenching end. This edition expands both from SN 12.2,
  following the call recorded in the MN 64 notes and reused in SN 12.11.
- The translation is divided into five sections: `The Setting`, `The Body Is
  the Easier Thing to Let Go Of`, `The Monkey`, `How the Learned Disciple
  Attends`, and `Disenchantment, Fading, and Release`.

## Re-audit Calls

- The Wave 6 roadmap listed `idappaccayatā` among this discourse's leverage
  signals. It is not here: the word does not occur in SN 12.61 at all. The
  four-line formula does occur, and that is what this surface anchors. The
  roadmap has been corrected.
- `ācaya`, `apacaya`, `ādāna`, `nikkhepana`, `ajjhosita`, `mamāyita`, and
  `parāmaṭṭha` are all ungoverned and all recur elsewhere. The
  hold-to / take-as-mine / grasp triple in particular is a strong candidate for
  a formula record, since it is the standard run-up to the not-self refrain.
- `assutavā` and `sutavā` remain ungoverned across the whole corpus. This is
  the seventh surface to need them.

## Practice Clarifications

- The argument is deliberately the opposite way round from what a reader
  expects. It does not say the body is self and the mind is not; it says that
  if someone must identify with something, the body is the less damaging
  choice, because its changing is visible and the mind's changing is not.
- The monkey simile is about rate, not about restlessness. The point is that
  the mind is replaced faster than it can be watched, so the sense of a single
  continuing thing is an artifact of not looking closely.
- The remedy offered is not to observe the mind harder but to attend to
  dependent arising, which is why the four-line formula and the full chain
  arrive at exactly this point in the discourse.
