# AN 8.39 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[AN 8.39: Abhisanda Sutta](an8-39-abhisanda-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `an8.39:1.1`–`an8.39:8.1`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/an8.39/pli/ms>.
- Source boundary: the discourse ends at `an8.39:8.1`. The segment after it,
  `8.2` (`Navamaṁ`), is collection apparatus marking the ninth discourse of
  the Dānavagga. The closing line `The ninth discourse is finished.` renders
  that marker only, matching AN 2.9, AN 3.88, AN 11.12, and SN 12.20.
- The discourse has 268 Pali words once the front matter is excluded. Much of
  its structure is carried by peyyāla abbreviation.
- Control surfaces: the conduct section of DN 2 for the abstention formula,
  AN 3.65 for `vera`, AN 6.63 for `puñña`, MN 137 and AN 6.63 for the
  `iṭṭha kanta manāpa` triad, and MN 61 for `affliction`.

## Governing Decisions

- **`puññābhisanda` is `stream of benefit` and `kusalābhisanda` is `stream of
  what is wholesome`.** `puñña` keeps its house default `benefit` rather than
  the controlled alternate `merit`. The alternate is reserved for chant
  titles, dedication formulas, and other fixed liturgical expressions; this is
  a discourse being taught, not a liturgy, and the reader-facing point is that
  these eight things do you good. `abhisanda` is the flowing-in of water, and
  `stream` keeps that image without the flood-of-grace overtone that
  `outpouring` would add. The pair is now governed by a phrase record so the
  two halves cannot drift apart.
- **`sukha` is `ease` in all three of its appearances.** The formula uses the
  word three times — `sukhassāhāra`, `sukhavipāka`, and `sukhāya` — and the
  English repeats it three times in the same places. `happiness` is a
  discouraged rendering in this repository and `satisfaction`, the headword
  default, belongs to the analytical contexts where `sukha` names a state.
  This is the recorded idiomatic alternate, used for an idiomatic line.
- **`sukhassāhāra` is `food for ease`.** `āhāra` takes its plain alternate
  `food` rather than the four-nutriments `nutriment`, which is scoped to the
  doctrinal analysis of what sustains becoming and is not what this compound
  is doing.
- **`iṭṭha kanta manāpa` is `wished for, loved, and agreeable`**, as in MN 137
  and AN 6.63. `hita` is `welfare`, not `benefit`, because `benefit` is
  already carrying `puñña` in the same sentence.
- **`sovaggika` is `heavenly` and `saggasaṁvattanika` is `leading to
  heaven`.** The two words say nearly the same thing, and the English keeps
  both rather than merging them. `sagga` keeps its governed `heaven`.
- **`dāna` is `gift` here.** The headword default `generosity` names the
  quality; this passage counts five of them (`pañca dānāni mahādānāni`), and
  `five generosities` is not English. A context rule for the countable sense
  is added to the record rather than leaving the surface to improvise.
- **`aggañña rattañña vaṁsañña porāṇa` is kept as four separate claims**:
  `known to be foremost, known to be long-standing, known to belong to an old
  lineage, ancient`. `known to be foremost` is used for `aggañña` instead of
  anything beginning with `first`, because the sentence it sits in is already
  counting gifts as first through fifth.
- **`asaṅkiṇṇa` and its three tensed restatements are `corrupted`.** The Pali
  says the same thing four ways — never corrupted, not corrupted before, not
  corrupted now, never to be corrupted — and the pile-up is the source's own
  emphasis, so it is not compressed. `adulterated` was rejected as outside
  the plain-English register.
- **`appaṭikuṭṭha samaṇehi brāhmaṇehi viññūhi` is `recluses and brahmins who
  have discernment do not fault them`.** `samaṇa` keeps its governed
  `recluse`, and `viññū` is read as qualifying both groups rather than naming
  a third, which is how the instrumental string reads.
- **`abhaya avera abyābajjha` is `freedom from fear, freedom from enmity,
  freedom from affliction`.** `vera` keeps its governed `enmity`.
  `abyābajjha` has no headword entry; `affliction` follows MN 2 and MN 61,
  where the same family is already rendered that way. The triad is now
  governed by a phrase record.
- **`pahāya … paṭivirato hoti` is `gives up … and refrains from …`.**
  `pahāna` takes its recorded `giving up` alternate for concise practical
  prose, and the absolutive is turned into a finite verb rather than an
  English `Having given up …` opener, which the plain-English standard
  rejects.
- **`bhāgī hoti` is `they come to share in`**, with `aparimāṇa` rendered
  `countless` for the beings and `without limit` for what the giver shares
  in. The same word does both jobs in the Pali, and the two English phrases
  keep the sentence readable while saying the same thing twice, as the source
  does.
- **The noble disciple takes `they`**, as on every other surface that renders
  `ariyasāvaka`.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source abbreviates in two places, and this edition follows its shape
rather than expanding everything.

The first is short. Refuge in the Buddha is written out in full, refuge in the
Dhamma is abbreviated to its head and tail (`Ayaṁ, bhikkhave, dutiyo
puññābhisando …pe… saṁvattati`), and refuge in the Saṅgha is written out in
full again. The English keeps both full statements where the source puts them
and names the second item once between them.

The second covers the middle three gifts. The source writes the abstention
clause for each of them — taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, false
speech — and abbreviates everything after it, then writes the fifth gift out
in full. The English keeps all three abstention clauses, states once what the
abbreviation stands for, and gives the numbering the source itself supplies:
these are gifts two through four and streams five through seven.

Nothing represented by a peyyāla is dropped, and no item is added, removed, or
reordered.

## Source-Audit Findings

- **`kāmesu micchācārā veramaṇī` is not in AN 8.39.** The `kāmesu-micchācāra`
  record cited that phrase to this discourse, and the strict verifier scored
  it `inflected` rather than `absent`, because every stem in it does occur
  somewhere. `veramaṇī` does not occur in the discourse at all: AN 8.39 has
  `kāmesumicchācāraṁ pahāya kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti`. The citation is
  repaired to that exact running-text phrase, and it now verifies `exact`.
  This is the fourth citation of this shape found in the project, after
  `ariyapuggala` at SN 55.30, `upasamānussati` at AN 11.12, and `dhammatā` at
  SN 12.20. The rule stands: an `inflected` verdict on a short headword or a
  precept formula is worth opening by hand.
- **`surāmeraya-majjapamādaṭṭhāna` had no example at all**, only a bare
  reference. The discourse contains the headword as a whole word, so it now
  carries an `exact` example rather than an unquoted reference.

## Re-audit Calls

- **`abyābajjha` and `byābādha` still have no lexicon entry. Found again here,
  not fixed in this packet.** MN 61's notes already recorded that gap and
  proposed a minor entry fixing `affliction` as the governed rendering. AN
  8.39 is now the second surface to need it in running text, and this packet
  governs only the `abhayaṁ averaṁ abyābajjhaṁ` triad, not the headword. A
  headword entry would also cover MN 2 and MN 61 and is the right next lexicon
  change in this family.
- **`samaṇa` is rendered `ascetics and brahmins` on three surfaces.** DN 2,
  MN 99, and MN 18 use `ascetic`, which the `samaṇa` record lists as a
  discouraged translation; MN 36, MN 11, MN 22, AN 3.65, MN 38, and now AN
  8.39 use the governed `recluse`. Nothing catches this, because
  `check_translation_drift.py` compares term records rather than translation
  prose. Recorded here rather than fixed, so that the repair happens as one
  deliberate pass across all three surfaces.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse's argument is that restraint is not only a restriction on the
  person practising it. Every being who might have been killed, robbed,
  betrayed, lied to, or endangered by someone drunk is given something
  concrete: they no longer have to fear this person. That is why the five
  abstentions are called gifts rather than rules.
- `Countless beings` is not a cosmological claim. It is the plain
  consequence of not limiting the abstention: the people it protects are not
  a list anyone drew up in advance.
- The last move is the surprising one. The giver comes to share in exactly
  what they gave. The text does not explain the mechanism, and this edition
  does not supply one.
- The three refuges are counted as streams of benefit alongside the five
  abstentions, in the same terms. Going for refuge is presented here as
  something that does you good, not as a membership requirement.

## Wave 10 Role

AN 8.39 is the third Wave 10 queue item. It was ranked for two orphan signals
and anchors both: `kāmesu-micchācāra` and `surāmeraya-majjapamādaṭṭhāna`, the
two precept terms whose only cited source was this discourse. Three further
orphans are closed honestly along the way — `dāna`, `saraṇa`, and `saṅgha` all
occur here in running text and previously cited only untranslated sources.
Corpus orphans go from 96 to 91.

Beyond the lexicon, the discourse gives the reader path its first text that
states what keeping the precepts does for other people, rather than listing
the precepts as training rules.
