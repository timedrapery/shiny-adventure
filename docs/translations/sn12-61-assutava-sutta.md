## Translation

### The Setting

So I heard.

At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

### The Body Is the Easier Thing to Let Go Of

"Bhikkhus, an unlearned ordinary person might well grow disenchanted with this
body made of the four great elements, might well fade toward it, might well be
released from it.

Why is that?

Because the building up and the wearing down of this body made of the four
great elements can be seen, its being taken up and its being put down. So an
unlearned ordinary person might well grow disenchanted with it, might well fade
toward it, might well be released from it.

But as for what is called the feeling mind, and the thinking mind, and knowing
— an unlearned ordinary person is not able to grow disenchanted with that, not
able to fade toward it, not able to be released from it.

Why is that?

Because for a long time an unlearned ordinary person has held to it, taken it
as mine, and grasped it: 'This is mine, I am this, this is my self.' So an
unlearned ordinary person is not able to grow disenchanted with it, not able to
fade toward it, not able to be released from it.

### The Monkey

It would be better, bhikkhus, for an unlearned ordinary person to take this
body made of the four great elements as self than to take the mind as self.

Why is that?

Because this body made of the four great elements is seen to last one year, two
years, three, four, five, ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty years, a hundred
years, and longer.

But as for what is called the feeling mind, and the thinking mind, and knowing
— by night and by day it arises as one thing and ceases as another.

Suppose a monkey roaming the forest wilds takes hold of a branch, lets it go
and takes hold of another, lets that go and takes hold of another. In the same
way, bhikkhus, what is called the feeling mind, and the thinking mind, and
knowing — by night and by day it arises as one thing and ceases as another.

### How the Learned Disciple Attends

There, bhikkhus, a learned noble disciple attends closely and wisely to
dependent arising itself:

'When this is, this comes to be; from the arising of this, this arises. When
this is not, this does not come to be; with the quenching of this, this ceases.

That is: with ignorance as condition, putting things together;
with putting things together as condition, knowing;
with knowing as condition, name-and-form;
with name-and-form as condition, the six fields of experience;
with the six fields of experience as condition, contact;
with contact as condition, felt experience;
with felt experience as condition, ignorant wanting;
with ignorant wanting as condition, taking personally;
with taking personally as condition, becoming;
with becoming as condition, birth;
with birth as condition, ageing and dying, sorrow, lamentation,
dissatisfaction, distress, and despair come to be.

That is how this whole heap of dissatisfaction comes to arise.

But with the complete fading and quenching of ignorance, the quenching of
putting things together;
with the quenching of putting things together, the quenching of knowing;
with the quenching of knowing, the quenching of name-and-form;
with the quenching of name-and-form, the quenching of the six fields of
experience;
with the quenching of the six fields of experience, the quenching of contact;
with the quenching of contact, the quenching of felt experience;
with the quenching of felt experience, the quenching of ignorant wanting;
with the quenching of ignorant wanting, the quenching of taking personally;
with the quenching of taking personally, the quenching of becoming;
with the quenching of becoming, the quenching of birth;
with the quenching of birth, the quenching of ageing and dying, sorrow,
lamentation, dissatisfaction, distress, and despair.

That is how this whole heap of dissatisfaction quenches.'

### Disenchantment, Fading, and Release

Seeing this, bhikkhus, a learned noble disciple becomes disenchanted with form,
disenchanted with felt experience, disenchanted with recognition, disenchanted
with putting things together, disenchanted with knowing.

Being disenchanted, they fade. Through fading, they are released. When it is
released, there is the knowledge: 'It is released.'

They discern: 'Birth is worn away, the spiritual life has been lived, what had
to be done has been done, there is no more of this state of being.'"

The first discourse is finished.
