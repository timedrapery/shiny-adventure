# AN 4.5: Anusota Sutta

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [AN 4.5 Translation Notes](an4-5-anusota-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- The four people are described through one sustained river image: going with
  the stream, going against it, standing firm, and crossing to dry land.
- `tiṇṇo pāraṅgato` is rendered `crossed over, gone to the far shore`,
  preserving completed-crossing language rather than replacing it with an
  abstract word such as "liberated."
- `sati` is `remembering`; `samāhita` is expressed through `composed`.

## Translation

### Four Ways of Meeting the Stream

"Bhikkhus, four kinds of people are found in the world.

Which four?

A person going with the stream.

A person going against the stream.

A person standing firm.

And a brahmin who has crossed over, gone to the far shore, and stands on dry
land.

### Going With the Stream

Who is the person who goes with the stream?

It is someone who indulges in sensual pleasures and does harmful actions.

This is called a person who goes with the stream.

### Going Against the Stream

Who is the person who goes against the stream?

It is someone who does not indulge in sensual pleasures or do harmful
actions. Even while suffering and distressed—weeping, with tears on their
face—they live the full and pure spiritual life.

This is called a person who goes against the stream.

### Standing Firm

Who is the person who stands firm?

It is someone who, with the wearing away of the five lower fetters, is
spontaneously reborn elsewhere. They reach final nibbāna there and will not
return from that world.

This is called a person who stands firm.

### Reaching Dry Land

Who is the brahmin who has crossed over, gone to the far shore, and stands on
dry land?

It is someone who, with the wearing away of the outflows, realizes for
themselves the release of mind and release through discernment, both free of
outflows. They enter and live in these releases here and now.

This is called a brahmin who has crossed over, gone to the far shore, and
stands on dry land.

These are the four kinds of people found in the world.

### The Four in Verse

People unrestrained in sensual pleasures,
still caught in passion and enjoying those pleasures here,
return to birth and old age again and again.
Overwhelmed by ignorant wanting, they go with the stream.

So a clear-sighted person, with remembering established,
does not indulge in sensual pleasures or harmful actions.
Even through pain, they should let sensual pleasures go.
Such a person is said to go against the stream.

Someone who has abandoned five stains,
a fully developed trainee who cannot fall back,
master of their heart, with their faculties composed,
is called a person who stands firm.

Whoever has fully understood things high and low
has swept them away; they have ended and are no more.
That sage has completed the spiritual life,
gone to the world's end, and reached the far shore."

The fifth discourse is finished.
