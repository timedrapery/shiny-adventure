# SN 56.17: Avijjā Sutta (Ignorance)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [SN 56.17 Translation Notes](sn56-17-avijja-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `avijjā` is rendered `ignorance`, then explained in the Buddha's own terms:
  not understanding dissatisfaction, where it comes from, how it ends, or the
  path that leads to its ending.
- The source abbreviates the final four-part instruction. This edition prints
  all four parts so the reader can see exactly what is to be understood.

## Translation

### What Ignorance Means

A bhikkhu sitting to one side said to the Buddha:

"Bhante, people talk about 'ignorance.' What is ignorance? When can someone be
said to be caught in it?"

"Bhikkhu, it means not understanding dissatisfaction, where it comes from, how
it ends, or the path of practice that leads to its ending.

This is what ignorance means. This is when someone can be said to be caught in
it.

So apply yourself to understanding:

- 'This is dissatisfaction.'
- 'This is where dissatisfaction comes from.'
- 'This is how dissatisfaction ends.'
- 'This is the path of practice that leads to its ending.'"

The seventh discourse is finished.
