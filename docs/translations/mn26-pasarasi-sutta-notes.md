# MN 26 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[MN 26: Pāsarāsi Sutta](mn26-pasarasi-sutta.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of MN 26 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially
  `pariyesanā`, `upadhi`, `yogakkhema`, `abhiññā`, `nibbidā`, `virāga`,
  `upasama`, `saddhā`, `viriya`, `sati`, `sampajañña`, `samādhi`, `paññā`,
  `jhāna`, `pīti`, `sukha`, `saññā`, `āsava`, `idappaccayatā`, and the four
  formless attainments.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as the repository's control
  surface for the two searches (ignoble and noble), the autobiographical
  awakening account, and the full meditative arc from the first mental theme
  through the cessation of recognition and felt experience — the "blinding
  Māra" sequence.

## Governing Decisions

- `pariyesanā` is rendered `search` (contextual; no standalone lexicon entry).
  `Ariyā pariyesanā` = `the noble search`; `anariyā pariyesanā` = `the
  ignoble search`. The verb `pariyesati` = "seeks, searches for." The
  two-searches framework is the structural spine of the sutta.
- `upadhi` is rendered `support` (major lexicon entry). In context, these are
  the things one holds as possessions and is attached to — partners and
  children, servants, livestock, gold and silver — which are described as
  `jātidhammā` (subject to birth), `jarādhammā` (subject to aging), etc.
  `Upadhi` names what underlies one's existence as a possession-holder: the
  substrate of clinging. The rendering "these are supports subject to birth"
  preserves the technical term while making the list structure clear.
- `yogakkhema` is rendered `security from the yoke` (minor lexicon entry).
  The full formula: `anuttaraṁ yogakkhemaṁ nibbānaṁ` = "nibbāna — the
  supreme security from the yoke." `Yoga` = yoke (binding to existence);
  `khema` = safety, security. Nibbāna is the supreme state of being free from
  what yokes beings to continued existence.
- `abhiññā` is rendered `higher knowing` (major lexicon entry). The formula
  `sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharāmī` = "having realized with my
  own higher knowing, entered into it, and live having achieved it." This
  appears throughout the Āḷāra and Uddaka passages: the realization being
  claimed is not mere book-knowledge but direct higher knowing of the attained
  state.
- `nibbidā` is rendered `disenchantment` (house default). In the conclusion
  about each teacher's system: "this teaching does not lead to disenchantment,
  fading, cessation, calming, higher knowing, awakening, or nibbāna." The
  seven-term formula (`nibbidā, virāga, nirodha, upasama, abhiññā, sambodha,
  nibbāna`) gives the full arc from first disillusionment to final liberation.
- `sambodha` is rendered `awakening` (no lexicon entry; rendering used
  contextually). `Sambodha` = complete understanding/awakening. It appears in
  the seven-term formula and is distinguished from `nibbāna` as the
  experiential awakening that results in nibbāna.
- `upasama` is rendered `calming` (minor lexicon entry). In the seven-term
  formula, `upasama` names the settling and stilling that follows fading and
  cessation. It is distinct from `samatha` (tranquility practice) and here
  names the quality of the mind once the fires have gone out.
- `saññā` is rendered `recognition` in the formless attainment names:
  `nevasaññānāsaññāyatana` = "sphere of neither-recognition-nor-non-recognition."
  `Saññā` → `recognition` is consistent with the heap rendering
  (`sannakkhandha` = recognition heap) and the perception-of-impermanence
  family (`aniccasaññā` etc.).
- `saññāvedayitanirodha` is rendered `cessation of recognition and felt
  experience` (no standalone lexicon entry). The compound: `saññā` (recognition)
  + `vedayita` (felt, from `vedanā`) + `nirodha` (cessation). This is the
  deepest meditative attainment, where both recognition and felt experience
  cease. `Āsavā parikkhīṇā honti` = "their outflows are exhausted" — since
  `āsava` → `outflow`, this renders as "their outflows are exhausted."
- The `ālaya` motif (mn26:19.3-4) — `ālayarāmā ālayaratā ālayasammuditā` — is
  rendered "the people delight in their resting-place, are pleased with their
  resting-place, rejoice in their resting-place." `Ālaya` = home, nest, the
  place where one settles; by extension, the clinging that serves as one's
  resting-place. The rendering preserves the repeated three-part formula
  (delight / pleased / rejoice) which mirrors the three-part response the
  teaching needs to interrupt.
- `idappaccayatā paṭiccasamuppādo` (mn26:19.4) is rendered `conditionality,
  dependent arising`. `Idappaccayatā` → `conditionality` (major lexicon entry).
  `Paṭiccasamuppāda` → `dependent arising` (house default). The two terms are
  paired here: conditionality names the structural principle; dependent arising
  names the full arising-chain teaching.
- `sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho
  nibbānaṁ` (mn26:19.5) is rendered "the calming of all putting-things-together,
  the relinquishing of all supports, the ending of ignorant wanting, fading,
  cessation, nibbāna." Each term uses the established house rendering.
- The verse passages (mn26:19.8-15, mn26:20.10-23, mn26:21.6-9,
  mn26:25.6-26) are rendered as verse with line breaks, not as prose. The
  Pali meter is not reproduced in English — the rendering aims for natural
  English verse that preserves the meaning and rhetorical force.
- The exchange with Upaka the Ājīvaka (mn26:25) is a set of verses entirely
  in the first person, with Upaka's two lines of prose response embedded. The
  translation preserves the verse-prose structure.
- `andhamakāsi māraṁ apadaṁ, vadhitvā māracakkhuṁ adassanaṁ gato pāpimato`
  is rendered "has blinded Māra, has put out Māra's eye without a trace, and
  has gone where the Wicked One cannot see." `Pāpimato` = the Wicked One
  (Māra). This is the closing formula for each attainment in the "blinding
  Māra" section (mn26:34-42).
- `tiṇṇo loke visattikaṁ` (mn26:42.3) is rendered "They have crossed over the
  world's entanglement." `Visattikā` = the world's clinging-web or entanglement
  — what keeps beings enmeshed in sensual existence.
- The sutta title `Pāsarāsisutta` = "Heap of Snares Sutta" (from `pāsa` =
  snare + `rāsi` = heap), named after the deer-and-snare simile in mn26:32-33.
  The sutta is also known as the Ariyapariyesanā Sutta in some recensions.
  Both names are noted in the Editorial Note.

## Re-audit Calls

- Resolved: `pariyesanā` now has a minor entry, `terms/minor/pariyesana.json`.
  The rendering "search" is contextual and consistent with the verb
  `pariyesati` = "searches, seeks." The "ignoble
  search" and "noble search" framing is the central organizing metaphor of the
  sutta. Future revisions should add a minor entry if the term is governed
  across multiple surfaces.
- Resolved: `sambodha` now has a minor cross-reference entry,
  `terms/minor/sambodha.json`. It is deliberately kept as a minor
  cross-reference to the existing major `bodhi` entry rather than a
  standalone major entry, since `bodhi` already governs `awakening` as the
  house default (and already cites `anuttarā sammāsambodhi` from this sutta
  as an example) — a separate major entry would risk drifting away from an
  already-governed near-synonym rather than staying consistent with it.
- Resolved: the seven-term formula
  (`nibbidāya...nibbānāya saṃvattati`) now has a formula record,
  `terms/minor/nibbida-viraga-nirodha-upasama-abhinna-sambodha-nibbana-formula.json`.
- Resolved: `saññāvedayitanirodha` now has a minor entry,
  `terms/minor/sannavedayitanirodha.json`.
- Resolved: `visattikā` ("the world's entanglement," mn26:42.3) now has a
  minor entry, `terms/minor/visattika.json`.
- The Bilara collapses many of the repeated passages in the sutta with `…pe…`
  markers. These include: the middle four qualities (sickness, death, sorrow,
  corruption) in mn26:13.3 (the bodhisatta's reflection), and the abbreviated
  cessation formula in mn26:30 (the five bhikkhus). The translation expands
  these in full, consistent with the repository's policy of preserving
  repetition for study readability.
- The "blinding Māra" formula (mn26:35-41) is abbreviated in the Bilara with
  `Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave …pe… pāpimato` for the second through eighth
  attainments. These are expanded in full in the translation, following the
  first attainment's formula as given in mn26:34.5.
- The Uddaka episode (mn26:16) is structurally parallel to the Āḷāra episode
  (mn26:15) with one key difference: Āḷāra Kālāma was the Bodhisatta's actual
  teacher (`ācariyo me`) who placed him as his pupil on equal footing
  (`attanā samasamaṁ ṭhapesi`), while Uddaka, son of Rāma, was a fellow
  practitioner (`sabrahmacārī`) whose teacher was Rāma — placing him
  (`udako rāmaputto sabrahmacārī me samāno ācariyaṭṭhāne maṁ ṭhapesi`) in
  the position of teacher. The translation preserves this distinction: Āḷāra
  is described as "my teacher," while Uddaka is described as "my fellow
  practitioner."

## Editorial Presentation

- The main translation is divided into thirteen sections: `The Setting`, `The
  Two Searches`, `The Bodhisatta's Own Search`, `Going Forth`, `Āḷāra
  Kālāma`, `Uddaka Rāmaputta`, `Arrival at Uruvelā and Awakening`,
  `Hesitation`, `Brahmā Sahampati's Request`, `The Lotus Simile`, `Who to
  Teach First`, `Upaka the Ājīvaka`, `The Five Ascetics`, `The Five Achieve
  the Noble Search`, `Sensual Pleasure and the Snare`, and `Blinding Māra:
  The Mental Themes and Beyond`.
- The two-searches section preserves the full sixfold treatment (birth, aging,
  sickness, death, sorrow, corruption) in the ignoble search, with each item's
  list stated in full. The noble search section restates the full sixfold
  formula with the `ādīnavaṁ viditvā` (having seen the danger) structure.
- The verse passages are set with Markdown blockquote formatting to distinguish
  them visually from prose. The original Pali verses are not reproduced —
  only the English rendering.
