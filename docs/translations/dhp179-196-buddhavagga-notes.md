# Dhp 179-196 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[Dhp 179-196: Buddhavagga](dhp179-196-buddhavagga.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of Dhp 179-196 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`dhp179:0.1` to `dhp196:6`), fetched from the upstream Bilara
  root.
- Control surfaces: Dhp 21-32 and Dhp 273-289 for the Dhammapada framing and
  verse register; SN 56.11 for the four noble truths; the `taṇhā`, `khanti`,
  `pāṭimokkha`, `adhicitta`, `saraṇa`, `kāma`, `papañca`, `nibbuta`, and
  `puñña` records.
- Working method: eighteen verses, seventeen of four lines and one (185) of
  six, each translated as one stanza and kept line for line where English
  allows.

## Dhammapada Framing

This chapter follows the Dhp 21-32 framing with no change: the verse range
and Pali chapter name as the title, verse numbers kept in bold, the
commentary's story titles (`vatthu`) left out, and the chapter close kept
(`Buddhavaggo cuddasamo`, `The fourteenth chapter, on the Buddha, is
finished.`). The root also closes the first recitation section
(`Paṭhamabhāṇavāraṁ`); that is a division for reciters, not part of the
chapter, and is not translated.

## Governing Decisions

- `taṇhā` is `ignorant wanting`, its governed default; `taṇhakkhaya` is `the
  ending of ignorant wanting`. `jālinī visattikā` is `that net, that
  entanglement`, following `visattikā` (`the world's entanglement`).
- `apadaṁ kena padena nessatha` is `who leaves no track, by what track will
  you lead him?` The Pali plays on `pada` as both footprint and path.
- `anantagocara` is `whose range has no end`.
- `jhānapasutā` is `intent on the mental themes`, following `jhāna`.
  `nekkhammūpasame ratā` is `delighting in the calm of renunciation`.
- `satīmataṁ` is `remembering`, following `sati`.
- Verse 183 is rendered `Not doing any evil, taking up the wholesome,
  purifying your own mind: this is the teaching of the Buddhas.` `pāpa` is
  `evil` here: the verse is a summary of all practice, and `bad` reads as too
  slight. `kusala` keeps its governed `wholesome`. `sāsana` is `teaching`;
  MN 122 and AN 6.19 use `instruction` for the Buddha's direct instruction to
  bhikkhus, and here the word names the whole teaching of all Buddhas.
- `khantī paramaṁ tapo titikkhā` is `patience, enduring patience, is the
  highest austerity`. `khanti` keeps its governed `patience`; `titikkhā` is
  its near-synonym, glossed rather than given a second noun.
- `pabbajita` is `gone forth` and `samaṇa` is left untranslated, as in the
  repository's other surfaces.
- `pātimokkhe ca saṁvaro` is `restraint in the pāṭimokkha`, the record's
  untranslated default. `adhicitta` is `the higher mind`.
- `appassādā dukhā kāmā` is `sensuality gives little gratification and much
  pain`. `assāda` is `gratification`, as in SN 22.26. `dukha` here is the
  plain adjective `painful`, set against `gratification`, so `pain` is used
  rather than `dissatisfaction`.
- Verses 186 and 187 are one sentence across two stanzas, and the
  translation keeps that.
- In verses 189 to 192, `dukkha` keeps its governed `dissatisfaction`, and
  the four truths follow SN 56.11's terms. `atikkama` is `going beyond`;
  `dukkhūpasamagāminaṁ` is `that leads to the calming of dissatisfaction`.
- `khema` is `secure`. The record `khema` is the personal name Khemā, a
  different word.
- `purisājañña` is `a thoroughbred among people`, keeping the horse image of
  `ājañña` from AN 11.9.
- `sukha` in verse 194 is `happy`, not the governed `satisfaction`. The verse
  praises four events as fortunate; `satisfying is the arising of Buddhas`
  would mislead. This is document-local.
- `papañca` is `proliferation`, `nibbuta` is `cooled`, and `akutobhaya` is
  `fearing nothing from any side`.
- `puñña` is `benefit`, its governed default.

## Practice Clarifications

- Verses 183 to 185 are the traditional summary of the teaching, often
  recited together: avoid harm, develop the wholesome, purify the mind, with
  patience and restraint as its daily form.
- Verses 188 to 192 contrast refuge in places with refuge in the Buddha,
  Dhamma, and Saṅgha, and define that refuge by seeing the four truths, not
  by belief alone.
