# Dhp 273-289 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[Dhp 273-289: Maggavagga](dhp273-289-maggavagga.md). The main translation is
the primary study surface; this file records debated translation choices,
source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of Dhp 273-289 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data (`dhp273:0.1` to `dhp289:5`), fetched from the upstream Bilara
  root.
- Control surfaces: Dhp 21-32 for the Dhammapada framing and the verse
  register; AN 3.136 and the three formula records for verses 277 to 279;
  SN 36.6 for `salla`; the `magga`, `nibbidā`, `virāga`, `santi`, `saṅkappa`,
  and `Sugata` records.
- Working method: seventeen verses, sixteen of four lines and one (282) of
  six, each translated as one stanza and kept line for line where English
  allows.

## Dhammapada Framing

This chapter follows the framing settled in the Dhp 21-32 notes, with no
change: one surface for the chapter, the verse range and Pali chapter name as
the title, verse numbers kept in bold, the commentary's story titles
(`vatthu`) left out, and the chapter close kept (`Maggavaggo vīsatimo`, `The
twentieth chapter, on the path, is finished.`). The root carries twelve story
titles, from `Pañcasatabhikkhuvatthu` above verse 273 to `Paṭācārāvatthu`
above verse 288.

## Governing Decisions

- `magga` is `path` throughout. `maggānaṭṭhaṅgiko seṭṭho` is `of paths, the
  eightfold is the best`; the noble eightfold path record is the background,
  but the verse does not say `noble`, and the translation does not add it.
- `saccānaṁ caturo padā` is `of truths, the four statements`. `pada` here is a
  statement or line of the four truths, not a footstep.
- `virāgo seṭṭho dhammānaṁ` is `of dhammas, fading is the best`. `virāga`
  keeps its governed `fading`, and `dhamma` stays untranslated, its headword
  default.
- `dvipadānañca cakkhumā` is `of those who walk on two feet, the one with
  vision`: the Buddha, though the verse does not name him.
- `dassanassa visuddhiyā` is `for the purifying of vision`, and `esa maggo
  visuddhiyā` is `this is the path to purity`. `visuddhi` has no record; it is
  a candidate headword in the Dhammarato profile.
- `aññāya sallakantanaṁ` is `knowing how the arrow is drawn out`.
  `salla` keeps its governed `arrow`, as in SN 36.6.
- `akkhātāro tathāgatā` is `Tathāgatas only tell the way`. `only` makes the
  contrast with `tumhehi kiccamātappaṁ` (`you yourselves must make the
  effort`) explicit; the Pali sets the two lines side by side without it.
- `jhāyino` is `practice the mental themes`, as in Dhp 21-32.
- Verses 277 to 279 keep the AN 3.136 wording of the three formula lines.
  `atha nibbindati dukkhe` is `they grow disenchanted with dissatisfaction`:
  `nibbidā` keeps its governed `disenchantment` and `dukkha` its governed
  `dissatisfaction`. The quoted line is `unsatisfactory` and the result line
  `dissatisfaction`, as the Pali uses the adjective in one and the noun in
  the other.
- `saṁsannasaṅkappamano` is `their attitude and mind sunk low`. `saṅkappa`
  keeps its governed `attitude`.
- `kammapathe` is `paths of action`, and `isippaveditaṁ` is `the path the
  seers made known`.
- `yogā ve jāyatī bhūri` is `from practice, wisdom grows`. `yoga` here is
  application to practice, not the record's `yoke`, which belongs to the
  bonds family; `practice` is document-local. `bhūri` is `wisdom`.
  `bhavāya vibhavāya ca` is `to growth and to decline`: here `bhava` and
  `vibhava` are the ordinary words for gain and loss, not the governed
  `becoming`, which would mislead.
- Verse 283 turns on a pun: `vana` is forest and also desire, and `nibbana`
  (`without forest`) echoes `nibbāna`. English cannot keep both; the
  translation keeps the forest and says so here. `vanatha` is `undergrowth`.
- `sinehamattano` is `your fondness for yourself`.
- `santimaggameva brūhaya` is `develop only the path to peace`. `santi` keeps
  its governed `peace`. Here it is a compound member (`santimagga`), so, like
  Dhp 202, this verse is not a clean anchor for the orphan major `santi`, and
  it is not cited for it.
- `antarāyaṁ na bujjhati` is `does not see what may cut it short`, and
  `antaka` is `the Ender`, a name for death.
- `nibbānagamanaṁ maggaṁ` is `the path that leads to nibbāna`.

## Practice Clarifications

- Verses 277 to 279 make the same move three times: seeing a characteristic
  with discernment leads to disenchantment, and that is the path. It is the
  Dhammapada's compressed form of the method SN 22.59 lays out.
- Verse 276 is the chapter's center: the Buddha points out the way, and the
  walking is yours.
- Verses 286 to 289 turn from the path to death, which does not wait for
  plans. The chapter ends by telling the wise to clear the path quickly.
