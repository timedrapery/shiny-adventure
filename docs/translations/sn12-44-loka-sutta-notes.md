# SN 12.44 Translation Notes

This document records the source and editorial decisions for
[SN 12.44: Loka Sutta](sn12-44-loka-sutta.md).

## Source Base

- Primary source: SuttaCentral Bilara Mahāsaṅgīti root text, segment IDs
  `sn12.44:1.1`–`sn12.44:5.12`.
- Canonical reader source: <https://suttacentral.net/sn12.44/pli/ms>.
- Cross-check: SuttaCentral's published Bhikkhu Sujato segmentation was used
  to confirm the source boundary and the scope of each peyyāla. Its wording
  was not used as the governing English surface.
- Control surfaces: the six-door contact formulas in MN 148, the governed
  dependent-arising sequence in SN 12.2, and the experiential use of `loka`
  recorded in SN 12.15.
- The discourse has about 182 Pali words after front matter is excluded. Much
  of its repeated structure is represented by peyyāla abbreviations.

## Governing Decisions

- **`loka` is `world`.** The term keeps its stable headword rendering rather
  than `universe`. In this discourse the world arises through a sense organ,
  its object, the corresponding knowing, contact, felt experience, and the
  dependent-arising sequence. The translation therefore treats it as the
  lived field of conditioned experience, not as a cosmological creation or
  the physical destruction of a planet.
- **`samudaya` is `arises` here.** Its governed alternate `arising` fits a
  passage about dynamic emergence better than the four-truth label `origin`.
  The question `lokassa samudayo` becomes the direct, ordinary-English `how
  does the world arise?`
- **`atthaṅgama` is `ends`.** The Pali word can describe disappearance or a
  setting like the sun. `Ends` is the clearest contemporary verb here and
  remains distinct from the linked `nirodha` terms, which keep `quenching`.
- **The six sense doors follow the established sense-field controls.** `rūpa`
  is `visible forms`; sounds, smells, and tastes remain ordinary English;
  `phoṭṭhabba` takes its plainer governed alternate `touchable` in `touchable
  things`; and `dhamma` takes its reader-facing sense-object rendering
  `ideas`. The six corresponding `viññāṇa` compounds are `eye-knowing`
  through `mind-knowing`.
- **`tiṇṇaṁ saṅgati phasso` remains `the meeting of the three is contact`.**
  The three are the sense organ, its object, and the corresponding knowing.
  The wording stays concrete and reuses the existing sense-contact control
  passage rather than replacing contact with a vague mental event.
- **The causal tail keeps the governed dependent-arising vocabulary.**
  `vedanā` is `felt experience`, `taṇhā` is `ignorant wanting`, `upādāna` is
  `taking personally`, `bhava` is `becoming`, and `jāti` is `birth`.
- **Fading and quenching remain separate.** `tassāyeva taṇhāya
  asesavirāganirodhā` is `with the complete fading and quenching of that very
  ignorant wanting`. This keeps `virāga` and `nirodha` audible as two linked
  movements rather than flattening both into generic cessation language.
- **The final chain uses `quenching` consistently.** This makes the relation
  between the quenching of ignorant wanting, the downstream links, and the
  whole heap of dissatisfaction visible without rotating between `ending`,
  `cessation`, and `quenching` inside one formula. `Ends` is reserved for the
  discourse's framing word `atthaṅgama`.

## Peyyāla Handling

The source prints the eye sequence in full, abbreviates the ear, nose, tongue,
and body sequences, then gives the mind opening and the final tail. It uses the
same structure on the ending side.

This edition expands the six entry points but consolidates their identical
tail. Each half therefore:

1. gives the eye formula in full;
2. names the five remaining sense door and object pairs; and
3. states once that the same contact and dependent-arising sequence follows
   in every case.

This supplies everything represented by the peyyāla without making a reader
work through the same eleven-link paragraph six times. No causal link is added
or removed.

## Practice Clarifications

- The discourse begins at live sensory contact. It does not claim that a
  single sensory event mechanically produces a universe. It shows how a world
  of involvement and dissatisfaction develops when contact conditions felt
  experience, wanting, taking personally, and the rest of the chain.
- The ending side does not require the senses or their objects to disappear.
  The same eye, visible forms, and eye-knowing are stated again. The change
  occurs when ignorant wanting completely fades and quenches, so the links
  built from taking experience personally no longer continue.
- The repetition across all six doors prevents a narrowly visual reading.
  Thoughts and ideas enter through the mind-and-dhammas case just as sights,
  sounds, smells, tastes, and touches enter through the bodily senses.
