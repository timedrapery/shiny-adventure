# AN 10.60 Translation Notes

This document is the companion rationale for
[AN 10.60: Girimānanda Sutta](an10-60-giriminanda-sutta.md). The main
translation is the primary study surface; this file records debated
translation choices, source-audit calls, and edition notes that govern it.

## Source Base

- Primary source: the Pali text of AN 10.60 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data.
- Control surface: the repository's current term policy, especially `sanna`,
  `anicca`, `viraga`, `nirodha`, `sankhara`, `tanha`, `sati`, `piti`,
  `citta`, `mano`, `panna`, and `vedana`.
- Working method: the discourse was governed as the third Aṅguttara Nikāya
  translation surface, following the roadmap's instruction to strengthen
  practice-perception and three-marks contemplative compounds, with
  ānāpānassati as the closing member tying the perception sequence back to
  the breath-based practice already established in MN 118 and MN 10.

## Governing Decisions

- `saññā` is rendered `recognition` throughout, following the house default
  (major). The ten perception names are rendered as `recognition of ...`
  compounds: `aniccasaññā` → `recognition of impermanence`, `virāgasaññā`
  → `recognition of fading`, and so on.
- `anicca` is rendered `impermanent` in the five-heaps reflection and
  `impermanence` in the perception name. The reflection `rūpaṁ aniccaṁ`
  is rendered `Form is impermanent` — the predicate adjective follows the
  house default for `anicca` (major).
- `virāga` is rendered `fading` (major, house default). The reflection
  formula `taṇhākkhayo virāgo nibbānan'ti` is rendered `the destruction of
  ignorant wanting, fading, nibbāna` — keeping `fading` as the final named
  quality before nibbāna.
- `nirodha` is rendered `quenching` (major, house default). Perceptions 6
  and 7 use identical reflection formulas differing only in the final term:
  `virāgo` in perception 6 and `nirodho` in perception 7. The distinction
  `fading` / `quenching` keeps both visible without collapsing them.
- `saṅkhāra` is rendered `what is put together` (major, house default) in
  the perception names (`sabbasaṅkhāresu anicchāsaññā` → `recognition of no
  desire for all that is put together`) and in the virāga/nirodha reflection
  formula (`sabbasaṅkhārasamatho` → `the stilling of all that is put
  together`). In the ānāpānassati steps, `kāyasaṅkhāra` and `cittasaṅkhāra`
  are rendered `bodily putting-together` and `mental putting-together`,
  keeping the saṅkhāra root audible in the breath-awareness context.
- `taṇhā` is rendered `ignorant wanting` (major, house default). The phrase
  `taṇhākkhayo` is rendered `the destruction of ignorant wanting`.
- `sati` is rendered `remembering` (major, house default). `Ānāpānassati`
  is rendered `the remembering of in-and-out breathing` in the perception
  list and section heading.
- `pīti` is rendered `delight` (major, house default). In the ānāpānassati
  step `pītipaṭisaṁvedī`, the rendering is `experiencing delight`.
- `citta` is rendered `feeling mind` (major, house default) throughout the
  ānāpānassati steps: `cittapaṭisaṁvedī` → `experiencing the feeling mind`,
  `abhippamodayaṁ cittaṁ` → `gladdening the feeling mind`, `samādahaṁ
  cittaṁ` → `composing the feeling mind`, `vimocayaṁ cittaṁ` → `releasing
  the feeling mind`.
- `mano` in `mano anattā` is rendered `thinking mind` (major, house default),
  keeping it distinct from `citta` within the same sutta.

## Re-audit Calls

- `asubhasaññā` is rendered `recognition of unattractiveness`. `Asubha`
  (not-beautiful, unattractive) names the body-review contemplation that
  works against sensual idealization. `Unattractiveness` is plain and
  functional without importing disgust-language or ascetic severity.
- `ādīnavasaññā` is rendered `recognition of drawbacks`. `Ādīnava`
  (danger, drawback, liability) is rendered `drawbacks` — consistent with
  the house family for this term across doctrinal prose.
- `bahudukkho kho ayaṁ kāyo` is rendered `This body has much hardship`.
  `Dukkha` in this physical context is rendered `hardship` rather than
  `dissatisfaction`, because the passage is specifically enumerating bodily
  illness and physical difficulty, not the broader doctrinal scope of
  dissatisfaction.
- The body-parts list in perception 3 is translated anatomically in plain
  contemporary English. Medical terms are avoided where common vocabulary
  is available.
- The disease list in perception 4 is translated fully rather than
  abbreviated. The Pali provides a comprehensive catalogue of bodily
  afflictions; the translation preserves this comprehensiveness as part of
  the teaching's force.
- `pahānasaññā` is rendered `recognition through abandoning`. The genitive
  construction could be rendered `recognition of abandoning` or `recognition
  through abandoning`. The second reading better captures the active practice
  described: not tolerating arisen unwholesome thoughts and bringing them to
  an end.
- `sabbaloke anabhiratasaññā` is rendered `recognition of no delight in the
  whole world`. `Anabhirata` = not delighting in, not finding pleasure in.
- `sabbasaṅkhāresu anicchāsaññā` is rendered `recognition of no desire for
  all that is put together`. `Anicchā` (without wish/desire) is distinct from
  `anicca` (impermanent); the two differ in their final syllable and in
  meaning. Sujato's rendering "perception of the impermanence of all
  conditions" conflates them. The house rendering preserves the distinction:
  `anicca` → `impermanent`; `anicchā` → `no desire`.
- `sabbūpadhippaṭinissaggo` in the virāga/nirodha reflection formula is
  rendered `the relinquishing of all acquisitions`. `Upadhi` (what is
  acquired, the substrate of existence) + `paṭinissagga` (letting go) =
  relinquishing all that is acquired.
- The `…pe…` abbreviation marks in the Bilara (ānāpānassati segments
  13.13–13.18) are expanded in full, consistent with the repository's policy
  of preserving repetition for study readability.
- `Dasamaṁ.` (the chapter-internal sutta counter) is rendered `The tenth
  discourse is finished.`

## Editorial Presentation

- The main translation is divided into three sections: `The Setting`,
  `The Ten Recognitions`, and `The Outcome`. Within `The Ten Recognitions`,
  each perception is introduced with a bold numbered heading (e.g.,
  **1. Recognition of Impermanence**). This keeps the parallel structure of
  the ten definitions scannable without multiplying heading levels.
- The ten perception names appear as a single list at the opening of the
  section, mirroring the Pali's enumeration before the individual definitions.
- The Buddha's framing statement (`if you recite these ten, his illness may
  subside`) opens the teaching and is repeated verbatim at the close of the
  ten definitions before the narrative conclusion — following the Pali
  structure exactly.

## Edition Status

- This companion supports the first stable study edition of the repo's
  AN 10.60 translation surface.
- Future revisions should focus on the `asubha` rendering if the house
  develops a narrower policy for the body-review family, the `kāyasaṅkhāra`
  / `cittasaṅkhāra` compound rendering if the saṅkhāra cluster develops
  explicit guidance for the breath-context compounds, or the `upadhi`
  rendering if that term gains a lexicon entry of its own.
