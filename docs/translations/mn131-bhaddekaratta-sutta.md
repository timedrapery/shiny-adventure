# MN 131: Bhaddekaratta Sutta

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of MN 131, designed to be readable
  as a single Markdown document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [MN 131 Translation Notes](mn131-bhaddekaratta-sutta-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of MN 131 as segmented in SuttaCentral's Bilara
  data.
- House choices that most affect this sutta: `bhaddekaratta` is rendered `one
  fine night`; `nandī` is rendered `relishing`; `rūpa` is `form`; `vedanā` is
  `felt experience`; `saññā` is `recognition`; `saṅkhārā` is `putting things
  together`; `viññāṇa` is `knowing`; and `attā` is `self`.
- This edition treats MN 131 as the repository's control surface for the
  relationship between time and self-identification: the past and future
  sections turn on relishing the five parts of experience, while the present
  section turns on regarding those same five as self.
- This is the first provisional study edition of the repository's MN 131
  translation surface.

## Translation

### The Setting

So I have heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's
Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

There the Buddha addressed the bhikkhus:

"Bhikkhus."

"Yes, Bhante," they replied.

The Buddha said this:

### One Fine Night

"Bhikkhus, I will teach you the summary and explanation of 'One Fine Night.'
Listen and pay close attention. I will speak."

"Yes, Bhante," the bhikkhus replied.

The Buddha said this:

"Don't chase after the past
or long for the future.
The past has been left behind,
and the future hasn't arrived.

See clearly what is happening now,
right where it is.
Don't be pulled away or shaken.
Know this, and keep developing it.

The work must be done today.
Who knows if death will come tomorrow?
There is no bargaining
with death and its great army.

Someone who lives this way—
energetic, tireless day and night—
is what the peaceful sage calls
someone who has lived one fine night."

### Chasing After the Past

"And how do you chase after the past, bhikkhus?

You think, 'This was what my form was like in the past,' and you relish that.

You think, 'This was what my felt experience was like in the past,' and you
relish that.

You think, 'This was what my recognition was like in the past,' and you
relish that.

You think, 'This was how I put things together in the past,' and you relish
that.

You think, 'This was what my knowing was like in the past,' and you relish
that.

This is how you chase after the past.

### Not Chasing After the Past

"And how do you not chase after the past, bhikkhus?

You think, 'This was what my form was like in the past,' without relishing
that.

You think, 'This was what my felt experience was like in the past,' without
relishing that.

You think, 'This was what my recognition was like in the past,' without
relishing that.

You think, 'This was how I put things together in the past,' without
relishing that.

You think, 'This was what my knowing was like in the past,' without relishing
that.

This is how you do not chase after the past.

### Longing for the Future

"And how do you long for the future, bhikkhus?

You think, 'My form may be like this in the future,' and you relish
that.

You think, 'My felt experience may be like this in the future,' and
you relish that.

You think, 'My recognition may be like this in the future,' and you
relish that.

You think, 'I may put things together like this in the future,' and
you relish that.

You think, 'My knowing may be like this in the future,' and you
relish that.

This is how you long for the future.

### Not Longing for the Future

"And how do you not long for the future, bhikkhus?

You think, 'My form may be like this in the future,' without
relishing that.

You think, 'My felt experience may be like this in the future,'
without relishing that.

You think, 'My recognition may be like this in the future,' without
relishing that.

You think, 'I may put things together like this in the future,'
without relishing that.

You think, 'My knowing may be like this in the future,' without
relishing that.

This is how you do not long for the future.

### Being Pulled Away by the Present

"And how are you pulled away by what is happening now, bhikkhus?

Here, an unlearned ordinary person has not seen the noble ones and is
unskilled and untrained in the noble Dhamma. They have not seen true persons
and are unskilled and untrained in the true person's Dhamma.

They regard form as self, or self as having form, or form as in self, or self
as in form.

They regard felt experience as self, or self as having felt experience, or
felt experience as in self, or self as in felt experience.

They regard recognition as self, or self as having recognition, or recognition
as in self, or self as in recognition.

They regard putting things together as self, or self as having putting things
together, or putting things together as in self, or self as in putting things
together.

They regard knowing as self, or self as having knowing, or knowing as in self,
or self as in knowing.

This is how you are pulled away by what is happening now.

### Not Being Pulled Away by the Present

"And how are you not pulled away by what is happening now, bhikkhus?

Here, a learned noble disciple has seen the noble ones and is skilled and
trained in the noble Dhamma. They have seen true persons and are skilled and
trained in the true person's Dhamma.

They do not regard form as self, or self as having form, or form as in self,
or self as in form.

They do not regard felt experience as self, or self as having felt experience,
or felt experience as in self, or self as in felt experience.

They do not regard recognition as self, or self as having recognition, or
recognition as in self, or self as in recognition.

They do not regard putting things together as self, or self as having putting
things together, or putting things together as in self, or self as in putting
things together.

They do not regard knowing as self, or self as having knowing, or knowing as in
self, or self as in knowing.

This is how you are not pulled away by what is happening now.

### The Teaching Repeated

"Don't chase after the past
or long for the future.
The past has been left behind,
and the future hasn't arrived.

See clearly what is happening now,
right where it is.
Don't be pulled away or shaken.
Know this, and keep developing it.

The work must be done today.
Who knows if death will come tomorrow?
There is no bargaining
with death and its great army.

Someone who lives this way—
energetic, tireless day and night—
is what the peaceful sage calls
someone who has lived one fine night."

"Bhikkhus, when I said, 'I will teach you the summary and explanation of One
Fine Night,' this is what I was referring to."

That is what the Buddha said. The bhikkhus were glad and welcomed what the
Buddha had said.

The first discourse is finished.
