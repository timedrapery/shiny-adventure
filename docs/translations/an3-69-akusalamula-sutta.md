# AN 3.69: Akusalamūla Sutta

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [AN 3.69 Translation Notes](an3-69-akusalamula-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- `lobha`, `dosa`, and `moha` are rendered as `greed`, `aversion`, and
  `delusion`.
- Their opposites are `non-greed`, `non-aversion`, and `clarity`. Here
  `clarity` renders `amoha`, literally "non-delusion."
- The Pali says that complete quenching is reached "here and now." This is not
  a reference to an awakened person's death.

## Translation

### Three Roots of Harm

"Bhikkhus, there are three roots of what is unwholesome.

Which three?

Greed is a root of what is unwholesome. Aversion is a root of what is
unwholesome. Delusion is a root of what is unwholesome.

Greed itself is unwholesome. Whatever a greedy person does through body,
speech, or mind is also unwholesome.

Overwhelmed by greed, with the heart taken over by it, a person may unjustly
make someone else suffer. They may attack them, imprison them, take their
property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking, 'I have power, and I
want power.' All of that is unwholesome.

In this way, many harmful, unwholesome qualities arise. Greed is their source,
greed is their origin; they are born from greed, and greed produces them.

Aversion itself is unwholesome. Whatever a person acting from aversion does
through body, speech, or mind is also unwholesome.

Overwhelmed by aversion, with the heart taken over by it, a person may
unjustly make someone else suffer. They may attack them, imprison them, take
their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking, 'I have power, and
I want power.' All of that is unwholesome.

In this way, many harmful, unwholesome qualities arise. Aversion is their
source, aversion is their origin; they are born from aversion, and aversion
produces them.

Delusion itself is unwholesome. Whatever a person acting from delusion does
through body, speech, or mind is also unwholesome.

Overwhelmed by delusion, with the heart taken over by it, a person may
unjustly make someone else suffer. They may attack them, imprison them, take
their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking, 'I have power, and
I want power.' All of that is unwholesome.

In this way, many harmful, unwholesome qualities arise. Delusion is their
source, delusion is their origin; they are born from delusion, and delusion
produces them.

### What Being Taken Over Looks Like

A person like this speaks at the wrong time, falsely, harmfully, against the
Dhamma, and against the training.

Why are they described this way?

Because they unjustly make someone else suffer. They may attack them, imprison
them, take their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking, 'I have
power, and I want power.'

When told something true, they deny it and will not admit it. When told
something untrue, they make no effort to sort it out by saying, 'That is
incorrect. That is not true.'

That is why they are said to speak at the wrong time, falsely, harmfully,
against the Dhamma, and against the training.

Overwhelmed by harmful, unwholesome qualities born of greed, with the heart
taken over by them, such a person lives in suffering here and now—troubled,
distressed, and feverish. After death, a bad destination can be expected.

The same is true of qualities born of aversion and delusion.

Suppose a large forest tree were smothered and wrapped in three choking vines.
The tree would come to ruin and destruction. In the same way, a person
overwhelmed by harmful, unwholesome qualities born of greed, aversion, and
delusion lives in suffering here and now—troubled, distressed, and feverish.
After death, a bad destination can be expected.

These are the three roots of what is unwholesome.

### Three Roots of Well-Being

There are three roots of what is wholesome.

Which three?

Non-greed is a root of what is wholesome. Non-aversion is a root of what is
wholesome. Clarity is a root of what is wholesome.

Non-greed itself is wholesome. Whatever a person free of greed does through
body, speech, or mind is also wholesome.

Not overwhelmed by greed, with the heart no longer taken over by it, a person
does not unjustly make someone else suffer. They do not attack them, imprison
them, take their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking, 'I have
power, and I want power.' That is wholesome.

In this way, many wholesome qualities arise. Non-greed is their source,
non-greed is their origin; they are born from non-greed, and non-greed
produces them.

Non-aversion itself is wholesome. Whatever a person free of aversion does
through body, speech, or mind is also wholesome.

Not overwhelmed by aversion, with the heart no longer taken over by it, a
person does not unjustly make someone else suffer. They do not attack them,
imprison them, take their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking,
'I have power, and I want power.' That is wholesome.

In this way, many wholesome qualities arise. Non-aversion is their source,
non-aversion is their origin; they are born from non-aversion, and
non-aversion produces them.

Clarity itself is wholesome. Whatever a person free of delusion does through
body, speech, or mind is also wholesome.

Not overwhelmed by delusion, with the heart no longer taken over by it, a
person does not unjustly make someone else suffer. They do not attack them,
imprison them, take their property, condemn them, or drive them away, thinking,
'I have power, and I want power.' That is wholesome.

In this way, many wholesome qualities arise. Clarity is their source, clarity
is their origin; they are born from clarity, and clarity produces them.

### What Clarity Makes Possible

A person like this speaks at the right time, truthfully, beneficially, in line
with the Dhamma, and in line with the training.

Why are they described this way?

Because they do not unjustly make someone else suffer. They do not attack
them, imprison them, take their property, condemn them, or drive them away,
thinking, 'I have power, and I want power.'

When told something true, they admit it and do not deny it. When told
something untrue, they make an effort to sort it out by saying, 'That is
incorrect. That is not true.'

That is why they are said to speak at the right time, beneficially, in line
with the Dhamma, and in line with the training.

### Pulling the Roots Out Completely

For such a person, the harmful, unwholesome qualities born of greed have been
abandoned: cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, done away with, and
unable to arise in the future.

They live at ease here and now—untroubled, undistressed, and unfeverish. They
reach complete quenching here and now.

The same is true of the harmful, unwholesome qualities born of aversion and
delusion.

Suppose a large forest tree were smothered and wrapped in three choking vines.
A person comes along with a spade and basket. They cut each vine at its root,
dig around it, and pull out every root, even the smallest strand.

Then they cut the vines into pieces, split the pieces, shred them into
splinters, dry the splinters in the wind and sun, burn them, reduce them to
ashes, and scatter the ashes in a strong wind or wash them away in a swift
river.

Those vines have been cut off at the root, made like palm stumps, done away
with, and made unable to grow again.

In the same way, the harmful, unwholesome qualities born of greed, aversion,
and delusion have been abandoned: cut off at the root, made like palm stumps,
done away with, and unable to arise in the future.

Such a person lives at ease here and now—untroubled, undistressed, and
unfeverish. They reach complete quenching here and now.

These are the three roots of what is wholesome."

The ninth discourse is finished.
