# Ud 8.3: Tatiyanibbānapaṭisaṁyutta Sutta (About Nibbāna, the Third)

## Editorial Note

- Companion translator notes:
  [Ud 8.3 Translation Notes](ud8-3-tatiyanibbanapatisamyutta-sutta-notes.md)
- Primary source: the Mahāsaṅgīti Pali text in SuttaCentral's Bilara data.
- This is the repository's first Udāna surface, so it also settles the
  collection's framing formula. See the notes.
- `ajāta abhūta akata asaṅkhata` is rendered as four parallel participles —
  `unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned` — and its positive counterpart as
  the same four words without the negation. The whole discourse is an argument
  that turns on that parallel, so the two series have to match word for word.
- `asaṅkhata` and `saṅkhata` keep their governed `unconditioned` and
  `conditioned`; `nissaraṇa` keeps `escape`; `paññāyati` is `is discerned`,
  following the same conditional shape in MN 43.

## Translation

### What the Escape Depends On

So I heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove,
Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

At that time the Buddha was pointing out, urging on, rousing, and gladdening
the bhikkhus with a Dhamma talk about nibbāna. And the bhikkhus were treating
it as something that mattered, attending to it, bringing their whole heart to
bear, listening closely.

Then the Buddha, understanding what this meant, spoke this inspired saying at
that moment:

"There is, bhikkhus, what is unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned.

If there were no such thing, bhikkhus — nothing unborn, unbecome, unmade,
unconditioned — then no escape would be discerned here from what is born,
become, made, conditioned.

But because there is what is unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned, an
escape is discerned from what is born, become, made, conditioned."

The third discourse is finished.
