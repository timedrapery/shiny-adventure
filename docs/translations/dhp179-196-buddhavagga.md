# Dhp 179-196: Buddhavagga (The Buddha)

## Editorial Note

- This is a repo-native study translation of the fourteenth chapter of the
  Dhammapada, verses 179 to 196, designed to be readable as a single Markdown
  document.
- Companion translator notes:
  [Dhp 179-196 Translation Notes](dhp179-196-buddhavagga-notes.md)
- Source basis: the Pali text of Dhp 179-196 as segmented in SuttaCentral's
  Bilara data. The verse titles from the later commentary are not translated.
- House choices that most affect this chapter: `taṇhā` is `ignorant
  wanting`; `khanti` is `patience`; `pāṭimokkha` is left untranslated;
  `adhicitta` is `the higher mind`; `saraṇa` is `refuge`; `kāma` is
  `sensuality`; `papañca` is `proliferation`; `nibbuta` is `cooled`; and
  `jhāna` is `mental theme`, as in Dhp 21-32.
- Each verse carries its Dhammapada number.
- This is the first stable study edition of the repository's Dhp 179-196
  translation surface.

## Translation

### Verses

**179** Whose victory cannot be undone,
whose victory no one in the world can follow:
that Buddha, whose range has no end,
who leaves no track, by what track will you lead him?

**180** In whom there is no ignorant wanting,
that net, that entanglement, to lead him anywhere:
that Buddha, whose range has no end,
who leaves no track, by what track will you lead him?

**181** The steadfast, intent on the mental themes,
delighting in the calm of renunciation,
fully awakened, remembering:
even the devas envy them.

**182** Hard it is to be born human,
hard the life of mortals,
hard to hear the true Dhamma,
hard the arising of Buddhas.

**183** Not doing any evil,
taking up the wholesome,
purifying your own mind:
this is the teaching of the Buddhas.

**184** Patience, enduring patience, is the highest austerity;
nibbāna is the highest, the Buddhas say.
Someone who harms another has not gone forth;
someone who troubles another is no samaṇa.

**185** Not blaming, not harming,
restraint in the pāṭimokkha,
moderation in eating,
a secluded place to sleep and sit,
and devotion to the higher mind:
this is the teaching of the Buddhas.

**186** Not with a shower of coins
is there contentment in sensuality.
Sensuality gives little gratification and much pain:
knowing this, the wise

**187** find no delight
even in heavenly sensuality.
The disciple of the fully awakened Buddha
delights in the ending of ignorant wanting.

**188** People driven by fear
go for refuge to many things:
to mountains and forests,
to parks, trees, and shrines.

**189** That is not a secure refuge,
that is not the highest refuge.
Going to that refuge,
no one is released from all dissatisfaction.

**190** But whoever has gone for refuge
to the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha
sees with right discernment
the four noble truths:

**191** dissatisfaction, the arising of dissatisfaction,
the going beyond dissatisfaction,
and the noble eightfold path
that leads to the calming of dissatisfaction.

**192** That is the secure refuge,
that is the highest refuge.
Going to that refuge,
you are released from all dissatisfaction.

**193** A thoroughbred among people is hard to find,
and is not born just anywhere.
Wherever that steadfast person is born,
that family prospers in happiness.

**194** Happy is the arising of Buddhas,
happy the teaching of the true Dhamma,
happy the harmony of the Saṅgha,
happy the austerity of those in harmony.

**195** Whoever honors those worthy of honor,
whether Buddhas or their disciples,
who have gone beyond proliferation
and crossed over sorrow and lamentation,

**196** whoever honors such ones,
cooled and fearing nothing from any side:
no one can measure their benefit
by saying it is this much.

The fourteenth chapter, on the Buddha, is finished.
