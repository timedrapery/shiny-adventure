# Dhammarato Talk Transcripts Profile

## Purpose

This note records a source-backed usage profile drawn from the Dhammarato talk
transcripts, a 48-part collection of machine transcriptions of recorded
teaching sessions, one-on-one calls, and group meetings.

The corpus covers 2,063 distinct talks from 2014-11-20 to 2026-10-17, or about
13.8 million words from Dhammarato's side of the conversation. It is the
largest record of how Dhammarato actually uses Pali and English in live
teaching.

## Editorial Status

The transcripts sit at the `dhammarato` priority in
[`osf-editorial-authority.md`](osf-editorial-authority.md): below OSF house
materials such as the OSF glossary and the Dhammarato quotes book, and above
Buddhadasa-line and outside profiles.

On their own the transcripts justify alternates, context rules, and notes. They
changed two house defaults only by explicit editorial decision on 2026-11-08,
recorded below. Any further default change based on this profile needs the same
kind of explicit decision.

Like the quotes profile, this profile concerns lexical choices and explanatory
framing. It does not make spoken teaching a sentence-style model for
translation prose, which still follows
[`PLAIN_ENGLISH_STANDARD.md`](PLAIN_ENGLISH_STANDARD.md).

## Method And Caveats

- Each talk was split into speaker turns, and the speaker with the most words
  in a talk was taken to be Dhammarato. In a few talks with long student
  turns this picks up student speech, which inflates some counts (for example
  craving in 2022).
- Counts were compared across ten periods of roughly 1.4 to 1.5 million words
  each, so changes over time are visible. The last period (parts 46–48) is
  about half that size.
- In later talks Dhammarato often reads published translations aloud. Those
  readings reintroduce words he rejects in his own voice, such as rapture,
  suffering, and singleness of mind. Only his own explanations count as
  evidence for a rendering.
- The transcripts are automatic speech recognition. Pali is frequently
  garbled; see the normalization ledger below.

## Default Changes By Editorial Decision

- `samadhi` -> `unification of mind`, with `unification` as the compact form
  in compounds and `composure` kept as a continuity alternate. He uses
  unification of mind about 600 times and unified mind about 200 times, always
  as gathering together and integration, never as concentration.
- `samma-samadhi` -> `right unification of mind`, with
  `right noble unification of mind` for his Samma Arya Samadhi heading form.
- `cetaso-ekodibhava` moved to `singleness of mind` so the second-jhāna formula
  does not read unification of mind born of unification of mind.
- `nivarana` -> `hindrance`, and `panca-nivarana` -> `five hindrances`, with
  `distraction` kept as the primary alternate. He says hindrance about 7,500
  times and distraction about 250 times, and the five hindrances 109 times
  against no uses of the five distractions.

- The five named hindrances follow his names, with the earlier
  distraction-family names kept as alternates: `kamacchanda` ->
  `sensual desire`, `byapada` -> `ill will`, `thina-middha` ->
  `sloth and torpor`, `uddhacca-kukkucca` -> `restlessness and worry`, and
  `vicikiccha` -> `doubt`. He never uses the distraction-family names. For
  uddhacca-kukkucca he says restlessness and worry about three times as often
  as restlessness and remorse, which stays as the alternate that follows the
  members' own defaults.

All 66 published translations, their notes, and the reader were re-rendered
to this wording on 2026-11-08.

## Confirmed House Defaults

The transcripts directly support these existing defaults:

- `dukkha` -> `dissatisfaction`: "The word Dukkha doesn't really mean
  suffering… The Dukkha is that you feel dissatisfied."
- `sati` -> `remembering`: "Sati, which is remembering, to keep coming back."
- `tanha` -> `ignorant wanting`: "It's the ignorant wanting that is Dukkha,"
  set against wise wanting.
- `sukha` -> `satisfaction`, `bodhi` -> `awakening`,
  `samma-sankappa` -> `right attitude` ("Samma Sanghappa is attitude").
- `upekkha` -> `dynamic balance`: "Upekkha does not mean equanimity"; the
  balance of a teeter-totter, sea legs, being able to handle anything.
- `piti` with `rapture` discouraged: "absolutely the wrong word"; pīti as
  success, the win, the wow factor.
- `anicca` with `temporary` discouraged: "rather than calling it temporary,
  you would call it process."

## Recorded Alternates And Notes

- `tathagata`: `Thus-Gone One` moved to discouraged ("I could not come up with
  a worse definition than that"); one who has come to the here-now is an
  explanatory gloss.
- `tathata`: `thisness` and `just as it is` added as explanatory alternates.
- `samma-ditthi` and `samma-vayama`: `right noble view` and
  `right noble effort` added as heading forms.
- `bojjhanga`: a path quality becomes an awakening factor when it becomes
  unremitting.
- `nibbana`: little nibbāna as a controlled explanatory phrase.
- `kammannacitta`: `mind fit for work`; `kalyanamitta`: `noble friend` and
  `spiritual friend`.
- Explanatory notes on `anatta` (no-soul polemic, not-self rendering),
  `anusaya` (the four-instincts map, explanatory only), `vedana` (like,
  dislike, don't know), `sanna` (labeling and storytelling), and `sankhara`
  (old saṅkhāra as old habits).

## Not To Encode

These are spoken slips or idiosyncratic readings, not policy:

- reversing taṇhā and upādāna in the dependent-arising order in early talks
  (later talks give the canonical order: I want it is taṇhā, I've got to have
  it is clinging)
- swapping dosa and moha in one 2016 talk
- equating atammayatā with step 16 of ānāpānasati
- treating pīti and paṭicca as the same word in two 2021–22 talks
- the four-instincts map as a translation of anusaya

## Normalization Ledger

Common speech-recognition spellings in the transcripts:

- `Srotapanna`, `Sotagami` -> `sotapanna`, `sakadagami`
- `Samskara`, `Sankaras` -> `sankhara`
- `Sukkha` -> `sukha`; `Tangha`, `Tanya` -> `tanha`
- `Loba`, `Moba`, `Hadosa` -> `lobha`, `moha`, `dosa`
- `Idya`, `Idyapada`, `Idiapada` -> `iddhi`, `iddhipada`
- `Idyapapajayata` -> `idappaccayata`
- `Atamayata`, `Atthamayata` -> `atammayata`
- `Tatata`, `Tathatha` -> `tathata`
- `Sanghappa`, `Sankapa` -> `sankappa`
- `Silabhata Paramasa`, `Sila Bhavana Paramasa` -> `silabbata-paramasa`
- `Salyatana`, `Saliyatana`, `Saliattana` -> `salayatana`
- `Sambojjhana`, `Sambhojana` -> `sambojjhanga`
- `Kalyametta`, `Kaliyametta` -> `kalyanamitta`
- `Trilokana` -> `tilakkhana`; `Upajaya` -> `upajjhaya`
- `Lokottara`, `Lokatara` -> `lokuttara`; `Preta` -> `peta`
- `Nupassana`, `Nipassana` -> `anupassana`
- `Anapanosati` -> `anapanasati`

Proper names also appear garbled, such as Wat Soan Mok or Mokkh (Suan Mokkh)
and Deepa Bhawan (Dipabhavan). Thai merit-making words such as saibat, takbat,
and tham bun are not Pali and are out of scope.

## Candidate Headwords

These appear in the transcripts but have no live record yet. They should enter
through the [candidate workflow](candidate-term-workflow.md), not directly into
`terms/`:

- `visuddhi`: the seven purifications, with MN 24's relay image
- `patikula-sanna` and `appatikula-sanna`: perceiving the repulsive and the
  unrepulsive, tied to his mettā teaching
- `dhammakaya`: the full body of the Dhamma
- `uccheda-ditthi`: annihilationism
- `dhutanga` and `sanghati`: monastic terms
